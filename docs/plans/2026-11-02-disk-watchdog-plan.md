# jawd: the disk watchdog

**The finding:** P11 of the data review, with what's left of D7. Disk space isn't watched: a history sync can need several times jaw.db's size in WAL, and media downloads and the spool share the disk, so `SQLITE_FULL` rolls a transaction back and then nothing can commit, the inbox included. Main already does D7's first half: an inbox commit that fails is kept and tried again from 10 ms doubling to 1 s, a full disk says "jaw's disk is full…" in an error `notice` and in `status`'s `storage.trouble`, and media checks the free space before each download (512 MB beside a file nobody asked for, 64 MB beside one asked for). What's left is D7's "pause background lanes and downloads" and P11's watchdog and ballast.

## What changes

- **`disk.rs`, the watchdog.** `Disk` reads the free space and size of the disk jaw's folder is on with `statvfs`, as media and the upgrade's check do, each time it's asked (`room`); a read costs about 1.4 µs here. While the free space can't be read, `room` answers as the disk was when it last could be, which is room if it never could, so `status` never says work waits while it goes. Below the floor, the background work and downloads below wait, and they go on once the disk has the floor and 256 MB more, so they don't stop and start at its edge. The floor is 2 GB, or 5% of the disk where that's less (1 GB of a 20 GB disk). A store opened without one (`Store::open`, every test but these, and `jawd`'s commands other than serving) has a disk that's never short of room and keeps no ballast; `jawd` serving watches its folder's.
- **The watchdog's own look** makes the ballast again and tells interfaces when something else fills the disk while jaw is quiet; background work, indexing, downloads, history blobs and the media scheduler's pass after each commit read the disk themselves. It comes every 10 s while the disk is short of room, the ballast isn't kept or the free space can't be read, and at once when the ballast is given up; otherwise no later than the disk could come down to the floor and margin if filled at 2 GB a second, and within 10 minutes (190 s with 384 GB free). Looking every 10 s whatever the disk's room was 18 of an idle jawd's 53.5 wake-ups a minute (the idle bisect, 5 November), against the design's idle budget, "no wake-ups, no polling". A fill at up to 2 GB a second is still told below the floor within 10 s of it, as it was; a faster one at the next look, or at the next commit's scheduler pass, whichever comes first, while background work waits at once, since it reads the disk itself, and a commit a full disk refuses is kept and tried again in the ballast's room, as below. `jawd status` says the disk as it was last read, which on a jawd with no commits, far above the floor, may be that look, minutes ago.
- **A network folder (the audit's O7):** the disk is still read each time it's asked, on the writer's thread before background transactions and indexing steps as on tokio's workers, so a server that stopped answering would hold them, as the audit found. Reading it on the watchdog's task every `POLL` instead, O7's second fix, would be 240 looks a minute for as long as jawd runs, against the idle budget, so jaw keeps to the design's answer, refusing a network or synced folder ("One process"), which main doesn't do yet and is left to a change of its own. The watchdog's longer wait adds no read anywhere.
- **The writer** waits for room before each background transaction, as it waits for the WAL's (`wait_for_disk`, beside `wait_for_room`), taking inbox rows and live writes meanwhile, so the watchdog never holds a live write up. The rows left in the inbox at a start (an unclean stop's, and those kept in quarantine) are converted as background work, so they never hold up live writes, but they never wait for room: the converter reads the live rows behind them in the same loop, which would wait with them. They come to the writer on a queue of their own (`Store::apply_leftovers`), taken after live writes, ahead of other background work, and while it waits for the disk, so they don't wait behind it either; their link previews' thumbnails go the same way. Indexing for search is background work too: a step waits while the disk is short of room. Every background producer (history, its blobs' conversion, the backfills, media's rows, avatars, merges) waits at its next transaction, so what it holds back is bounded by what it does before one.
- **Erasures never wait for the disk** (O17 of both audits, with disappearing on main): a disappearing or view-once message that goes is gone from the files however short of room the disk is, as the design's "Erased when asked" says, and an erasure frees room besides. The timer's erasures come to the writer on the leftovers' queue, so they're taken ahead of other background work and while it waits for the disk, in its short transactions that still wait for the WAL's room, end after each erasure and commit at NORMAL; the `once` backfill and the other backfills still wait. An erasure in the live lane (a deletion for you, a revoke, a view-once message closed) never waited, but what it leaves to do would have: so the writer's idle step merges a contentless index whole while that's due or under way, and empties the WAL an erasure left once that's due, before it looks at the disk, and only indexing and the merges no erasure left wait for room; and the writer takes its idle steps while background work waits for the disk too. Its wait for the next step never ends before a step waiting for the disk, or to be tried again, may go, with nothing left to index as with a backlog, so a disk short of room never has it wake every millisecond. A step of theirs that a full disk refuses is tried again later, as any idle step is, giving up no ballast itself, unlike the writer's other checkpoints (the ballast, below): on a full disk the ballast goes with the writer's next commit, or the checkpointer's next pass or idle emptying, and the step tried again then has its room.
- **Downloads.** Media's scheduler starts no download while the disk is short of room, one an interface asked for included: it stays asked and goes once there's room, when the scheduler is woken. `media.paused` says why. The history fetcher waits for room before it asks the engine for a blob. Avatars, one small picture at a time, wait at their next transaction.
- **Interfaces are told** with a `notice`, as the protocol has room for: `warning` as the disk goes below the floor ("jaw's disk has 1.8 GB free, under the 2.0 GB it keeps…, so history, downloads and other background work wait until there's room; messages still arrive and send"), and `info` once it has room again. Main's error notice for a write a full disk refused stays as it is.
- **The ballast.** jawd keeps a 64 MB file, `ballast`, in jaw's folder: reserved with `F_PREALLOCATE` on macOS and `posix_fallocate` on Linux, or written as zeros where neither works, under `ballast.partial` and renamed once it's whole. When a write to jaw.db fails for a full disk (the writer's `failing`, which every commit it tries again goes through, and the checkpointer's passes), jawd deletes it, so the next try of the commit, the checkpoint or the rollback has room. The writer's own checkpoints give it up too: its restart of the WAL past WAL_HARD, which runs after inbox groups and live writes as well as before background work, and its copy of the last few frames as background work or indexing waits for the WAL; its TRUNCATE at a clean stop goes through `failing` with the writer's other retries. So the ballast goes wherever SQLite checkpoints jaw.db while jawd runs: nothing else does, as only the writer commits, with `wal_autocheckpoint` off. The watchdog makes it again, looking at once when it's given up and every 10 s until it's kept, once the disk is above the floor with room for it beside the floor; a ballast of another size is made again, and a partial one is removed.
- **`jawd status`** gets `disk`: `free`, `size`, the `floor`, `paused` while background work and downloads wait, the `ballast` bytes kept (0 once given up, until it's made again), how often it was `ballast_given_up`, and the background transactions' `waits` for room and `waited_ms`; `jaw status` and `jawd status` print it. The protocol's `status` row says so.
- **`scripts/check-data-audit.py`** lists `crates/jawd/src/disk.rs` as the data layer.
- **Left alone:** the free space read is `statvfs`'s, which leaves out what macOS can purge; and on APFS a local snapshot taken while the ballast was there holds its blocks until macOS purges it, so the ballast's room comes at once only where no snapshot holds it.

## The tests

All with a fake free-space source; the real disk is never filled. A test hook in jaw's VFS (`noted::fill`) has writes to jaw.db's files fail with `SQLITE_FULL` while the fake disk has nothing free, so a full disk is SQLite's own failure.

- `disk.rs`: the floor (2 GB, or 5% where that's less); `room` going below the floor and back, at the floor and 256 MB more, saying why while it's short, and what waits for room going on once there's room; the ballast made at its size, holding its room, under its partial name and renamed, given up once, not made while the disk is short of room nor before it has the margin, made again once it has; a partial one removed and one of another size made again; and the notices, a warning as the disk goes below the floor and an info one as its room comes back, once each; and while the free space can't be read, what waits for room waiting if the disk was short of room when last read, as `status` says, and going if it had room or was never read. The watchdog's wait: as long as a fill at 2 GB a second takes to bring the disk down to the floor and margin (a margin of 10 GB, so leaving it out shows), within 10 minutes; every 10 s near the floor and margin, under the margin, below the floor, past it while short of room, while the ballast can't be made or has been given up with no room for it beside the floor, and while the free space can't be read, never read or read before. In paused time, with the watchdog itself running: idle far above the floor it reads the disk once a wait, 7 times in 650 s where every 10 s is 66; a disk filling at 2 GB a second, and at a quarter of that, is told below the floor within 10 s of going below it; a ballast given up while the next look is 100 s off has the watchdog look at once, and it's made again within 10 s of the disk having room; and with the next look far off, what waits for room waits at once as the disk goes below the floor, interfaces are told then, and it goes on once there's room.
- `store`: below the floor a background transaction waits, and indexing for search, while inbox rows and live writes go; above it they go on; `status` says so and counts the wait. On a full disk an inbox row's commit fails, the ballast is given up, and the row is committed in its room; the ballast isn't made again while the disk is short of room, and is once it has room. A checkpoint a full disk refuses gives up the ballast, and the WAL is emptied in its room: the idle TRUNCATE's, and a pass of the checkpointer's, once WAL_EASY frames wait with jaw.db alone full, no commit refused, and the next pass copies the WAL in the ballast's room. So do the writer's own: its restart of the WAL past its bound after a live write, the next starting it again; and, the checkpointer kept asleep, its copy of the last few frames as indexing waits for the WAL, the next copying them. The rows left in the inbox go in short transactions: half BATCH_ROWS of their changes, which only BATCH_TIME and the early end for a waiting live write can split, as in a converter's batch, take more than one, with a live write waiting and with nothing waiting, and so do twice BATCH_ROWS; a live write waiting as the first starts goes in between them, and so does one sent once it's answered; and while BATCH_ROWS of their changes go in, at least ten inbox rows handed over one at a time are acked. `jawd status` counts those waiting for the writer among its background work. Below the floor, with the timer running, a message with a link and a captioned photo among thirty of each are erased at their time, their words out of every index's segments once links' and the library's are merged whole and the WAL emptied, and so is a disappearing message deleted for you in the live lane, while a history message applied before them waits until the disk has room; idle below the floor, the writer takes a step no more often than the watchdog looks at the disk again; and with nothing left to index, a full jaw.db refusing the WAL's emptying has it wait to try again rather than take a step each millisecond, and the WAL is emptied once jaw.db has room, the disk still short of it. And below the floor in a writer with nothing left to index or merge, as a running jawd's usually is rather than a store just opened, a history message waits for room and goes in once the disk has it, though nothing else comes to wake the writer; a live message's text waits to be indexed; and the WAL a disappearing message deleted for you leaves is emptied while that text still waits.
- `media`: below the floor, a photo that downloads by itself and one an interface asks for aren't downloaded, and `media.paused` says why; above it both are, without asking again.
- `sync`: below the floor, a history notification's blob isn't asked for; above it, it is and is stored. Below the floor, with history already waiting for room in the writer, a row left in the inbox from before jawd started and a live row after it are converted, and the left row's thumbnail is kept, while history still waits. With room, while four tasks keep background work coming, a batch of rows left in the inbox and a live row after them are converted while it still comes.
- `jaw-client`: `describe` says the disk's line, and `paused` while background work waits, once.
- `jawd` serving: started on a temp folder as it serves, its `status` says the free space and size of that folder's disk, as statfs, a second reader, says them within a busy disk's churn, and its floor, and once the watchdog's first look has run, a 64 MB ballast in jaw's folder that holds its blocks; started on a faked disk (`start_watching`), an interface connected to it hears the `warning` notice as the disk goes below the floor and the `info` one once it has room.

## Each rule's switch

Each switched off in turn and its test shown failing, the results beside the report: no wait before a background transaction; a wait that doesn't take inbox rows and live writes; indexing not waiting; the scheduler starting downloads below the floor; it not looking again while it waits; the fetcher not waiting; no notice either way; resuming at the floor rather than past it; the floor not the lesser of the two; the ballast not given up by the writer, nor by the checkpointer; made while the disk is short of room, or never made again; a partial one left, or one of another size kept; one that doesn't hold its room; `media.paused` not saying the disk's reason; and `describe` not saying the disk's line. From the audit's first round: the rows left in the inbox waiting for the disk as other background work does (the change's first rule), waiting for it themselves on their own queue, or not taken while background work waits for it; and their thumbnails waiting for it. jawd serving opening a store that isn't watched, its watchdog never started, no notices task, `status` without the disk, and the real reader taking the disk's size for its free space. The checkpointer's passes not giving up the ballast, and its idle TRUNCATE not giving it up, each on its own. A free space that can't be read counting as room. The writer's restart of the WAL not giving up the ballast, nor its copy of the last few frames, each on its own. From its second round: the rows left in the inbox taken after other background work rather than ahead of it; their transactions unpaced, each batch of them in one; the converter waiting for room before it hands them to the writer; and their queue left out of `status`'s count of the writer's background work. From its third round: their transactions ended only at BATCH_ROWS, and no inbox rows or live writes taken between them. From its fourth round: inbox rows and live writes taken only after the first of a batch's transactions; live writes but not inbox rows taken between them; and their transactions with no BATCH_TIME. From O17: the timer's erasures back in the background lane; the disk looked at before the whole merges and the WAL's emptying in the idle step; no idle steps while background work waits for the disk; and the wait for the next idle step, with nothing left to index, ignoring a step's wait for the disk or to be tried again. From its fifth round: indexing held below the floor only while something is left to merge; indexing let through below the floor once the WAL's emptying is due; and the wait for the disk not looking at it again each disk::POLL. From the idle fix: the watchdog looking every 10 s whatever the disk's room, as before, or every 10 minutes; its loop sleeping 10 s whatever the look says; the margin left out of the wait; no cap; no 10 s at least near the floor; the last reading taken while the free space can't be read; the ballast's state ignored; no look at once when the ballast is given up; and `room` answering as the last look found the disk rather than reading it.
