# jawd: the disk watchdog

**The finding:** P11 of the data review, with what's left of D7. Disk space isn't watched: a history sync can need several times jaw.db's size in WAL, and media downloads and the spool share the disk, so `SQLITE_FULL` rolls a transaction back and then nothing can commit, the inbox included. Main already does D7's first half: an inbox commit that fails is kept and tried again from 10 ms doubling to 1 s, a full disk says "jaw's disk is full…" in an error `notice` and in `status`'s `storage.trouble`, and media checks the free space before each download (512 MB beside a file nobody asked for, 64 MB beside one asked for). What's left is D7's "pause background lanes and downloads" and P11's watchdog and ballast.

## What changes

- **`disk.rs`, the watchdog.** `Disk` reads the free space and size of the disk jaw's folder is on with `statvfs`, as media and the upgrade's check do, each time it's asked (`room`); a read costs about 1.4 µs here. While the free space can't be read, `room` answers as the disk was when it last could be, which is room if it never could, so `status` never says work waits while it goes. Below the floor, the background work and downloads below wait, and they go on once the disk has the floor and 256 MB more, so they don't stop and start at its edge. The floor is 2 GB, or 5% of the disk where that's less (1 GB of a 20 GB disk). A store opened without one (`Store::open`, every test but these, and `jawd`'s commands other than serving) has a disk that's never short of room and keeps no ballast; `jawd` serving watches its folder's.
- **The writer** waits for room before each background transaction, as it waits for the WAL's (`wait_for_disk`, beside `wait_for_room`), taking inbox rows and live writes meanwhile, so the watchdog never holds a live write up. The rows left in the inbox at a start (an unclean stop's, and those kept in quarantine) are converted as background work, so they never hold up live writes, but they never wait for room: the converter reads the live rows behind them in the same loop, which would wait with them. They come to the writer on a queue of their own (`Store::apply_leftovers`), taken after live writes, ahead of other background work, and while it waits for the disk, so they don't wait behind it either; their link previews' thumbnails go the same way. Indexing for search is background work too: a step waits while the disk is short of room. Every background producer (history, its blobs' conversion, the backfills, media's rows, avatars, merges) waits at its next transaction, so what it holds back is bounded by what it does before one.
- **Downloads.** Media's scheduler starts no download while the disk is short of room, one an interface asked for included: it stays asked and goes once there's room, when the scheduler is woken. `media.paused` says why. The history fetcher waits for room before it asks the engine for a blob. Avatars, one small picture at a time, wait at their next transaction.
- **Interfaces are told** with a `notice`, as the protocol has room for: `warning` as the disk goes below the floor ("jaw's disk has 1.8 GB free, under the 2.0 GB it keeps…, so history, downloads and other background work wait until there's room; messages still arrive and send"), and `info` once it has room again. Main's error notice for a write a full disk refused stays as it is.
- **The ballast.** jawd keeps a 64 MB file, `ballast`, in jaw's folder: reserved with `F_PREALLOCATE` on macOS and `posix_fallocate` on Linux, or written as zeros where neither works, under `ballast.partial` and renamed once it's whole. When a write to jaw.db fails for a full disk (the writer's `failing`, which every commit it tries again goes through, and the checkpointer's passes), jawd deletes it, so the next try of the commit, the checkpoint or the rollback has room. The writer's own checkpoints give it up too: its restart of the WAL past WAL_HARD, which runs after inbox groups and live writes as well as before background work, and its copy of the last few frames as background work or indexing waits for the WAL; its TRUNCATE at a clean stop goes through `failing` with the writer's other retries. So the ballast goes wherever SQLite checkpoints jaw.db while jawd runs: nothing else does, as only the writer commits, with `wal_autocheckpoint` off. The watchdog makes it again, every 10 s, once the disk is above the floor with room for it beside the floor; a ballast of another size is made again, and a partial one is removed.
- **`jawd status`** gets `disk`: `free`, `size`, the `floor`, `paused` while background work and downloads wait, the `ballast` bytes kept (0 once given up, until it's made again), how often it was `ballast_given_up`, and the background transactions' `waits` for room and `waited_ms`; `jaw status` and `jawd status` print it. The protocol's `status` row says so.
- **`scripts/check-data-audit.py`** lists `crates/jawd/src/disk.rs` as the data layer.
- **Left alone:** the free space read is `statvfs`'s, which leaves out what macOS can purge; and on APFS a local snapshot taken while the ballast was there holds its blocks until macOS purges it, so the ballast's room comes at once only where no snapshot holds it.

## The tests

All with a fake free-space source; the real disk is never filled. A test hook in jaw's VFS (`noted::fill`) has writes to jaw.db's files fail with `SQLITE_FULL` while the fake disk has nothing free, so a full disk is SQLite's own failure.

- `disk.rs`: the floor (2 GB, or 5% where that's less); `room` going below the floor and back, at the floor and 256 MB more, saying why while it's short, and what waits for room going on once there's room; the ballast made at its size, holding its room, under its partial name and renamed, given up once, not made while the disk is short of room nor before it has the margin, made again once it has; a partial one removed and one of another size made again; and the notices, a warning as the disk goes below the floor and an info one as its room comes back, once each; and while the free space can't be read, what waits for room waiting if the disk was short of room when last read, as `status` says, and going if it had room or was never read.
- `store`: below the floor a background transaction waits, and indexing for search, while inbox rows and live writes go; above it they go on; `status` says so and counts the wait. On a full disk an inbox row's commit fails, the ballast is given up, and the row is committed in its room; the ballast isn't made again while the disk is short of room, and is once it has room. A checkpoint a full disk refuses gives up the ballast, and the WAL is emptied in its room: the idle TRUNCATE's, and a pass of the checkpointer's, once WAL_EASY frames wait with jaw.db alone full, no commit refused, and the next pass copies the WAL in the ballast's room. So do the writer's own: its restart of the WAL past its bound after a live write, the next starting it again; and, the checkpointer kept asleep, its copy of the last few frames as indexing waits for the WAL, the next copying them. The rows left in the inbox go in short transactions: more than BATCH_ROWS of their changes take more than one, and a live write waiting as the first starts goes in between them.
- `media`: below the floor, a photo that downloads by itself and one an interface asks for aren't downloaded, and `media.paused` says why; above it both are, without asking again.
- `sync`: below the floor, a history notification's blob isn't asked for; above it, it is and is stored. Below the floor, with history already waiting for room in the writer, a row left in the inbox from before jawd started and a live row after it are converted, the live one within 2 s of arriving, and the left row's thumbnail is kept, while history still waits. With room, while four tasks keep background work coming, a batch of rows left in the inbox and a live row after them are converted while it still comes.
- `jaw-client`: `describe` says the disk's line, and `paused` while background work waits, once.
- `jawd` serving: started on a temp folder as it serves, its `status` says the free space and size of that folder's disk, as statfs, a second reader, says them within a busy disk's churn, and its floor, and once the watchdog's first look has run, a 64 MB ballast in jaw's folder that holds its blocks; started on a faked disk (`start_watching`), an interface connected to it hears the `warning` notice as the disk goes below the floor and the `info` one once it has room.

## Each rule's switch

Each switched off in turn and its test shown failing, the results beside the report: no wait before a background transaction; a wait that doesn't take inbox rows and live writes; indexing not waiting; the scheduler starting downloads below the floor; it not looking again while it waits; the fetcher not waiting; no notice either way; resuming at the floor rather than past it; the floor not the lesser of the two; the ballast not given up by the writer, nor by the checkpointer; made while the disk is short of room, or never made again; a partial one left, or one of another size kept; one that doesn't hold its room; `media.paused` not saying the disk's reason; and `describe` not saying the disk's line. From the audit's first round: the rows left in the inbox waiting for the disk as other background work does (the change's first rule), waiting for it themselves on their own queue, or not taken while background work waits for it; and their thumbnails waiting for it. jawd serving opening a store that isn't watched, its watchdog never started, no notices task, `status` without the disk, and the real reader taking the disk's size for its free space. The checkpointer's passes not giving up the ballast, and its idle TRUNCATE not giving it up, each on its own. A free space that can't be read counting as room. The writer's restart of the WAL not giving up the ballast, nor its copy of the last few frames, each on its own. From its second round: the rows left in the inbox taken after other background work rather than ahead of it; and their transactions unpaced, each batch of them in one.
