# Audit: unlink

Range: fff9e612..09de2ec0 in unlink (`~/src/jaw-unlink`), 7 November 2026. Auditor: database-expert.

Five commits: `1e31b7c9` the engine and its contract, `2f94ec88` jawd (the demo engine's logout and relink, sync's refresh after a link), `f61b7c1f` the terminal, `05ec1ca1` the Mac, `09de2ec0` the docs. No schema change and no migration.

## In short

Changes needed: one inconsistency and two missing tests.

- **F1, inconsistency.** After `jaw unlink`, nothing stops another number linking into the same jaw.db. Its rows then mix with the first account's, and what the first account left waiting is sent from the second. Measured.
- **T1, test gap.** Nothing tests that a Connected racing the clearing of a half-made device says nothing. Both guards' mutants pass every test.
- **T2, test gap.** Nothing tests the ten-minute cap on a link that is connected but unfinished. With the cap removed, every test passes.
- **What holds:**
  - Logging out deletes only the device and forgets the account. The messages, media, folders, drafts and settings stay.
  - A logout WhatsApp can't be told of changes nothing.
  - A crash at any point of a logout leaves a state the next start reads as not linked.
  - A failed link's device is cleared only once the link has failed, but for the rare cases in O5. Nothing of jaw.db is written for it.
  - The owner's linked account takes its old paths, except that an interface's `link.cancel` no longer disconnects it.
  - A trial rebase on today's main is clean.

## Findings

### Must be fixed before landing

**F1. Inconsistency: another number linked after a logout mixes into jaw.db, and what the first account left waiting goes from the second.**
- **Where:**
  - `sync::logged_out` (`crates/jawd/src/sync/mod.rs:254`) empties `sync_state.account` and keeps everything else, `me` (`:201`) included.
  - `linkStart` (`engine/internal/wa/engine.go`) links whichever number scans the code. Nothing compares that number with the account whose messages jaw.db keeps.
  - The outbox (`store/schema.rs:571`) carries no account. App-state patches are outbox rows too (`:1517`). A row waits "for as long as WhatsApp can't be reached" (`send/outbox.rs:3`).
- **Measured, with a probe in the send tests.** It logs out as `sock.rs`'s `logout` does (the engine's Logout, then `logged_out` and its push), then sends another number's Done and Connected.
  - A message written while logged out waited, unsent.
  - Once 351919999999 linked, that message went from it.
  - Ana's chat then held three messages marked `mine`: one sent by each account, plus the one left waiting.
  - `sync_state.me` and `account` became the second number's.
- **What else follows:**
  - Chats the two accounts share merge by JID, and both accounts' messages count as yours.
  - Read positions, drafts and folders carry over to the second account.
  - Rows converted before the second account's Connected commits use the first account's JIDs (first-run-me's H3).
- **Why it's an inconsistency, not data loss:** nothing of the first account is deleted. But jaw.db can't then tell which account a row came for, so the choice the design promises ("keeps the messages until you choose") can't be made afterwards. And jaw's own queued sends act on another account.
- **Why it blocks now:**
  - Main already allows this after a logout from the phone. first-run-me rated the JID part hygiene, since a second account in one folder wasn't part of the owner's first run.
  - This branch makes it the obvious path: `jaw unlink`, then `jaw link` with the other phone. Its prompts say jawd keeps your messages.
- **What must be true before it lands:** a jaw.db that keeps one account's messages links only that account, until the choice exists.
  - Another number is refused before anything of it is kept, saying which number the folder holds. Another folder (`JAW_HOME`) can hold the other account.
  - A folder never linked takes any number.
- **One way to do it:**
  - jawd names the kept account's number (from `me`) in `LinkStart`.
  - The engine refuses any other number in whatsmeow's `PrePairCallback` (`pair.go:204`). It runs before the device gets its ID or is saved, so session.db is left with nothing and the phone shows the link failed.
- **Tests:** another number refused, with no device left and nothing in jaw.db; the same number linked again as now; a fresh folder taking any number.

**T1. Test gap: a Connected racing the clearing of a half-made device.**
- **The guards:**
  - `finished` refuses a retired client (`engine/internal/wa/pairing.go:109`).
  - `abandon` retires the client under the pairing's lock (`:163`).
  - Together they make a Connected that the client was dispatching as its device was cleared say nothing.
- **What breaks without them:**
  - Without the first, the engine says online for an account that isn't linked.
  - While `renew` is still deleting, it names the cleared device's account. jawd then stores that account and its JIDs for a device session.db no longer has.
  - Without the second, the same Connected names the account outright. That is 7 November's "already linked" in a race, for example a device removed right at its first login.
- **Mutants:** `finished` returning true for a retired client, and `abandon` retiring after the unlock. Each passes every test.
- **Fix:**
  - Add a test that clears a half-made device through a refusal, then hands `say` that client's Connected; nothing may be said. Written in the scratch clone, it passed 3 of 3 on the head and failed with the first mutant.
  - The second mutant needs a test seam between `p.end()` and `retire`, as the send-retry fix's `look_slowly` hook is one.

**T2. Test gap: the ten-minute cap.**
- **Where:** `unfinished` gives a connected link its minute again only while less than `linkLongest` (10 minutes, `pairing.go:22`) has passed since pairing (`:133`).
- **Measured:** with the cap removed, every test passes. The existing test pins only that a connected link isn't cleared within 200 ms.
- **What breaks without it:** a link that is connected but never says Connected stays half made for as long as the connection lasts, and `link.start` refuses it all that time ("linking is finishing").
- **Fix:** make the cap a field the tests can shorten, as `finishing` is. Test that a connected link past it is cleared and says why.

### May stand as follow-ups

**O1. Other: the client retired as its half-made device is cleared keeps a live store.**
- **Where:** `forget` (`pairing.go:181`) disconnects the client, and `renew` deletes the device's rows through the container. The client's own `Store` is never marked deleted, as whatsmeow's `Store.Delete` would mark it.
- **Measured, with a probe:** after a clearing, the retired store had `Deleted` false and kept its ID. Its `Save` put the device back in session.db: 0 devices, then 1.
- **How it could happen:** only through a whatsmeow reconnect slipping in after `Disconnect`. That's `autoReconnect` between its wait and its connect, or the 515 reconnect's. The next start would then read the cleared device as the account.
- **Untested:** `forget`'s disconnect has no test; removing it passes every test.
- **Fix:**
  - In `forget`, delete the client's device with `Store.Delete`. That's whatsmeow's own idiom: a deleted store can neither connect nor save.
  - Disconnect through `reach`, so a test can check both.

**O2. Other: a logout whose session.db delete fails after WhatsApp has answered.**
- **What happens:**
  - whatsmeow's `Logout` returns "error deleting data from store" once WhatsApp has removed the device.
  - The engine says "can't log out" with retry (`engine.go:523`), and keeps the client and the device.
  - jawd keeps the account and says linked and online, since whatsmeow's own disconnect says nothing.
- **Measured, with a probe:** the reply was as above, and the client and device were kept. By whatsmeow's code, its Logout on the now disconnected client then fails sending its request, so another `jaw unlink` can't help.
- **Limits:** the next start reads it right, through WhatsApp's 401. It needs session.db's write to fail. Main's Logout did the same.
- **The protocol's text:** "engine_unavailable … the account linked as it was" isn't true in this case.
- **Fix:** once WhatsApp has answered, take the logout as done whatever the delete does. Retire and renew; renew's delete tries again, and if it can't, the next start's 401 finishes it. Answer done.

**O3. Other: an engine that stops while a link finishes**, which the builder left as it was.
- **What happens:**
  - whatsmeow has saved the half-made device. The next engine takes it for the account and names it as it connects, so jawd says linked before WhatsApp has taken the device.
  - If WhatsApp refuses it with a logout code, it reads as logged out from another device.
  - With any other refusal, jaw stays linked to a device it can neither use nor log out: logging out needs WhatsApp, and `link.start` refuses while an account is linked.
- **The protocol's text:** "A link the phone has paired isn't linked until it has finished" doesn't hold across that restart.
- **Follow-up:** note a pairing durably in `PrePairCallback`, before whatsmeow saves the device. The next engine can then treat that device as still finishing. Until then, say so in the protocol's text.

**O4. Hygiene: the protocol's README and the not-cleared refusal.**
- **Not in the README:**
  - `link.start` is also refused `engine_unavailable` while the device a logout or failed link left isn't cleared yet (`engine.go:589`, `:598`).
  - `link.logout` can answer `internal` when jaw.db can't forget the account (`sock.rs:917`).
  - O2's case.
- **Untested:** no test reaches the not-cleared refusal. Both of its mutants pass every test.

**O5. Other: what a cleared device leaves.**
- **Nothing of jaw's data:**
  - No connection event names a half-made device, so jawd stores no account or JIDs for it.
  - The device never connects again, apart from O1's window.
- **On the phone:** it stays in Linked devices, taking one of WhatsApp's companion slots, until it's removed there or WhatsApp drops it. Link failed could say so.
- **Rare cases where an accepted device is cleared:**
  - A connection that drops after its login and is between whatsmeow's reconnects at the minute's check is cleared, though WhatsApp had taken it. That is what the design's "isn't connected a minute after pairing" says.
  - A temporary ban or an outdated version at the first login clears the device, so it has to be linked again afterwards.

**O6. Other, for the lander: 0.1.0's notes.** 0.1.0 is published, so `docs/releases/0.1.0.md` stays as published. Drop `09de2ec0`'s hunk to it (line 53).

## What I checked

**Logging out deletes only the device, and forgets the account.**
- **The engine:**
  - whatsmeow's Logout sends remove-companion-device, disconnects, and deletes the device's row. Its other rows go with it through session.db's cascades, foreign keys being on.
  - `renew` then deletes any device left, and makes a fresh one in memory. That one is saved only when it pairs.
- **jawd:** `logged_out` makes one synced commit (`apply_durable`) emptying `account`, then updates the live state. It writes nothing else.
- **Measured:** in the probe, the first account's message stayed, read.
- **When WhatsApp can't be told:** a failure with retry, no device deleted and no jaw.db write. The builder's test covers it, and the code reads so.

**A crash at any point leaves a state the next start reads as not linked.**
- **Before WhatsApp answers:** nothing has changed.
- **After it answers, before session.db's delete:**
  - The device is still there, and the next engine connects with it.
  - WhatsApp refuses it with a logout code, as for a device removed from the phone while jaw was off. whatsmeow deletes it and says LoggedOut.
  - The engine says LOGGED_OUT and renews. At a fresh start the device isn't half made.
  - jawd forgets the account with a synced commit. Its reason reads "logged out from another device", not "from jaw".
- **After the delete, before jawd's commit:**
  - The next engine has no device and says UNLINKED.
  - jawd, starting linked from `account`, takes that as a logout ("this device isn't linked any more", `sync/mod.rs:753`) and forgets the account, synced. Main's test `an_account_logged_out_while_jawd_was_off_ends_up_logged_out_and_the_next_start_unlinked` covers this.
- **After the commit:** hello says unlinked.
- **A power cut:** session.db commits at `FULL` with `checkpoint_fullfsync` (`session.go:61-65`), so the delete reaches the drive before jaw.db's `F_FULLFSYNC`. A power cut that loses it brings the device back, and WhatsApp's refusal logs it out.
- **Not half-forgotten:** `me` is kept on purpose, as after the phone's logout (first-run-me), and so are the outbox, drafts and folders. F1 is what follows from keeping them.

**The owner's linked account takes its old paths.**
- The pairing lives in memory and is empty at start, so a device present at start isn't half made.
- `state`, `do`, `finished`, `loggedOut` and the refusals therefore behave as before.
- The one change it sees: an interface's `link.cancel` (`only_linking`) no longer disconnects it. That fixes Jaw.app's linking screen disconnecting an account just linked, which main does.

**A failed link's clearing.**
- **What clears a device:**
  - WhatsApp refusing it at its first login.
  - A PairError once the device had an ID or was deleted.
  - A minute unconnected after pairing.
  - Ten minutes connected without Connected.
  - `link.start` while the half-made client isn't connected.
- **Ten minutes is enough:** whatsmeow says Connected after the post-login IQs whether they succeed or not (`connectionevents.go:187-205`), each taking up to 75 s.
- **A slow phone changes nothing:** its part is done before PairSuccess, and the timers count only the client's connection.
- **A cleared client stays down:** `Disconnect` marks the disconnect as expected, which cancels a pending auto-reconnect (`client.go:643-675`), except for O1's window.

**The contract and the interfaces' states.**
- **`link.logout`:** `not_allowed` with no device or while a link finishes; `engine_unavailable` when WhatsApp can't be told (O2 aside).
- **`link.start`:** `not_allowed` while an account is linked; `engine_unavailable` while a paired link is connected and finishing. A half-made link that isn't connected is cleared first.
- **`link.cancel`:** with `only_linking`, it leaves any device with an ID connected. jawd's stop still disconnects whatever is connected.
- **The terminal:** it reads hello and asks first; `jaw link` asks `link.start` whatever hello says.
- **Jaw.app:** it sends `link.cancel` only while nothing is linked, and the engine would leave a linked account connected anyway.
- **The protocol's README** has every new path, but for O3's and O4's.

**Sync's refresh.**
- `linked_now` (on main already) now also brings the people-and-groups refresh at the first Connected after a link in the same engine's run, without the app-state refetch.
- J1 (the refresh keyed on restarts alone) fails its test.
- A Done whose Connected never came in its engine's run would leave `linked_now` set, and the next engine's refresh would skip its refetch. But the engine says Done and Connected back to back, and before that Connected whatsmeow has synced no app state, so nothing is missed.

**SQL and acknowledgements:** no new SQL, no schema change and no migration, so nothing to pin in the plan test. Nothing new is acknowledged.

- **What's new, three bullets:**
  - **Logging out from jaw.** `jaw unlink` logs jaw out of WhatsApp after saying what that does and asking (`--yes` doesn't ask), as do Log out of WhatsApp in the terminal's palette and Help › Log Out… in Jaw.app. jaw stops being one of your account's linked devices, and only your phone can link it again. jawd keeps your messages, media, folders, drafts and settings, and the linking screen follows, as after a logout from the phone.
  - **A failed link no longer leaves jaw stuck.** whatsmeow saves the device as the phone pairs, before WhatsApp has confirmed it. So a link that failed after that, WhatsApp refusing it or the connection not coming, could leave a half-made device, and linking again then said jaw was already linked. jaw-engine now clears it and says why, and linking starts afresh. `jaw link` links again when jawd only thought it was linked, and Jaw.app's linking screen no longer disconnects an account it has just linked.
  - **A folder links only its own account.** A folder whose jaw.db keeps an account's messages links only that account, so two accounts' messages never mix in one folder. The phone of another number is refused as it pairs, before anything of it is kept, and jaw and Jaw.app say which number the folder belongs to. Another account takes a folder of its own: `JAW_HOME=<folder> jaw`, or Jaw.app with `--home` or `JAW_HOME`. A folder that keeps no account's messages links any number. It goes by the number, so an account whose number has changed is refused too, until jaw can choose what becomes of the messages.
