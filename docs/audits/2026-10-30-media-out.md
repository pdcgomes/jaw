## What was asked, checked

### I4 (`28ab429c`)

- **The condition** (`shown_deleted!`, `store/write/outbox.rs:254`). Message `t` is deleted for everyone, or being, when:
  - it's deleted, or a revoke of it isn't failed;
  - or a copy of it, by ID, in a chat merging into the chat `t`'s ends in (its own, or the one it's being merged into) is;
  - or the copy in the chat `t`'s is being merged into is.

  Merges are flattened (`repoint` points the merges into a chat being merged at the chat kept), so a chat kept is never itself merging, and the two clauses reach every copy. Your deletion is done to the copy in the chat kept (`located`). A revoke that goes marks its message deleted in the transaction that drops its row (`acted`), so the condition has no gap.
- **Where it's used:**
  - the sender's read of an HD row (`OUTBOXED`, `:273`);
  - the writer's withdrawal (`HD_SHOWN_DELETED`, `:322`, in `withdrawn`, `:543`);
  - `tidied`'s `HD_OF_DELETED` (`:387`);
  - `act`'s refusal (`SHOWN_DELETED`, `:319`), unchanged for `act`, whose copy is always the one kept.
- **A deletion refused meanwhile:** the writer keeps an HD row whose message's deletion no longer holds, and the sender reads it again and sends it (`send/outbox.rs:436`). The sender's read and the writer's check are one expression on one join, so they can't disagree into a loop.
- **Plans:** `plans.txt` lists the four statements only as fragments of their `concat!`. The outbox's plan test (`every_statement_sending_runs_reads_an_index_and_sorts_nothing`) pins them whole: `OUTBOXED` by ID and by `n`, `SHOWN_DELETED`, `HD_SHOWN_DELETED` and `HD_OF_DELETED`. Each look is a search of a primary key or an index, and the test passes.

### Inconsistency

- **I4. A merge can leave an HD copy free to go after you delete its message for everyone.**
  - **Where:**
    - `act` takes the HD rows of the copy `located` finds in the chat the deletion names (`store/write/outbox.rs:572`, `:590`; `store/write.rs:1994`), and `deleted_for_everyone` those of the copy the amendment names (`:1136`); neither looks at the copy in the chat merging with it.
    - After the fold, `tidied`'s `HD_OF_DELETED` (`:362`) takes an HD row only if the copy kept is `deleted`. Your own deletion isn't that until it has gone: `act` only queues it, and `SHOWN_DELETED` (`:296`) and `RETRYABLE` count it as being deleted.
    - The sender withdraws an HD row by its own target's `deleted` alone (`OUTBOXED`, `:255`; `Outboxed::withdrawn`, `:1369`).
  - **What happens** (my store test, round one's I3 shape):
    - A photo of yours in HD under her LID, its SD message sent and its HD copy on its way, and the same message under her number, as history brings one.
    - You delete it for everyone from jaw in the chat under her number. The HD row and its copy stay, the copy found having none.
    - Then her two JIDs are found to be one person, and the merge folds the LID's copy into the one being deleted. After the fold:
      - the HD row is queued, targeting a message with your deletion on its way;
      - it's neither withdrawn nor held nor an orphan;
      - `retryable` says it may not go again.
    - So the sender sends the HD copy as soon as its upload is done, until your deletion has gone and its amendment's `deleted_for_everyone` takes the row.
    - The same holds before the fold, while the HD row names the LID's copy, which nothing deletes.
    - A deletion from your phone landing on the copy under her number leaves the HD copy free to go until the fold, which then takes it (the builder's S1e test).
  - **Why it matters:** it's I1's harm. jaw.db says the message is being deleted for everyone (`send.retry` refuses its HD copy for that reason) while the sender sends its full-quality copy. An HD video's copy is on its way for minutes, and that's when a message sent to the wrong chat is deleted.
  - **Fix:**
    - Decide an HD row is withdrawn by what `SHOWN_DELETED` says of its target: deleted for everyone, or being, there or in a copy merging with it. Do it in the sender's read and the writer's `withdrawn`, so a deletion or a fold committed after the row's read is caught.
    - Give `tidied`'s `HD_OF_DELETED` the same condition, so the fold removes the row rather than leave it for the sender.
    - Or have `act` and `deleted_for_everyone` take the HD rows of every copy of the message in the chats merging with the one found.
    - Pin the statements.
  - **Test:** mine, `audit_r2_your_deletion_of_the_copy_kept_leaves_the_hd_copy_a_merge_folds_into_it`, in the store's outbox tests, asserting no HD row is left to go after the merge. ### Replays

- **Each step only from the state before it.** `PREPARED` changes only a copy `preparing`, and `UPLOADED` only one `uploading` with its hash (`store/write/outgoing.rs:127`, `:141`). Without either guard, `a_send_cancelled_after_its_copy_is_made_or_uploaded_but_before_jaw_db_is_told_stays_failed` fails (M12a, M12b). `COPY_FAILED` and `UNSENT` change only a copy still on its way and a row still queued, so a cancel or a failure that loses its race with the upload changes nothing, and the message goes.
- **A cancel, then `send.retry`, then the cancelled job's late step:** the late step finds the copy `preparing` again and applies, which is right, since its copy is really made and in the cache; the retry's own job then changes nothing.
- **`send.media` again.** Its rows are written with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` (the message, its outbox row, its copy), and an HD copy's own row is guarded by its ID's tombstone. The key is looked for on a reader before anything is kept (`send/media.rs:136`) and again in the transaction (`store/write/outbox.rs:848`). With both looks off, `the_same_key_answers_with_the_same_messages_and_sends_nothing_new` fails (M24a with M24b); with either alone, nothing does (T5).
- **A send queued before a stop that WhatsApp then refuses** fails, since it waited for its copy in this run and so can't have gone before (`send/outbox.rs:460`). Without that, `a_send_refused_once_its_copy_is_ready_at_the_next_start_fails_since_it_cant_have_gone_before` fails (M19). The rule holds because a copy's `ready` is final: nothing moves a copy out of it.
- **A step jaw.db can't keep** fails its message once, with its error in quarantine, rather than being made or uploaded again (`outgoing.rs:673`, `:787`). With the quarantine ignored, `a_copy_jaw_db_cant_say_is_made_or_uploaded_fails_its_message_rather_than_going_round_again` fails (M11a, M11b).
- **A kill at each commit.**
  - Kill tests cover: after `send.media`'s answer (the copy is made from jaw's copy at the next start), mid-make, mid-upload, and once the message was taken (sent again with the same keys, nothing uploaded again).
  - A kill between `link` and the made copy's commit leaves a file media in's start sweep removes; between the drop's commit and the removal, a file media out's start sweep removes.

### Inconsistency

- **I1. An HD copy goes after its sent message is deleted for everyone.**
  - **Where:**
    - `deleted_for_everyone` (`store/write/outbox.rs:1099`) runs `ORPHANED_HD` (`:349`), which spares an HD row whose message reached the server;
    - `Outboxed::withdrawn` (`:1328`) withdraws reactions, edits and votes, not `hd` rows;
    - `send.retry`'s HD path (`send/outbox.rs:586`) queues the HD copies `hd_failed` finds without asking `RETRYABLE` whether their message may be sent again;
    - an HD row waiting for its copy keeps the front of its chat's queue (`send/outbox.rs:448`, `work/serial.rs`), so an action queued after it waits.
  - **What happens,** in three tests of mine, each failing at the head. A photo sent in HD has its SD message gone and its HD copy still uploading; then:
    - deleted for everyone from your phone, the HD row stays, and the HD copy goes once its upload is done (sent: the message, then its HD copy);
    - with the HD copy's upload refused, then deleted for everyone from your phone, `send.retry` on the message answers ok and sends the HD copy;
    - deleted for everyone from jaw, `delete` answers at once, but its revoke waits behind the HD copy, which goes first (sent: the message, its HD copy, then the revoke).
  - **Why it matters:** jaw.db holds the message as deleted for everyone while jaw sends its full-quality copy to the chat. A video's HD copy can take minutes to make and upload (P2, P3), which is when a message sent to the wrong chat gets deleted.
  - **Fix:**
    - an HD copy never goes once its message is deleted for everyone, or being: `deleted_for_everyone` deletes the message's HD rows whatever its status (their copies' rows and files go with the cascade and the triggers);
    - `withdrawn()` takes `hd` rows too, for a deletion committed between the row's read and its send;
    - your deletion for everyone of a message with an HD copy on its way withdraws that copy in `act`'s transaction, so the revoke doesn't wait behind it;
    - `send.retry`'s HD path asks `RETRYABLE` of the message, as the message's own retry does.
  - **Tests:** my three, in the diff, as they are.

- **I2. A failed HD copy whose sent message is deleted for you is kept for good.**
  - **Where:** `deleted_for_you` (`store/write/outbox.rs:1090`).
    - `FORGOTTEN` takes the failed rows naming the message by `message`, which an HD row doesn't have, and `ORPHANED_HD` spares an HD row whose message reached the server.
    - The message's row then goes, setting the HD row's `target` and its copy's `message` to NULL.
    - The rule for an orphaned HD row (`send/outbox.rs:435`) reaches only queued rows.
  - **What happens** (my test): a photo sent in HD, its HD upload refused, its SD message sent, then deleted for you. The HD row stays, failed, with its copy's row and its place toward `OUTBOX_MOST`. `send.retry` on the message answers error 3, and nothing ever removes them. `KEPT` keeps the copy's file in the cache past the cache's size for as long.
  - **What it breaks:** "every failed outbox row of a message of yours can be sent again by `send.retry`, so whatever makes one impossible to send removes it in the same transaction" (`tidied`'s comment, `:1074`), and the outbox's and the cache's bounds.
  - **Fix:** a deletion for you takes the message's HD rows, whatever their state and its status. The orphan rule stays, for a deletion committed after the row was read, and for whatever else removes a message's row.
  - **Test:** mine, in the diff.

- **I3. A merge's fold leaves an HD copy on its way without its message.**
  - **Where:** `folded` (`store/write/outbox.rs:1499`) moves the outbox's `message` and `target` and the send keys to the copy kept; nothing moves `outgoing.message`, which goes NULL when the copy folded is deleted (`store/write.rs:1865`).
  - **What happens** (my store test): a photo of yours sent under a person's LID, its HD copy uploading, and the same message under her number, as history brings one. Once the merge has run, the HD row's target is the kept message, and its copy's message is NULL. So:
    - the HD copy goes with no media row, since `COPIED` and `KEYED` skip a copy with no message;
    - the message isn't given a new version for it, and its job pushes stop;
    - once it has gone, `outgoing_unnamed` drops its file, so jaw keeps nothing of the HD copy it sent.
  - **Fix:** `folded` moves it too, `UPDATE outgoing SET message = ?1 WHERE message = ?2`, pinned in the plan test.
  - **Test:** mine, in the diff.

### Unpredictable performance

None of these breaks a data budget, and each can stand as a follow-up; P1's fix comes naturally with T2, and P3's with I1.

- **P1. The 8 GB bound is checked after the files are kept, and outside the writer** (`send/media.rs:177` to `:179`).
  - Where the disk can't clone (another volume, ext4, an external disk), a request of 30 files of 2 GB writes 60 GB into jaw's folder before it's refused, and requests at once each pass the same reader's sum.
  - `keep` checks no free space first. A full disk is transient to the writer, which tries again, so nothing is lost, but every write waits meanwhile.
  - **Fix:** sum the files' sizes from their metadata against the bound and the disk's room before keeping any, and check `kept` plus the request again in `attach`'s transaction, refused as `Full` is.
- **P2. A video's SD copy waits for its HD copy to be made before it uploads.**
  - `take` marks both copies' rows running for the whole making job (`media/outgoing.rs:392`), so the SD copy, made first, isn't uploaded until the job ends.
  - Measured: a 40 s 1080p video sent in HD had its SD copy made at 4.4 s and its HD copy at 9.9 s, and no upload was asked before then. The protocol says "an SD copy uploads while its HD one is made".
  - **Fix:** take a copy's row out of `running` once it's placed.
- **P3. Everything sent to a chat after an HD send waits behind its HD copy.** The HD row keeps the front of its chat's queue while its copy is made and uploaded (`send/outbox.rs:448`), so texts, reactions and deletions wait for the HD transcode and upload; I1's third path rides on it. The SD message's place keeps the chat's order, as the phones do; the HD copy, a child of a message already sent, doesn't need one.
- **P4. `send.media`'s transaction grows with its files and the group's members.** Each message of yours in a group gets a recipients row per member in the same transaction (`RECIPIENTS`, `store/write/outbox.rs:354`). For 31 messages in a group of 1,024, that statement alone took 22 to 27 ms on a plain connection at load 55, against the live writer's 5 ms. Main's forward has the same shape; both could write recipients in short background steps, as regrouping does.
- **P5. Media out's floor is 64 MB free** (`media/mod.rs:116`, `send_keep_free`), against background downloads' 512 MB, so copies can take the disk down to their size plus 64 MB, where jaw.db's WAL may need up to 64 MiB.

### Other (follow-ups)
