### Hygiene (follow-ups)

**H10. The branch's idle test fails when the writer's steps come after the WAL is emptied.**
- **Where:** `an_idle_store_empties_its_wal_and_then_its_checkpointer_sleeps_until_the_next_commit` (`store/mod.rs:2377`), from `7efd9da` (`fff4b92` in the first audit).
- **What happens:**
  - The test commits a lone message, waits for the WAL to be emptied, then expects the checkpointer to sleep through ten idle periods.
  - But the message's index step and merge step commit after it. If the writer is held off for longer than the 50 ms idle period, the checkpointer empties the WAL before those steps run.
  - The test then starts watching, and the two commits wake the checkpointer.
- **Waiting for indexing first still leaves one wake-up:**
  - A merge step that merges nothing commits an empty transaction.
  - `committed()` (`store/mod.rs:854`) kicks the checkpointer after every commit.
  - So once the WAL is empty, that kick costs one more wake-up, when the idle timeout after it runs out.
- **Measured,** with a switch, `IDLESLOW`, that holds the writer 80 ms before each index step:
  - **As written:**
    - it failed once in about fifteen runs of the store's suite, at a load of 36 to 67, with 5 wake-ups where 3 were expected;
    - alone, it passed 40 of 40 at a load of 52 to 58;
    - under the hold, it failed 10 of 10, by 6 wake-ups each time.
  - **Waiting for `store.indexed()` before watching the WAL:** it passed 10 of 10 without the hold. Under the hold it failed 10 of 10, each time by exactly one wake-up.
  - **That wait, plus a change to `committed()`** (a switch, `FIXKICK`):
    - The change: `committed()` does nothing after a commit that SQLite's WAL hook didn't report, so a commit that wrote nothing leaves the frame count and the checkpointer alone.
    - It passed 10 of 10 under the hold, and 10 of 10 without.
    - It still fails under the first audit's M3, a checkpointer that keeps its timer once the WAL is empty: 5 of 5, with and without the hold.
    - The whole test binary gives the same results with the change as without it.
- **Fix before pushing, since the gate can trip on it.** Either:
  - make both changes above; or
  - in the test alone, wait for `store.indexed()` and allow one wake-up. Every failure under the hold, once indexing was waited for, was off by exactly one, and M3 gives nine.

  The change to `committed()` changes the writer, so it needs a look of its own. The test's change needs none.

### Missing test (blocks the approval)

**T1. Nothing tests that a live write riding an inbox commit gets its text indexed.**
- **Where:** `store/mod.rs:1008`, `self.backlog = true` after an inbox commit that carried live writes. This branch added that line.
- **What happens:** since `92f503c`, every new message's text waits in `fts_backlog`, and only the `backlog` flag makes the writer index it. During a catch-up, every durable live write goes into an inbox group's synced commit, and so do the live writes queued behind it: a send, a read, a draft, a conversion. Line 1008 is the only thing that makes their text indexed.
- **Measured:** with the line removed (mutation M6), the entire suite passes, 333 tests; only my probe fails (and the plan test, which trips on my scratch file's SQL).
- **Effect of such a regression:** a message sent during a catch-up would be stored and shown, but not found by search until some later transaction set the flag or jawd restarted. On a quiet account that could last indefinitely.
- **Fix:** a test that does the following, failing without line 1008:
  - streams inbox rows;
  - makes a durable live write with text, so it rides a group's commit (assert that `storage().shared_writes` moved);
  - stops the stream and writes nothing more;
  - finds the text.

  My probe does exactly that: `audit_a_live_write_that_rides_an_inbox_commit_is_indexed` in `/tmp/jaw-budgets-audit/mut/crates/jawd/src/store/audit.rs`. It passes on the branch and fails with the line removed.

### Unpredictable performance (a follow-up)

**P1. An upgrade through migration 11 leaves jawd above its steady memory budget until it restarts.**
- **Where:** `store/mod.rs:327` sets `temp_store = 2` (memory) on every connection, the writer's included. Data steps run on the writer (`store/schema.rs:883-901`).
- **Measured,** on the million-message demo account at version 10, sampling from outside:
  - jawd reached 179 MB resident during the upgrade and stayed there;
  - its idle minute afterwards read 130 MB footprint and 186 MB resident;
  - the same account already at version 11 idles at 75 and 81 MB.
- **Why:** `CREATE INDEX` sorts in memory, since temp files are in memory, and the memory isn't given back. The extra grows with the database. The owner's 73 MB jaw.db would see roughly a tenth of it. The demo's million messages run 30 MB of footprint over the 100 MB budget, 55 MB above the same account without the upgrade, for the one run after it.
- **Fix:** run data steps with `temp_store = FILE`, set back afterwards, and run `PRAGMA shrink_memory` after migrating. Or measure it and say so. It needs no further audit.

- **Measured on jawd's own writer connection** (`connect(Role::Writer)`), on 1,000,709 messages at a load of 27 to 30:

  - the backup took 2.2 to 3.1 s for a 595 MB jaw.db.
- **On a plain connection** (2 MB cache and SQLite's automatic checkpoints, as `measure_the_room_the_data_steps_take` opens one), the same step took 4.97 and 8.42 s, nearly all of it a checkpoint inside the commit. That fits the written 3.4 to 6.8 s.
- **Fix:** both are within 10 s per million. Write the cost as jawd runs it, and say how it was measured. The sentence is a Rust comment, outside the hashed SQL, so it can be corrected after merge too.

**H2. Migration 11 doesn't declare a class.**
- **Where:** `store/schema.rs:696-708`.
- **What:** every migration since 5 names the class of what it changes; migration 10, for example, says "Class: jaw's own rows of the outbox". This one replaces an index of `messages`, which can always be rebuilt from the rows, so nothing is at risk.
- **Fix:** a line such as "Class: an index, derived from messages' own columns", in the Rust comment. Before merge it could also go in the SQL, with the lock's hash updated.

**H3. A sync test races the history drain, and fails more often on this branch.**
- **Where:** `sync/tests.rs:1421-1425`, `a_history_blob_the_engine_couldnt_ask_for_is_never_quarantined`.
- **What happens:** the test waits for the blob's message row, then asserts at once that its notification has drained. The drain is a separate synced commit after the rows.
- **Measured:**
  - The test failed in my gate run, 2 of 10 runs alone on the branch, and 0 of 10 on the base at a similar load.
  - A variant that waits for the drain passed 10 of 10 on each build. On the branch the drain came 0.4 to 22 ms after the rows (median 3.5 ms); on the base, 0.4 to 3.2 ms (median 0.7 ms). The drain now also waits for the walk's thread to finish, which likely accounts for the wider gap; I didn't establish the cause.
  - The gap is harmless: the notification stays until its drain commits, and a crash in between stores the blob again, idempotently.
- **Fix:** wait for the drain before asserting, for example `until("both to wait", || store.count("inbox").unwrap() == 2)`. Worth doing before pushing, since CI would trip on it.

**H4. The tests' check of FTS5 can't see a stale index.**
- **Where:** `store/mod.rs:1701` and `sync/tests.rs:717` run FTS5's `integrity-check` at rank 0.
- **What:** rank 0 checks only the index's own consistency, and passes an index made stale by hand; my probe shows that. Rank 1, which compares the index with its content table, can't be used in jaw. It wants every row of `messages` indexed, so a message without text (a revoked one, a photo without a caption) fails it, as the probe also shows.
- **Fix:** an exact check: FTS5's postings, read through `fts5vocab(main, messages_fts, instance)`, against a fresh FTS5 index of `messages.text` built with the same tokenizer, for the rows not in `fts_backlog`. That's `index_against_text` in my probe file. It caught the stale index and passed every scenario below.

**H5. Nothing pins that a commit between the WAL's emptying and the checkpointer's sleep still wakes it.**
- **Where:** `store/mod.rs:1420-1440`.
- **Why it holds:** the kick channel keeps one pending kick (`bounded(1)`, `:427`), and the checkpointer reaches `recv()` only after its `TRUNCATE`, so a kick sent meanwhile is still there when it sleeps.
- **Measured:**
  - My probe parks the checkpointer at that point with a test hook, commits, then lets it go to sleep; the WAL is emptied again after the idle time.
  - With pending kicks dropped before the sleep (M4, a lost wake-up), the probe fails with 57,712 bytes left in the WAL, and every store and sync test still passes.
- **Why it doesn't block:** a wake-up lost there loses nothing. The frames are committed, and the next commit wakes the checkpointer.
- **Fix, when convenient:** adopt the probe.

**H6. Only one direction of `touches_fts` is tested.**
- **Where:** `store/write.rs:552-561`.
- **Measured:**
  - With every live write asking for a merge, as before the branch (M12), `a_long_catch_up_has_its_text_indexed_as_it_goes` fails, as the builder says.
  - With none asking (M7), the store and sync suites pass. Nothing tests that a live edit or deletion of an indexed message is followed by merging.
- **Why it doesn't block:** search is right either way. What depends on it is the design's erasure rule, deleted words merged out of FTS5's segments, which no test covers before or after this branch.
- **Fix:** when erasure gets its test, delete an indexed message live and check that a merge step follows.

**H7. Text that comes in a later copy of a message is indexed in that copy's commit, not through the backlog.**
- **Where:** `store/write.rs:1630` puts only new rows in the backlog; the update trigger is at `store/schema.rs:519-523`.
- **What:** a message stored without text (a photo, a placeholder) whose later copy brings some has it indexed by the trigger inside that live write. My probe finds it at once, with nothing in the backlog. That's correct, but in that case FTS5's work at commit lands in a live write, which the commit message says no longer happens. Edits do the same, by design.
- **Fix:** none needed now. Say so in the comment, or have such rows wait in the backlog too.

**H8. Indexing waits for the WAL by looking again every millisecond.**
- **Where:** `store/mod.rs:788-793` and `:806-814`.
- **What:** while an index step finds no room, the writer comes back every `INDEX_PAUSE` to look again, as background batches do with `HOLD_POLL` (`:1189`). The demo-load audit's P2 asked for two things:
  - the kick, which is done: each wait now ends within a checkpointer pass, and a million messages indexed in 71.6 s here;
  - no polling, which isn't done. It happens only while work waits, never when idle.
- **Fix:** block on the checkpointer's progress, through a channel or a condition variable.

**H9. Lone live writes less than a millisecond apart hold indexing off.**
- **Where:** `store/mod.rs:798-801` and `:1279-1282`.
- **What:** the 100 ms stream step applies only after an inbox group or a conversion of several rows. Other live writes don't set `live_at`, so indexing waits for a gap of 1 ms. My first burst probe wrote back to back and saw no step until it stopped. Interfaces don't write that fast, so this doesn't arise in practice.
- **Fix, if it ever matters:** let any live write start the stream step, without the pause.

## What I checked, and how it came out

**The blob walk (`a216dc5`)**
- **A bad or truncated blob changes nothing.**
  - `check()` (`convert/history.rs:127-151`) decodes every message, conversation, status and the head as prost would before `take()` is first called. Once it passes, the same bytes can't fail later.
  - The builder's test tries every truncation and three bit flips at every byte. It fails without the check (M9).
  - In `sync/fetch.rs:203-208` a blob that doesn't decode is removed and fetched again, as before, with nothing handed over.
- **A blob delivered twice, or out of order with live messages, gives the same rows.**
  - The walk produces the same changes in the same order as decoding whole. Only the chunk boundaries now span conversations, and every write is an idempotent upsert settled per transaction.
  - The same 200,000-message history went through the base and the branch. Every history row is identical except `at`, shifted by the 92 to 93 s between the runs because the demo builds history relative to now, and `raw`, which carries those times. People and chat settings are identical. `history_kept` has the same structure and byte length row for row.
  - I SIGKILLed the branch during the fifth blob and started it again: exactly 200,000 distinct history messages, one `history_kept` row per blob, and an empty inbox, quarantine and spool.
- **The byte-for-byte `history_kept` can't make a replay differ.**
  - Nothing in jawd reads `history_kept` back. It's upserted by the offer's ID, and the same blob gives the same bytes.
  - What the walk leaves out of the rest is a subset of what the old code left out. The old code dropped any entry whose ID became a row anywhere in the blob; the walk drops only an entry whose own conversion made a row with its raw. So it keeps at least as much.
- **Memory stays bounded for the largest blobs.** The walk holds the encoded blob, the rest, the chunk being converted, at most two chunks waiting (`HANDED`) and the one being stored. One blob of 200,000 messages (37.3 MB) peaked at 165 MB footprint and 171 MB resident, within the 250 MB history budget. The other runs are in the Measurements.

**The checkpointer (`fe4fd33`, `fff4b92`)**
- **No wake-up is lost, for a commit landing between finding the WAL empty and going to sleep:** H5's probe.
- **Indexing past the soft limit wakes it.** `hold()` (`store/mod.rs:1199-1205`) sets `held` and kicks once per wait, and the builder's test fails without either (M1, M2). A hold can't be left set: only an index step that has room clears `backlog` and `unmerged`, so the writer keeps coming back until it does.
- **A clean stop and its marker still empty the WAL.**
  - The marker's durable write kicks the checkpointer, then the writer's own `TRUNCATE` empties the WAL.
  - A checkpointer asleep in `recv()` runs no checkpoint that could make the writer's `TRUNCATE` wait, and its loop ends when the writer's side of the channel drops.
  - Every stop I made took 29 to 44 ms and left `jaw.db-wal` at 0 bytes.
- **The 64 MiB bound for history holds as it did:** 12,411 frames (48.8 MiB) at a million messages, and 12,384 to 12,399 frames (49 MiB) at 200,000 on both builds.

**The search backlog (`92f503c`)**
- **Nothing is lost from the index, even across a kill mid-step.** An index step inserts into FTS5 and deletes the backlog rows in one transaction, and at start the backlog is read from the table. I SIGKILLed jawd during history and, separately, during indexing:
  - at each kill, every indexed message matched its text exactly and no backlog row named a missing message;
  - after each restart, every message with text was indexed and matched it (200,393 and 200,339).
- **An edit or a deletion of a message still in the backlog leaves no stale or deleted text findable.** My probe covered:
  - an edit, a deletion for everyone, a deletion for you and a plain deletion, each on messages already indexed and on messages still waiting, each in a transaction of its own;
  - an edit that arrived before its message.

  Nothing stale or deleted was found before or after indexing, and the index matched the text exactly. A message waiting in the backlog was never in FTS5, so its old text never went in.
- **A disappearing message's expiry:** nothing in jawd acts on `expires_at` yet, so there's no path to check. When it comes, it should delete or blank through the same triggers.
- **A chat merge leaves no backlog row pointing at a moved or removed message.**
  - Moves keep each message's `n`, and the delete trigger removes the row of a folded or tombstoned copy.
  - My probe merged a number's chat (indexed) into its LID's (waiting), with one message only in each, the same message with a later edit in the LID's chat, and one deleted for you in the number's. The kept copy is found once, as last edited; the deleted one isn't found in either; no backlog row names a missing message.
- **The backlog is reported in `jawd status`** as `stored.unindexed` ("N messages to index for search"). It holds one integer key per message waiting and is indexed newest first. Like the inbox, it has no cap: it grows with a burst and drains after, a step every 100 ms meanwhile.
- **`find` during a burst may be incomplete, but is never wrong.** My probe made 1,500 live writes with edits, deletions for everyone and deletions for you among them, 3 ms apart, while searching. Its 9,810 searches checked 829,282 hits, and every one had the word and its marks. Afterwards the backlog was empty and the index matched the text exactly.

**Migration 11 (`724cbde`)**
- **Its kind:** a data step is right by the design's definitions. It builds an index on `messages`, whose cost grows with the rows, and it can't be a backfill, since SQLite builds an index in one statement. Unlike migration 10 (the M2 audit's H20), the backup and the whole-database `foreign_key_check` are what the design asks of such a step. The written cost includes them, but overstates the step (H1).
- **Its class, lock entry and fingerprint:**
  - its class isn't declared (H2);
  - line 11 of `migrations.lock` says `data` with the text's hash, and the test checks it;
  - the expected fingerprint is computed from the migrations themselves, and the upgrade from every earlier version passes.
- **A fault at every statement:** the existing test injects a failure at each of its two statements from every earlier version, and leaves jaw.db as it was. It passed in the gate.
- **The owner's first run from version 1:** with the generated version-1 file of the spike's shape (`/tmp/audit-frm-fixture`) and `--engine demo`, the branch and the base end with the same rows. The only differences are the demo engine's three live messages, stamped with each run's time. The branch's file is at version 11, with `integrity_check` ok, no foreign-key violations, an empty backlog and an index matching the text exactly.

**The unread recount**
- **Exact against its source:** on three version-11 files, every chat's count through the index equals the count from the table, and the stored counts equal the derived ones, one chat at the 1,000 cap among them. The files were the branch's history account, the same after a catch-up, and one migrated from version 10. `integrity_check` was ok on all three.
- **Pinned:** `plans.txt` pins the five statements that now read only the index (the recount, and the read and receipt lookups beside it) as `COVERING INDEX messages_by_time`, with no temporary b-tree. Two foreign-key lookups from `chats` moved to the narrower `messages_by_id`, also covering. No other plan changed. The index grew from 17.2 to 28.3 MB per million messages.

**The engine's memory (`24629d0`)** sets `GOMEMLIMIT=160MiB` and `GOGC=50` unless jawd's own environment has them, and the builder's test checks that. It changes no data path. I didn't measure it with a real history sync, since I can't run jaw-engine on an account. The design's 200 MB during a full sync is a budget, not a measurement; a Go heap above 160 MiB would only make its collector run more often, capped at half the CPU.
