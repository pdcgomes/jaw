### Fix before pushing

**T1. Three of the branch's tests have timing bounds a loaded machine can break.**
- **Severity:** hygiene: the code is right, and each test catches what it's for.

**The two tests of the checkpointer's flush need the writer to outrun a pass within 15 s.**
- **Where:** `the_checkpointer_flushes_jaw_db_after_a_pass_the_writer_outran_and_not_after_one_that_reached_the_end` (`:3024-3038`) and `the_checkpointer_flushes_jaw_db_without_letting_go_of_this_processs_locks_on_it` (`:3093-3115`), both through `converting_until(.., flushed > 0)` (`:3027`, `:3102`), which stops after 15 s.
- **What happens:** the checkpointer flushes only after a pass the writer outran, so a commit has to land during a pass. Ten-message conversions back to back usually do that within seconds. In a whole suite on a loaded machine, they can go 15 s without one.
- **Seen:** the builder's gate failed the first, and mine failed the second ("the checkpointer never flushed jaw.db", at a load of 54 to 70). Run 8 at a time, 48 runs passed at a load of 61 to 64, so it takes a whole suite's contention.
- **Fix:** make sure some pass is outrun, with a deadline, rather than waiting for one:
  - live writes all the while, and among them bursts of 4,000 messages, whose passes the live writes land in;
  - until the checkpointer has flushed, or 60 s;
  - both tests call it, as `outrunning_a_pass(&store)`, in place of `converting_until`.

  Alone, the first test then takes 2.1 to 3.9 s in all, at a load of about 68.

  It catches both of the first test's mutants, 3 of 3 each. With no flush after an outrun pass, it fails at its 60 s deadline; with a flush after every pass, in about 2 s. In three whole store suites at a load of 73 to 91, it passed every time, but so did the tests as written: making them fail took the whole gate's suite. So the case for it is its construction: each burst makes a pass long enough that a live write lands in it. The helper:

  ```rust
  /// Live writes all the while, and among them bursts long enough to copy for one of those
  /// writes to land in their pass, until the checkpointer has flushed jaw.db after one, however
  /// busy the machine, or for 60 s.
  async fn outrunning_a_pass(store: &Store) {
      let stop = Arc::new(AtomicBool::new(false));
      let writes = {
          let (store, stop) = (store.clone(), stop.clone());
          tokio::spawn(async move { converting(&store, &stop).await })
      };
      let (began, mut n) = (Instant::now(), 100_000);
      while store.wal.flushed.load(Ordering::Relaxed) == 0 && began.elapsed() < Duration::from_secs(60) {
          store.apply(Lane::Live, burst(n, 4_000)).await.unwrap();
          n += 4_000;
      }
      stop.store(true, Ordering::Relaxed);
      writes.await.unwrap();
  }
  ```

  A test-only pause in the checkpointer between its pass and its decision, with a commit made meanwhile, would make the first test exact. But that tests the decision rather than SQLite's skipped sync, and the burst form covers both.

**The reader test's 2 s bound on an ack is short of what a try at the edge may take with the drive busy.**
- **Where:** `a_reader_on_the_wals_frames_holds_acks_up_only_a_moment_and_the_wal_starts_again_once_it_lets_go` (`:2797-2836`), whose `slowest < 2 s` is at `:2823`.
- **What happens:** the test takes the WAL past the edge behind the reader, on purpose. Once the reader lets go, the writer's try at the edge waits up to RESTART_MOST (1 s) for the checkpointer's pass over what the reader held, by design, then makes its own copy and two flushes. The rows that arrive meanwhile go in groups of 256, each a synced commit.
- **Seen:** it failed 1 of my 6 store suites ("an ack of 5069 took 2.21 s", at a load of 74 to 77), and 2 of 32 runs 16 at a time (2.06 and 2.07 s, at 71 to 72). The transaction log of each of those two shows the same thing:
  - one try at the edge took 1.38 to 1.41 s and started the WAL again;
  - 1,421 to 1,423 rows were queued by its end;
  - synced group commits took up to 470 to 550 ms.

  Run 8 at a time at 55 to 60, 24 runs passed, with 372 of 611 tries at the edge and the longest 1.02 s. In my floods, 1 try of 247 was at the edge, so this is the test's case rather than a common one.
- **Fix:** give the bound room for a try at the edge, RESTART_MOST and the restart's two flushes, and hold the reader longer than that bound, so the test still tells a moment from the whole hold.

Both fixes are in tests alone, so they need no further audit.

### Unpredictable performance (follow-ups)

**P1 (the first audit's), measured again: in a flood with acks released in groups, about 2.5% of the acks wait behind a restart's pause, which is two flushes of the drive.**
- **The builder's quiet-machine figures stand untested here.** The machine's load stayed between 37 and 95 throughout this audit, so I couldn't run the pairs at 28 or less.
- **At a busy machine's load** (four interleaved pairs, 50 to 68), the head was better in 2 and worse in 2:
  - the head's ack p99 was 65.2 to 471.8 ms, main's 58.2 to 409.1, the spread in each build wider than any difference between them;
  - the head's WAL peaked at 60.5 to 61.1 MiB, main's at 290.0 to 852.1;
  - every flood kept up with 2,000 a second, and nothing went missing.

  With the first audit's five pairs at 50 to 83, the head was better in 4 of 9 and worse in 5. So above a load of 50, I find no consistent difference in the p99, and don't find the builder's 9 of 12 better, though the builder's pairs went down to 29, where I have none. The difference in the WAL is consistent.
- **What the pause is made of,** from six floods with the transaction log, at a load of 54 to 85:
  - each restart held the writer 43 to 67 ms at p50 on the head, 88 to 110 ms at p90;
  - a reader held up 1 try in 247, and none in the four runs that wait for readers, so readers aren't the cost;
  - the frames left to copy matter little: with fewer than 256 left, a restart held the writer 28 to 56 ms at p50 in each run; with more, 37 to 67 ms.

  What's left is SQLite's: a checkpoint that reaches the WAL's end syncs the WAL before it copies (`sqlite3.c:69713`) and `jaw.db` after (`:69769`). It does both inside the window where no commit may land, or it wouldn't reach the end.
- **Who waits:** the rows that arrive during the pause, which go in the next inbox group: 2.0 to 2.7% of each flood's 60,000 acks. That's more than 1%, so it sets the p99 and leaves the p50 alone, which fits the builder's quiet-machine result (worse at p99 in 7 of 8 pairs, 47.8 ms against 30.7).
- **It should not block landing; it stands as P1, beside P2:**
  - the design requires the bound, which main breaks 4.5 to 13 times over in these floods (the data review's D3, the demo-load audit's P1);
  - neither build meets the 10 ms budget with 256 acks in flight;
  - grouped acks are the demo engine's. jaw-engine acks one at a time, so a restart delays at most the one row in flight, once each 60 MiB written, and the first audit's catch-ups showed no clear difference.

  P1 and P2 are one cost seen twice: the pause's two flushes, and the third the next transaction makes as it syncs the new header. That third shows as the first inbox commit after a restart taking 14.5 to 54.0 ms at p50, against 11.3 to 18.2 for the rest.
- **The cheapest way to take restarts off the acks' path:** there's no cheap way to take them off entirely while inbox commits share `jaw.db`'s WAL, since rows that arrive during the pause can't commit until it ends. In order of cost:
  1. **Shorter pauses, one line:** the checkpointer copying however few frames wait once the WAL is past WAL_SOFT, as it does while background work waits. In two floods, that halved the frames left to copy (p50 219 to 229, against 381 to 436) and brought the pause to 30 to 45 ms at p50. It can't remove the two flushes, and the ack p99s (85.0 and 198.2 ms, against the head's 141.6 and 180.1) don't separate at that load. It adds passes between WAL_SOFT and WAL_HARD, each with two flushes of its own; inbox commits didn't slow there (p50 11.3 to 14.9 ms, against 12.3 to 16.0), but it wants a quiet machine's pairs before it's adopted.
  2. **Fewer pauses:** a WAL bound three times larger would put fewer than 1% of a flood's acks behind one, under the p99. But 64 MiB is the design's number, so that's the owner's call.
  3. **Off the path entirely:** the inbox in a database file of its own, with its own small WAL, so an ack waits for the inbox's synced commit and never for `jaw.db`'s restart. Conversion would delete the row after its own commit, and replay idempotently after a crash. That changes the design, where an inbox row goes in its conversion's transaction, so it isn't cheap.
  4. **Not worth it:** not waiting for readers while rows are queued. Readers held 1 try in 247, and in two floods the pause wasn't shorter for it (56 and 101 ms at p50).

  For P2's third flush, the first audit's fix stands: start the WAL's next generation inside the pause, with a small write of the writer's own.

### Must change before landing

**W1. A restart a reader holds up can leave background work waiting for the checkpointer's 10-second idle timer.**
- **Severity:** unpredictable performance. It's a regression of a rule main enforces and tests: background work waiting for the WAL is woken by the checkpointer's passes, never left to its idle timer. Main's tests are `background_work_waiting_for_the_wal_gets_room_with_nothing_else_committing` and `indexing_for_search_waiting_for_the_wal_gets_room_with_nothing_else_committing`, after the demo-load audit's P2 and the budgets' `9726312`. The stall comes on a path this change adds, and no test covers it.
- **Where:** the checkpointer's `Ok((true, _, _)) => break` (`store/mod.rs:1645`), with the writer's restart in `bound_wal` and `restart_wal` (`:1435-1475`).
- **What happens:**
  1. Background work waits for the WAL (`wait_for_room`, or an index step's `hold`).
  2. An inbox group or a live write commits, and `committed()` kicks the checkpointer. Then `bound_wal` tries a RESTART: past WAL_SOFT whenever background work waits, since `7d7980e`.
  3. SQLite holds the checkpoint lock through the restart, including the up to RESTART_READERS (20 ms) it waits for a reader still on the WAL's frames, then answers busy.
  4. The checkpointer, woken by that commit, tries its PASSIVE pass meanwhile. SQLite refuses it the lock without waiting, and the new `break` sends it back to `recv_timeout(IDLE)`.
  5. The reader lets go, and nothing commits. Background work polls `room()` every millisecond, which stays false while more than WAL_NEAR (64) frames wait. The writer copies frames itself only at 64 or fewer, and nothing kicks the checkpointer.
  6. The stall ends at the next commit, or after IDLE (10 s), when the checkpointer empties the WAL.
  - Before the branch, the checkpointer kept looking every HOLD_POLL behind a reader and copied the rest as soon as the reader let go. The writer only took the lock for its own copy of the last 64 frames, which tries again at every poll.
- **Measured** with my probe (`audit_background_after_a_restart_a_reader_held_up`), 8 trials each, at a load of 48 to 57. In each trial, history waits for the WAL, a reader holds a snapshot, eight live writes of ten messages go 60 ms apart behind it, then nothing, and the reader lets go:

  | Build | Frames behind the reader | Background still waiting after the reader let go |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | The head | 171 to 366 | 9.96 to 9.99 s in 7 trials; 21 ms in 1 |
  | The base, `2541c9e` | 171 to 420 | 14 to 27 ms in all 8 |
  | The head, kicking the checkpointer after a restart that didn't happen | | 14 to 16 ms in all 8 |
  | The head, the checkpointer trying a refused pass again while background work waits | | 14 to 32 ms in all 8 |

  With a single live write behind the reader, 64 frames or fewer were left, and both builds resumed within 31 ms, since the writer's own copy covers that.
- **When it can happen:** a reader holds the WAL's frames longer than 20 ms during a restart while background work waits, and that commit is the last before a quiet spell. Two of jawd's reads can take that long:
  - a search (its budget is 50 ms over a million messages);
  - `jawd status`, whose six `count(*)`s each run on a fresh connection (`state.rs:191-196`). On the million-message account, `count(*) FROM messages` took 8 to 10 ms on a fresh connection, with outliers of 48 to 50 ms at a load of 56, and 527 ms cold.

  In my four first runs on the head, with a chat opened every OPEN_EVERY, it didn't happen: background work's waits reached 334 ms at p99 at most. So it's rare, but each time it costs history or indexing 10 s.
- **Fix:** either of these, both measured above:
  - at the end of `bound_wal`, kick the checkpointer when the try was busy (`let _ = self.kick.try_send(());`), the narrower of the two;
  - or, in `checkpoint()`, on a refused pass while `wal.held`, sleep HOLD_POLL and try again rather than break.

  Add the probe as a test, asserting background work resumes well short of IDLE once the reader lets go. It fails 7 of 8 as the branch stands, and passes 8 of 8 with either fix. It's `audit.rs` in `~/Library/Application Support/Cursor/AgentStores/cursor_agent_stores/1ed70e92-18c6-4a50-9de6-c3c498461792/files/wal-bound-audit/`, a child module of `store`, with the hooks it needs in `probe-hooks.patch` beside it.

## What I checked, and how it came out

**Durability**
- **Inbox commits stay FULL with `fullfsync`.** `store_inbox` calls `synced(true)` (`store/mod.rs:1201`). `commits_synced` reads both settings back and refuses an unsynced commit (`:129-133`, `:1256-1259`). The branch doesn't touch either.
- **`checkpoint_fullfsync` is on every connection.** `connect()` sets it for every role and reads it back (`:373`), and `every_connection_to_jaw_db_is_as_asked_and_its_checkpoints_reach_the_disk` passes. Every sync in my traces was FULL. The writer's RESTART runs at NORMAL or FULL, so its checkpoint syncs come from `checkpoint_fullfsync`: `walSyncFlags`, `sqlite3.c:63251-63256`, and `CKPT_SYNC_FLAGS`, `:59491`.
- **No ack before its synced commit.** `bound_wal` runs after the commit, the acks and the shared writes' answers (`:1155`) and after a live write's answer (`:1419`). The trace found no ack or durable answer without a FULL WAL sync of its commit before it.
- **A restart or truncate can't lose a committed transaction or show a torn state.** The writer's restart is SQLite's own RESTART checkpoint:
  1. it copies the WAL to its end;
  2. it syncs `jaw.db` FULL once the copy reaches the end (`sqlite3.c:69764-69770`);
  3. it waits up to RESTART_READERS for readers on read marks 1 and up;
  4. only the next write starts the WAL over. A reader that starts after a full copy reads `jaw.db` alone, and a reader still on the frames makes the restart answer busy, with nothing overwritten.

  jawd adds no file operation of its own. In the traces, every one of 86 restarts and 6 truncates came after a FULL sync covering every write to `jaw.db`, and every new header was synced before its frames.
- **The flush reaches the disk and drops no locks.**
  - `SQLITE_FCNTL_FILE_POINTER` hands over the main database's file, and my shim saw every flush as a sync of `jaw.db`: 156 and 146 in two runs.
  - `SQLITE_SYNC_FULL` makes the unix VFS call `F_FULLFSYNC` (`sqlite3.c:43935`, `:44103`, `:44117`, `:44004`).
  - Nothing is opened or closed, so no POSIX lock can go. The builder's test fails when the flush goes through a file of its own, the previous builder's version (the builder's mutant).
  - The sync runs on the checkpointer's own connection, between statements, on its own thread.
- **Kills.** 42 runs of a store with every kind of write, each in a process of its own, killed with SIGKILL:
  - before and after the writer's restart, including one a reader held;
  - at a new WAL header, and at the first frame after it;
  - mid-copy into `jaw.db` by the writer and by the checkpointer;
  - at the checkpointer's sync of `jaw.db`;
  - before and after a pass, and before and after the flush;
  - at the idle truncate;
  - and from outside at set times.

  After recovery, every acked row (156 to 23,621 a run) and every durable answer (3 to 726) was in `jaw.db`. There were no duplicates, `quick_check` was ok, and there were no foreign-key violations. A kill doesn't drop the OS's cache, so it tests jawd's order of commit and ack and SQLite's recovery; the trace is what tests the order a power cut needs.
