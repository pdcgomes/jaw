### Findings in main's store, found while measuring sending

**W1. The writer always takes the inbox first, so a steady stream of incoming rows holds back every live write, including `send.text`, `read`, `draft.set` and the live conversion of incoming messages (unpredictable performance; breaks a budget).**

How it happens:
- `Writer::run` picks the inbox before live writes with `select_biased!` (`store/mod.rs:623`).
- `commit_inbox` groups whatever inbox rows are waiting into one synced commit (`:791`).
- While rows arrive faster than one row every flush (about one every 5 ms), the inbox is ready again at every turn, so a live write waits until the stream stops.
- The converter turns inbox rows into messages in the live lane (`sync/drain.rs:342`). So incoming messages also don't reach interfaces until the stream ends, against the 5 ms budget for "an incoming message on its way to interfaces, once committed".

Measured, release build: a scratch test fed the inbox at a steady rate from a thread for 4 s while timing 60 durable live writes 50 ms apart.

| Inbox rate | Live write p50 | Live write max |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 a second | 7.4 ms | 14.6 ms |
| 100 a second | 7.8 ms | 16.4 ms |
| 300 a second | 7.6 ms | 1.64 s |
| 500 a second | 7.4 ms | 3.51 s |

The 500-a-second write waited until the stream ended. In an earlier run whose stream didn't end, the live write never ran. A catch-up after reconnecting delivers bursts like this.

*Fix (the writer's, on main):* let a waiting live write take each inbox group's synced commit with it, which costs nothing extra. Or take at most one live write between two inbox groups. Then show durable live writes within budget while the inbox drains at 500 a second.

This isn't sending's code, and nothing is lost: rows wait on disk and acks still follow their commit. But it is what makes `send.text` slow when it would show most, and incoming messages late, so it should be fixed before the first version is used day to day.

**W2. The checkpointer isn't woken when background work starts waiting for the WAL, so a background transaction can wait the full 10 s idle timeout (unpredictable performance; background only).**

How it happens:
- A commit wakes the checkpointer. If background work isn't waiting yet and fewer than 1,024 frames are uncopied, it skips the pass (`store/mod.rs:1167`).
- The writer's next background transaction then finds the WAL past its soft limit and waits (`wait_for_room`, `:890`). Nothing wakes the checkpointer again until its 10 s idle timeout (`:1149`), which empties the WAL.
- Live writes are still served meanwhile (`:898`), so only background work is held.

Measured on sending's step tests, with 102,300 receipts written through the writer first:
- Two runs each had one regroup step and one letting-go step of 10.03 s.
- 21 to 23 of 909 letting-go steps took 3 ms or more, at p99 6.6 to 8.1 ms.

With one line added in a scratch copy, `self.kick.try_send(())` after `held` is set in `wait_for_room`:
- the 10 s waits became one 21.5 ms restart of the WAL;
- letting-go steps at 3 ms or more fell to 1, at p99 1.5 ms.

*Fix (durability's, on main):* that line, or a checkpointer that runs passes while `held` without waiting for a kick. A test: background work past the soft limit with no other commits gets room within FINISH_AFTER plus one pass.

Most of N11's loosened allowance (one step in thirty over 3 ms, none over 15 ms) comes from W2, so tighten it to about one in a hundred once W2 is fixed.

### I5: the read position on sync's columns

The builder folded the plan's `reads` table into sync's `chats` columns:
- `read_n` beside `read_at`, with a NULL `read_n` meaning a time, `(T, i64::MAX)` (`THROUGH`, `write.rs:585`);
- `told_at` and `told_n`, jaw's own, where NULL means WhatsApp knows as much as jaw (`told_now`, `:656`).

That keeps one position, which is what the plan was for. Against each amendment:

1. **WhatsApp's position replaces a guess until it's known, and reading here then owes what the phone still showed unread.** `set_read` takes WhatsApp's position while `read_known = 0` or the phone's counts stand, even an earlier one, and sets "told" to NULL (`:795`). Once known, an earlier position only moves "told" on. `told_now` puts an implicit "told" before the phone's newest `phone_unread` messages from others. Tested in `whatsapps_position_replaces_a_guess…` and `upgraded_read_positions_owe…`: a chat with phone counts 2 owes exactly its newest two once read here.
2. **A time is `(T, i64::MAX)`.** It's stored as a NULL `read_n`, so no row is rewritten. A history message at exactly T counts as read (tested).
3. **The phone's counts live with the position.** They stay on `chats` beside it, written only through `write_read` and in the same statement as the position, which is what the amendment's fallback asked for.
4. **Your own messages.** One sent from jaw (`Message::here`) moves the position but freezes "told" first, so what it reads is owed. One from your other devices moves both. Tested in `a_message_sent_here_reads_the_chat_and_owes_its_receipts_but_one_from_your_phone_doesnt`.
5. **The migration's kind.** I agree with migration 7 as a schema step. My amendment asked for a data step only because it would have dropped `read_at` and `read_known`, rewriting `chats`. Now nothing is dropped or rewritten:
   - three nullable columns are added; the `CHECK` on `told_n` reads each chat once, and chats are few next to messages;
   - the outbox rebuild copies a table nothing on main ever wrote.
   
   On the owner's copy, the whole upgrade from version 1 to 8 served in 5.5 s, the backup and sync's data steps included.
6. **Merges.**
   - `start_merge` refuses anything but two one-to-one chats (`:1258`).
   - `merge_reads` runs at the start and again just before the gone chat is deleted (`:855`). The later position wins, "told" goes back to just before the gone chat's oldest message WhatsApp hasn't heard of, and nothing ever earlier.
   - `folded` moves a copy's send and key to the kept copy, or drops the send if the kept copy reached the server (`outbox.rs:730`).
   - The receipts lane waits while a merge runs (`Owing::Merging`). `Told` lands only if "told" is still where the lot began (`TOLD`, `outbox.rs:126`).
   
   All are tested, the group refusal apart (H12).

I traced the merge's "just before", `(at, n - 1)`, because a message of the kept chat could sit at exactly `n - 1`. It can't be skipped: "told" is the smaller of the kept chat's own and that point, so anything of the kept chat above its own "told" stays owed. The builder's fix for a gone chat never read here is right: "told" goes back only as far as the gone chat's oldest untold message, not to the start of the kept chat's history.

Sync's recount pass is honoured as well. A merging chat counts from the later of its own and the kept chat's `(at, n)`, and its own position and "told" stay as the merge found them (`settle_each`, `:720`). That's tested by a read during a merge, which owes g1 and k2 and clears "told" only once `Told` lands.

### Is the demo-engine `send.text` figure a real-world concern?

Not in itself.
- The bench sends 1,000 messages back to back, and the demo engine answers each with two receipts at once. Each receipt is its own synced inbox commit, and the inbox goes first, so each `send.text` waits about three flushes: 19.0 to 20.0 ms p50 in my three runs.
- With the receipts held back, `send.text` took 6.5 to 7.2 ms p50 (13 to 14 ms p99), against 5.06 to 5.09 ms p50 (9.7 to 10.1 ms p99) for a bare synced commit. What sending adds is the engine's sent replies sharing the writer. The builder's no-engine figure (5.1 to 5.8 ms) agrees.
- In real use, receipts come hundreds of milliseconds later and at most one inbox flush is ahead of a send.

The real-world concern is W1. In a steady stream of incoming messages, a send waits for the stream to end, not three flushes.

### The `settled` queue

The questions were whether letting go of recipients later could leave a wrong tick, a wrong info sheet, or rows that never go, and whether the receipt that completes a message is now light. On each:

- **No wrong tick.**
  - A message goes into `settled` only once its status is at its end for its kind: read, or played for voice, audio, video and video notes (`settled`, `:363`).
  - While its rows are going, a later receipt or regroup still sees some recipients and runs `group_reached` on them. It can only find a laggard or nobody behind.
  - What it finds goes through `advanced`, which never moves a status back, and an end status has nowhere further to go. A text message at read, with half its rows gone, gets a late delivered receipt: `group_reached` says "sent" at most, and `advanced("read", "sent")` is read.
- **No wrong info sheet.**
  - Nothing outside sending reads `recipients`; I searched every crate. No info sheet exists yet.
  - M2's `message.info` must build its list from `receipts`, which keeps every recipient's times, and not from `recipients`, which never lasted past read even before this commit (H5). That goes on M2's list as N10.
- **No rows that never go.**
  - `SETTLED` adds a row only when the message has recipients (`:151`).
  - `LetGo` deletes up to 128 of them in primary-key order with no sort (`:153`, plan pinned). It removes the `settled` row once a step deletes fewer than 128, so an exact multiple of 128 takes one more, empty, step (`:255`).
  - Recipients are written only by the enqueue, once per message, so none can come back after a message is settled.
  - Deleting the message cascades to both tables.
  - The queue is bounded by the messages with recipients left, drained by the worker, and counted in `jawd status`.
  - Measured: after every step had run, `recipients` and `settled` were both empty at 64, 256 and 1,024 members.
- **The completing receipt is light.**
  - At 1,024 members it now takes 0.64 to 0.74 ms, over four runs. That's the walk that proves nobody else is behind, plus one insert into `settled`.
  - The other receipts on that message took 0.06 to 0.11 ms at p50 and 0.15 to 0.30 ms at p99.
  - Deleting 1,024 rows and their index entries has moved into the background: 9 steps of 0.5 to 0.9 ms (p50), 1.0 to 1.9 ms (p99).

**One step over 3 ms in a hundred.** In the case the builder's test covers, where the group's members stay, the steps' own work is well inside the budget. Regrouping had a p99 of 0.69 to 0.97 ms and never reached 3 ms in five runs. Letting go had a p99 of 1.0 to 1.9 ms, with 0 to 2 steps of 909 at 3 ms or more (worst 12.4 ms).

Those outliers come from outside the step. The writer has SQLite's own checkpoints off (`store/mod.rs:325`), so a checkpoint is never paid inside a step's commit, but a checkpoint on another connection or other processes' I/O can stall a commit; the load average was 19 to 26. A wall-clock test can't tell those apart, so allowing about 1% (`:1231`) is fair for it. The bound that matters is the one built into the step, and that is N9.

### Findings in the third look

#### Unpredictable performance (breaks the budget in one case)

**N9. A regroup step is sized by the group's members now, but its walk costs as many rows as the message had recipients.**

How it happens:
- `most = 1024 / members` (`:521`), with `members` the group's current size (`GROUP_SIZE`, `:211`).
- The laggard walks start from the message's recipients, then check each against `group_members`. That's the pinned plan, `SEARCH s USING PRIMARY KEY (message=?)` first, so a walk costs about 1.5 µs for each recipient.
- After a mass departure, `most` grows while each walk still covers every recipient, and only the 2 ms backstop (`REGROUP_FOR`, `:33`) ends a step. A step can therefore take 2 ms plus one more walk of about 1.5 ms.
- Worse, most of those steps look at messages already read, which can't move. Even so, `group_reached` checks for "played" first, and walks past every recipient who has left before it finds one who hasn't played.

Measured, release build: a 1,024-member group, 100 messages read by members 1,001 to 1,024, and members 1 to 1,000 leaving in one change (2.1 to 2.5 ms, live). Over two runs:
- regrouping took about 25,050 steps of 2.1 to 2.5 ms each, roughly a minute of writer time;
- 109 and 627 of them (0.4% and 2.5%) took 3 ms or more;
- the worst took 49.5 ms and 11.3 ms.

The worker's letting go would shorten that minute in practice, but not the length of a step.

*Fix (both parts tried in a scratch copy):*
- Size the step by the larger of the group's members and the next message's recipients.
- Skip, without calling `group_reached`, a message already at its end for its kind (read for text, played for voice), and don't count it against the step.

With both, the same departure ran at 1.2 to 1.5 ms a step, with 11 and 34 of 101,000 steps at 3 ms or more (environmental). The normal case was unchanged: regrouping p99 0.97 ms, letting go p99 1.9 ms. Skipping messages at their end also spares the live path, where a late receipt for a read text message does the same walk. *Test:* the departure above in the release test, within its tolerance.

### The re-audit in short

The fixes do what the first audit asked, and each has a test:
- `send.text` takes a key, and a second request with it answers with the same message, including two requests at once.
- A committed send always answers a success and is queued at once.
- Any receipt clears the outbox row.
- A refused resend of a message that may have gone waits a day as `unconfirmed` instead of showing `failed`.
- A member leaving only notes who left. The group ticks are looked at again for that group, all its messages, in background steps.
- A chat waiting to try again gives up its place in the send lane.
- Read receipts go oldest first, 1,000 at a time, and "told" never passes the last message named.
- The crash tests kill a child jawd, so only one writer is ever alive.

I ran the sending tests 15 times, and the two-writer collision didn't come back.

Four things stand between this branch and landing:
1. **N1 (inconsistency).** A message whose send is settled by a receipt never gets its disappearing-message timer.
2. **N2 (breaks a budget).** In a 1,024-member group, one background step of regrouping takes 22 to 29 ms, against the 3 ms budget. A live message arriving meanwhile waits that long.
3. **I5.** The one read position, which waits for the rebase. The builder's plan is right in shape and needs the six amendments below.

### Findings in the re-audit

#### Inconsistency

**N1. A message whose send is settled by a receipt never gets its disappearing-message timer.**

How it happens:
- `sent` sets `expires_at` when the engine's reply arrives (`store/write/outbox.rs:312`, `EXPIRES` at `:97`).
- But a receipt now clears the outbox row (`received`, `:326`, called from `store/write.rs:1157`). The engine's reply then finds no row and returns before `EXPIRES` runs.
- `send_text` writes the message with `expires_at = NULL`, so nothing else sets it.

That path is taken whenever a receipt beats the reply, and after a crash between the server's ack and the commit of the reply, which is exactly the case I2 was about. Measured in a scratch test, in a chat with a 24-hour timer:
- the message settled by a delivered receipt kept `expires_at = NULL`;
- the message settled by the reply got its time.

Nothing expires rows yet, so nothing shows it today. But the row is wrong now, and once expiry lands that message stays on this Mac after it has gone from every phone.

*Fix:* in `received`, when the `DELETE` removed a row, run `EXPIRES` with the same `now`. Better still, move `EXPIRES` into one helper that both `sent` and `received` call, so any path that settles a send starts the timer. *Test:* in a disappearing chat, a receipt before the reply leaves `expires_at` set.

#### Unpredictable performance (breaks a budget)

**N2. A regroup step looks at 16 messages whatever the group's size: 22 to 29 ms in one transaction at 1,024 members.**

Each step calls `group_reached` for up to `REGROUP_AT_ONCE = 16` messages (`store/write/outbox.rs:26`, `regroup` at `:459`). When the member who left was the last one holding a message up, the "read" check has to walk every other recipient to find nobody behind. That walk is the expensive case, and it's the one regrouping exists for.

The store ends a background transaction only between steps, so one step is one transaction however long it takes. Measured: 100 messages read by everyone but member 1, then member 1 leaves, each step timed alone, release build.

| Group size | Each of the 7 steps |
| --- | --- |
| 64 | 1.7 to 2.1 ms |
| 256 | 6.0 to 7.5 ms |
| 1,024 | 22 to 29 ms |

That's about 0.1, 0.4 and 1.6 ms a message. The background budget is 3 ms, and a live message that arrives meanwhile waits for the step to finish, against its 5 ms p99. The worker sends 64 members × 4 steps at once (`send/regroup.rs:15-16`). After a full member refresh drops several stale members from big groups, that is a run of such transactions.

*Fix:* bound a step by its cost, not a fixed count. Either stop once about 2 ms have passed, after at least one message, saving `after` as the last message looked at; or take `max(1, 2048 / recipients)` messages. Both stay idempotent and resumable as `after` already makes them. *Test or bench:* the scenario above at 1,024 members, with no step over 3 ms.

#### Hygiene (follow-ups; none blocks)

**N3. The I3 fix made `sending_goes_through_the_socket_with_the_pending_row_in_its_answer` flaky.** `send_text` now queues the message before reading its row back for the answer (`send/outbox.rs:153-156`). With the demo engine's instant reply, the answer sometimes carries `sent`. This happened in 1 of my 15 runs:
- `left: "sent"`, `right: "pending"` at `send/tests.rs:1053`.

No data is wrong. The row carries its `v`, and windows keep the newest. But the README promises `pending`, and a flaky test in the gate hides real failures. *Fix:* build the answer first and queue straight after (`let sent = answer(..).await; queue(..); Ok(sent)`). `answer` never fails, so I3's guarantee holds.

**N4. A write of a send's outcome that fails is logged and dropped** (`send/outbox.rs:275-279`). The row is still on disk, so nothing is lost. But:
- If `Failed { next_at }` doesn't commit, `attempts` stays 0, so a later refusal of a message that did reach the engine fails it instead of leaving it `unconfirmed`.
- If `Unconfirmed` doesn't commit, the job ends with the row still `queued` and not in the queue until the next start. The day's timer, started anyway, then marks it `failed` from `queued`. `ATTEMPTED` has no guard on `state`.

*Fix:* write outcomes through `settle`'s retry loop, as `Sent` already is. Start the timer only once `Unconfirmed` has committed. Guard the timer's `Failed` with `state = 'unconfirmed'`.

**N5. "May have gone" also covers rows that never reached the engine.** `may_have_gone = resumed || row.attempts > 0` (`:246`). A message queued while offline, still there after a restart, and then refused (a number not on WhatsApp) waits a day as `unconfirmed` before showing `failed`. That's safe, but slow to tell the user. *Follow-up:* note on the row when it's first handed to the engine, and count only those rows.

**N6. `regroup` and `send_keys` are queues, and neither is in `jawd status`.** Both are bounded. `regroup` has one row per member still to look at. `send_keys` holds a day of keys, with 64 pruned per enqueue. "Every file and queue is bounded and reported" asks for their counts too.

**N7. jaw doesn't send a key yet.**
- `send_text` in `crates/jaw/src/wire.rs:283` sends no `key`.
- On main, `081a209` makes jaw wait up to 8 s, after jawd is back, for a row of yours with the same text before it puts the text back. That covers the crash case well.
- It's still a guess that can miss: say the chat isn't open and a reply arrives first. Then the text comes back and Enter sends a second message.

*Follow-up (jaw, after the rebase):* make one key per composed message. When the answer is lost, ask `send.text` again with that key once jawd is back, instead of matching text. jawd's half (I1) is done and tested.

**N8. The unconfirmed day isn't tested end to end.** Neither failing once the day has passed nor `resume` restarting the timer with the time left has a test. `UNCONFIRMED_FOR` is a constant, so tests can't shorten it. Put it in `Timing` and test both.

### The read-position merge plan (I5)

The plan is judged on the basis that sync keeps `chats.read_at` and `read_known` until this merges, and doesn't add a second table.

**It is right in its shape:**
- one table, `reads`, of jaw's own class;
- a position as `(at, n)`;
- "told" at or behind it, moving with the position when WhatsApp is the source;
- one `advance_read` that calls sync's derivation in the same transaction, so `UNREAD_AFTER` and `SET_UNREAD` go;
- a chat merge that keeps the later position and the earlier "told", before the gone chat is deleted;
- plans in `plans.txt`.

I checked it against sync-v1 at `2395b12`:
- `settle` derives unread and mentions from `read_at` and `mentions_me`, minus `system` and `call` (`write.rs:557-610`).
- `set_read` takes positions from app state, read-self receipts and history (`write.rs:616-640`).
- Migration 6 starts every chat at its newest message with the phone's counts standing in (`schema.rs:495`).
- `merge_step` moves messages, keeping their `n`, and deletes the gone chat last (`write.rs:1036-1072`).

**It needs six amendments.** The first three would otherwise make the data wrong.

1. **Keep sync's "replaceable until known" rule.** sync's `set_read` takes WhatsApp's position as said, even an earlier one, while `read_known = 0` or the phone's counts stand in (`WHERE read_known = 0 OR phone_unread IS NOT NULL OR …`). Migration 6 depends on it. It starts every chat at its newest message with `phone_unread` as a stand-in, and the first `markChatAsRead` from app state must be able to put the position where the phone really has it.

   Step 3's "only forward" would lock in the migration's guess. Step 2 then makes "told" equal that guess, which is the newest message. When you read those messages in jaw, no receipt goes for the ones the phone still shows unread, so their senders never see them read, and the position never becomes exact.

   The rule should be: while `known = 0` or the phone's counts stand, a position from WhatsApp replaces the row's position, and "told" goes with it. After that, the position only moves forward, and jaw's own `read` always sets `known = 1`. Likewise, while the phone's counts stand, "told" sits before the newest `phone_unread` messages from others (sending's `told_before`), not at the position. The migration sets it that way, so reading them in jaw sends their receipts. Step 5's fallback only covers a chat's first read, and after the migration every chat with messages already has a row.

2. **A position given as a time is `(T, i64::MAX)`, not "the newest message up to T".** sync's `read_at`, app state's timestamp and history's position are all times, and some messages up to T are still to come from history. With the newest message's `n`, a history message at exactly T arrives with a larger `n` and counts as unread. A chat with no message up to T has no position at all. `(T, i64::MAX)` reads "everything up to T", compares correctly with `(at, n)` everywhere, satisfies the `CHECK`, and spares the migration a search per chat. A position from a message stays `(at, n)`.

3. **Keep the phone's counts with the position.** They are counts as of the position: sync drops them when you write, and replaces them on each `set_read`. Split across two tables, they have to be changed together in every transaction that moves the position. Move `phone_unread` and `phone_mentions` into `reads`. A jaw-own row can carry fetched columns; it is only handled more carefully for it. If they must stay on `chats`, make `advance_read` the only writer of both, and say so where they're declared.

4. **Say which of your own messages move "told".** One sent from jaw moves the position but not "told". The messages before it are then owed receipts, as the phone sends them when you reply. One that arrives from another of your devices moves both, since that device has told WhatsApp. Step 3 says only "when WhatsApp is the source", which leaves your own messages open.

5. **The migration is a data step, and the plan test moves with it.**
   - Dropping `read_at` and `read_known` rewrites `chats`, whose size isn't fixed, so declare it a data step and let durability's backup cover it. Rows with `read_at IS NULL` get no `reads` row, which is the same as reading from the start. `known` is copied as it is, and "told" equals the position.
   - Fold it into sending's migrations, numbered after sync's 6 at merge, rather than adding a fourth.
   - `plans.rs`'s `STAND_INS` names the column `read_at`. That, and every statement in `plans.txt` that reads `chats.read_at`, changes with it.
   - sync's count becomes `(at, n) > (?, ?)` with its `kind NOT IN ('system', 'call')` kept. `messages_by_time` is `(chat, at)`, and the rowid makes it exact at equal times, as I measured in the first audit. Pin the plan.
   - *Test:* upgrade from fixtures with known, unknown, `NULL` and phone-count rows.

6. **Merging chats: what moves and what can't.**
   - sync merges a person's two direct chats, so `recipients` and `regroup` (groups only) never take part. Assert that, rather than moving them.
   - `outbox` and `send_keys` rows must move to the kept message before a duplicate is folded away, or the cascade drops a pending send.
   - Merge `reads` in the last `merge_step`, just before `DELETE FROM chats`. The position is the later of the two, "told" the earlier, `known` the larger, and the phone's counts come from the row whose position wins. Until then, writes for the gone chat go to the kept one, and `(at, n)` positions don't depend on which chat a message is in.
   - Receipts owed for messages that came under the gone JID will go to the kept chat's JID. Check on the spare number that WhatsApp takes that for LID-addressed messages.

**Also keep:** WhatsApp's read clears `marked_unread`, as sync's `set_read` does. jaw's `read` doesn't (H10). Moving the position alone makes no new version of the chat, as in sync's `settle`.

With these amendments, I agree with the plan. If the rebase follows it, the next audit is a check of that code and its tests against this list, not another design round.

### Summary

The core is sound:
- `send.text` commits the message row and its outbox row in one transaction through `apply_durable` before it answers. The bench's 6 ms answers are the cost of `F_FULLFSYNC`.
- The ID is made once and kept in the message row, so every resend, on backoff, after a restart or on `send.retry`, carries the same ID.
- The engine's `Sent` reply only removes the outbox row. If that commit is lost, the next start sends again with the same ID, never with a new one.
- Status only moves forward, apart from `send.retry`, which takes failed back to pending. Receipts that arrive before `Sent` are kept.
- The read position is synced before any receipt goes. "Told up to here" is advanced only after the engine has sent the receipts, and a `CHECK` keeps it at or behind the position.
- The outbox is bounded at 1,000 rows, failed ones included, and is counted in `jawd status`.
- Every new statement's plan is pinned, and all four sending steps are idempotent. That matters because the durability branch's writer retries a transaction that failed for a passing reason.

I checked a crash at each point the brief names:

| Where it stops | What happens after a restart |
| --- | --- |
| Before the commit | Nothing was kept, and the interface hears an error. |
| Between the commit and the engine's send | The outbox row is queued again and sent with its ID. |
| Between the engine's send and its reply | Sent again with the same ID. |
| Between the reply and the commit that keeps it | Sent again with the same ID. |

Each has a test, except that the third and fourth rely on how the demo engine models WhatsApp (see I2).

It can't land as it is, for these reasons, detailed under Findings:

- An answer can say "failed" for a message that is committed and will be sent.
- A message that WhatsApp already has can be shown as failed, and its outbox row is left behind.
- A group's ticks can stay wrong for good after a member leaves.
- One member change took 2.9 s in a live transaction.
- Sixteen chats that keep failing can stop every other chat from sending.
- Mentions are adjusted by hand, which the design forbids.

### Findings

#### Data loss

**L1. On this branch alone, a recipient who asks for a message again after jawd restarts never gets it.** When a recipient's device can't decrypt a message, it sends a retry receipt. whatsmeow answers from a 256-entry in-memory cache, or from its retry store if `UseRetryMessageStore` is on (`whatsmeow/retry.go:100-137`). This branch doesn't set it (`engine/internal/wa/engine.go:96-97`), and `session.db` runs at `synchronous=NORMAL` (`engine.go:83`). So after an engine restart, or a power cut that rolls `session.db` back, that recipient shows "waiting for this message" for good. Sync-v1's audit asked for this "before sending lands" (its H4).
*Fix:* the durability branch already sets `client.UseRetryMessageStore = true` and runs `session.db` at `FULL` with `_txlock=immediate` (`jaw-durable/engine/internal/wa/engine.go:95`, `session.go:25`). Keep both at the rebase. This finding closes when the branch is rebased onto them.

#### Inconsistency

**I1. A dropped `send.text` answer turns into a second message.** The client reports "the connection to jawd dropped before it answered, so it may not have been done" (`crates/jaw-client/src/client.rs:89-102`). jaw then puts the text back in an empty box (`crates/jaw/src/view.rs:412-416`), and Enter sends it again with a new ID. If jawd committed before the drop, which is exactly the crash point between the commit and the answer, both messages are sent, the first one from the outbox at the next start. `send.text` is the one write in the range that isn't idempotent: nothing lets a second request find the first.
*Fix:* give `send.text` an idempotency key from the interface, kept `UNIQUE` in the outbox (or in a small `sends` table that outlives the outbox row for a day or so). A repeated key answers with the existing message and its row. Simpler still: let the interface make the ID itself (`3EB0` and 18 hex digits) and send it. A second `send.text` with an ID jawd already has then answers with that message, and `ENQUEUE`'s `ON CONFLICT` already makes the write idempotent. jaw should also stop restoring the text when a pending row with that key is already in the window. Test: answer lost after the commit, the request made again, one message and one send.

**I2. The same ID on resend: safe from duplicates, but a refused resend shows the message as failed and keeps its outbox row.**

What's known:
- jawd never makes a second ID for a message. Retries, `send.retry` and restarts all reuse the message row's ID (`send/outbox.rs:172`).
- whatsmeow itself sends a message again with the same ID when a recipient asks (`handleRetryReceipt`).
- Recipients' clients keep messages by chat, sender and ID, so a second delivery of the same ID is the same message to them. WhatsApp's retry mechanism relies on this.

What isn't known, as the builder found: what the server's ack says to a second `<message id=X>` once the first was accepted. It could be success, or an error code. Since `bad56c2`, a 4xx code makes the send a refusal (`engine/internal/wa/send.go:100-106`, `transient`), and the outbox's `failed()` marks the message `failed` (`store/write/outbox.rs:228-238`).

So after a crash between the engine's send and the commit of its reply, a resend the server answers with, say, 409 shows a delivered message as failed. Its outbox row stays `failed` and counts against the 1,000 forever. A later delivered or read receipt moves the status on (failed ranks below sent), but nothing removes the row. I reproduced the leftover row in a scratch test: a send failed by refusal, then a `delivered` receipt, left status `delivered` with outbox state `failed`. The same happens whenever a receipt comes in while a send is in flight and its reply is a failure. `send.retry` would then send the same ID again, which is still not a duplicate.

*Fix, safe whichever way the server answers:*
- (a) A receipt that takes a message to sent or further removes its outbox row, in the same transaction (`apply_amendment`). The deliver loop already re-reads the row before each attempt, so nothing is sent after that.
- (b) Treat a row as possibly sent once it has been handed to the engine. That is any row resumed after a restart, or with `attempts > 0`. When a possibly-sent message is refused with a non-transient code, don't mark it `failed`. Keep it pending, in an outbox state of its own such as `unconfirmed`, until a receipt settles it. After a bound (a day, say) mark it failed with an error that says it may have gone the first time.
- (c) Record what the server answers to a resend (a `jawd status` counter, with the code in the log). Then try it once with a scratch account: kill the engine after the server's ack and before its reply, restart, and look. That answers the question for good.
- (d) Soften the protocol README's claim that "WhatsApp and the recipients' phones take [it] for the same message" to what has been verified.

Tests: a receipt removes the outbox row; a refusal of a resumed row leaves it unconfirmed, not failed.

**I3. `send.text` can answer with an error for a message it has durably committed, and then not send it until the next start.** After the synced commit, `send_text` reads the row and the outbox `n` on the read pool (`send/outbox.rs:119-125`). If that read fails, the interface gets "couldn't read jaw.db" for a message that is pending and will go at the next start, which leads to the duplicate in I1. `queue` is also never called, so the message waits for a restart.
*Fix:* never answer an error once the commit has succeeded. Return the outbox `n` from the write itself (`Applied`, or a returned value from the `Enqueue` step), build the answer's row from the message already in memory if the read fails, and queue the message in every case.

**I4. A group's ticks stay behind for good when the last member who hadn't read leaves, if they're in another group with you.** `members_left` (`store/write/outbox.rs:337-357`) takes the member's newest 100 recipient rows across every group (`SENT_TO_MEMBER`, with no filter on the chat), and only then keeps those in the group they left (`MINE_IN`). Measured: in a 1,024-member group, 100 messages read by everyone but member 1, with member 1 also in another group with 150 newer messages from you. After member 1 leaves, **0 of the 100** become `read`. Nothing looks at them again, so the derived status stays wrong. The cap of 100 per member has the same effect on older messages even within one group.
*Fix:* select the member's messages in that chat only. A `chat` column in `recipients`, keyed `(chat, member, message)`, makes that a range search. Look at all of them, not 100, in background batches (see P1). Test: the scenario above ends with all 100 read.

**I5. Unread is recounted here and adjusted by hand elsewhere, and mentions are kept by hand (rule "Derived values are derived").** `read` recounts unread from the position (`UNREAD_AFTER`), but it sets `mentions = iif(unread = 0, 0, mentions)` (`SET_UNREAD`, `outbox.rs:63-64`). Reading half a chat leaves mentions counting messages already read. At `0ab87c5`, sync still adjusts `unread` by hand as messages arrive, so the two methods meet in one column. Sync-v1's rework (`d2166e5`) derives both unread and mentions (`mentions_me`) from a read position.
*Fix (at the rebase):* `read` moves the one read position and calls sync's derivation. `UNREAD_AFTER` and `SET_UNREAD` go, and there is one cap constant. See "What the merged read position must keep".

#### Unpredictable performance

**P1. A member change re-derives group ticks inside the live transaction: 2.9 s measured.** `set_members` calls `members_left`, which for every member who left runs `group_reached` on up to 100 messages, each a walk over the recipients (`write.rs:1205-1213`, `outbox.rs:337-357`). Measured: 199 members leaving a 1,024-member group at once (100 messages) took **2,899 ms** in one live transaction. Nothing commits meanwhile, inbox acks included. A full member list from the refresh at start (`all: true`), with stale members dropped, takes this path.
*Fix:* `members_left` only queues the work: a small jaw-own table of (chat, member) to look at, filled in the member change's transaction. The background lane then works through it in transactions of at most 3 ms. Pin the new statements. Test: a member change's transaction stays within the live budget however many leave.

**P2. Since `bad56c2`, sixteen chats whose first message keeps failing in a way that might pass can stop every other chat from sending.** Each chat's queue holds one of `Serial`'s 16 permits for the whole of `deliver` (`work/serial.rs:57-69`). That includes its sleeps and its wait for the connection (`send/outbox.rs:154-213`), and there is now no time limit. One of whatsmeow's untyped errors that recurs, such as "failed to get devices" for one contact, is retried forever while connected. Sixteen such chats hold every permit, and nothing else is sent. Nothing is lost, since every message stays pending on disk, but sending stops.
*Fix:* give up the permit while waiting, by putting the job back with its `next_at` instead of sleeping inside it. After many attempts while connected (say a day's worth), fail a retryable error too. Show the oldest attempt and the attempt count in `jawd status`.

**P3. Writer time per send and receipt. Within budget for small groups; the large-group numbers are follow-ups.**
- `send.text`: 6.0 to 6.2 ms p50, 7.8 to 11.5 ms p99, almost all of it the flush.
- A send to a 1,024-member group writes 1,024 `recipients` rows before the flush: 2.0 ms p50 and 9.5 ms p99 at `NORMAL`.
- Each receipt in that group costs 0.35 ms p50, 4.8 ms p99 and 6.7 ms max, and 418 ms in total for one message's 1,024 delivered receipts.
- `read` on a 200,000-message chat where you wrote 9 of every 10 messages costs 3.3 ms before its flush. `UNREAD_AFTER` walks your own messages through table lookups.
- `draft.set` (every 1.5 s while typing) and `read` (every second while reading) each cost a full flush on the one writer. A live message queued behind one waits 6 to 10 ms, against the 5 ms live budget.

*Follow-ups:* release acks in groups after one synced commit (the design allows it), so drafts, reads and sends share a flush. Consider a partial index on others' messages (`messages (chat, at) WHERE mine = 0`) for unread and receipts, priced against the cost of writing it for every message. Keep `recipients` writes out of the synced commit if a group's size makes them long.

**P4. A slow sending call holds up its connection.** `sock.rs` awaits every sending method inline (`sock.rs:270-276`). `chat.start` waits for `IsOnWhatsApp`, up to whatsmeow's 75 s, and that connection's later requests, `send.text` included, wait behind it. *Follow-up:* answer sending calls the way finds are answered, when they're done.

#### Hygiene

**H1. `RECEIPTS_AT_ONCE` overstates "told".** `owed` names the newest 1,000 messages owed, and `Told` then moves "told" to the position. The older owed messages are marked told without a receipt (`outbox.rs:420-431`, `send/reads.rs:100-112`). It's never ahead of what's durable, but it is ahead of what WhatsApp heard. *Fix:* flush oldest first in batches of 1,000, moving "told" to each batch's newest message, or document it as deliberate. Either way, add a test for the bound. None exists, and every bound needs one.

**H2.** The 1,000-row outbox check reads the count before taking the enqueue lock (`send/outbox.rs:74-93`), so concurrent connections can each pass it. Count inside the `Enqueue` step's transaction instead, and quarantine nothing for it: refuse.

**H3.** `send_text` queues under the JID it was given, but `resume` and `retry` queue under the chat's resolved `id` (`send/outbox.rs:126` versus `:136`, `:256`). Two sends to one chat named by its number and its LID can then run in different queues and out of order. Queue under the resolved chat.

**H4.** `Sent` rewrites a message's `at` to the server's time but leaves `t`, so search orders it by the local time. It also sets the chat's `last_at` by hand (`SENT_AT`), even when the new time is older than another message's. Recompute the chat's last message the way sync's merge does, and give `t` a fresh value or document why it stays.

**H5.** `recipients` grows by one row per member for every group message you send: 20 messages to a 1,024-member group made 20,480 rows. Nothing removes them while the message lives. Prune a message's rows once it reaches its final status for its kind (read, or played for voice), and report the count in `jawd status`. "Every file and queue is bounded".

**H6.** `OWING` scans `reads` (one row per chat ever read), once at start. That's acceptable, and pinned. A partial index on chats still owed would make it a search.

**H7.** When disappearing messages expire rows, which nothing does yet, a message with an outbox row must not expire. Its timer counts from when it was sent, and since `bad56c2` a message can wait offline for longer than a 24-hour timer.

**H8. The crash tests run two writers on one file.** `lifetime` ends the runtime, but the first `Store`'s writer thread lives on. In 1 of 15 runs of the sending tests, a change was quarantined with "UNIQUE constraint failed: events.seq", and `a_message_the_server_had_before_a_restart_is_not_sent_again` failed. `jaw.lock`, which the design requires, isn't taken by any branch yet. Stop the first store's writer before reopening, or kill a child process. Land `jaw.lock`.

**H9.** Delete-for-me of a pending message removes its outbox row through the cascade, which cancels the send. One already handed to the engine goes anyway, and its `Sent` is then ignored, with the tombstone keeping it deleted. That's acceptable; say so in the protocol.

**H10.** Reading a chat clears `marked_unread` in jaw only (`SET_UNREAD`). Sync's rework fetches app state in full at every engine connect, which will set it back. Send the matching app-state patch when it lands, or keep `marked_unread` out of what reading changes until then.

### Migrations 6 and 7

- **Kind:** both are schema steps. Migration 6 rebuilds `outbox`, but nothing on `main` ever wrote to that table, so the copy costs the same however big the database is. Migration 7 only creates `recipients` and an index. At the rebase, each needs a line in `migrations.lock` with its kind. The numbers are placeholders: sync-v1's rework takes 5 (re-hashed, not merged yet) and 6 (a data step rebuilding `messages` and `people`), so these become 7 and 8.
- **Classes:** `outbox`, `reads` and `recipients` are all jaw's own, as the comments say. Their foreign keys cascade from `messages` and `chats`. That's right for a real deletion, but see the rebase list for merges and rebuilds.
- **Migration 6's rebuild** follows the twelve steps for a table nothing refers to:
  - a `STRICT` table with `AUTOINCREMENT`, so outbox keys the in-memory queue holds are never reused;
  - `CHECK` on `state` and `attempts`;
  - every row copied in `created_at` order;
  - a row that names no message fails the step instead of being dropped, with a test that the database is then as it was.

  The old `outbox_by_chat` index goes, rightly, since nothing reads by it any more. I couldn't count the old table's rows in the owner's `jaw.db` without opening its WAL, which I may not touch. The step fails rather than drops, so the worst case is a refusal to start, not a loss.
- **Missing:** the frozen-hash and fingerprint lines (they come with durability), and an upgrade test from a fixture database. Both are rebase items.

### What the merged read position must keep

Sync's rework keeps `read_at`, `read_known`, `phone_unread` and `phone_mentions` on `chats`, declared "fetched from WhatsApp". This branch keeps `reads (chat, at, n, told_at, told_n)` as jaw's own. The merged design should be one table of jaw's own with:

1. **A position as `(at, n)`, not a time alone.** Two messages in the same millisecond are otherwise ambiguous. I checked that SQLite's row-value range search over `messages_by_time` is exact at equal times: unread after (5, 2) and owed in ((5, 1), (5, 3)] both came out right.
2. **The position only moves forward, from every source:**
   - jaw's `read`, committed `FULL` before its reply;
   - read-self receipts from your other devices;
   - `markChatAsRead`'s message range, mapped to the newest message up to its last timestamp;
   - the migration's starting point.

   Sources other than jaw's own commit at `NORMAL`, since their raw is already in the inbox.
3. **"Told" stays at or behind the position (the `CHECK`).** It moves only after the engine confirms the receipts. It also moves with the position, without sending anything, when the position came from WhatsApp, so jaw doesn't send receipts for what the phone already read. A chat's first read starts "told" at the position WhatsApp is known to have (`read_known`), and falls back to `phone_unread` only when that isn't known. Today's `told_before` guesses from `chats.unread`.
4. **Unread and mentions come from the position** (`mentions_me`), recomputed in the same transaction as any change to the position or the chat's messages. There is one cap, and the phone's counts stand in until history arrives.
5. **The class is jaw's own:** never dropped or rebuilt, and copied by any rebuild. A refetch of app state can move the position forward but never back, and never touches "told".
6. **A chat merge keeps both rows' meaning.** The kept position is the later of the two, and "told" the earlier. Sending a receipt again is harmless; skipping one loses it. This has to happen before the merged chat is deleted, or `ON DELETE CASCADE` drops it with its unsent receipts.
7. **Plans pinned** in sync's `plans.txt`, with "owed" found by a search, not a scan.
