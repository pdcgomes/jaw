## In short

Nothing is marked data loss or inconsistency.

- **Typing stays in jawd's memory.** sync hands the engine's typing to `Typing::heard`, which never waits and never writes. Nothing on typing's way reaches the writer, the inbox, an ack or the outbox, and the engine forwards it unacked and unstored.
- **Presence in conversations is read, never written, by this branch.** It comes from M6's rows through `read::chat_presence` (two key lookups, pinned), in the window's own read transaction, and is told from that read. What the branch changes is when M6's `Following` and `Unknown` steps go: a keepalive timeout now writes only whom jawd follows, and only a new connection forgets who was around.
- **Everything is bounded:** typing's intake, presence's heard queue, whom a connection follows, the presence and roster chats each connection keeps.
- **WhatsApp's limits hold,** each switched off here: 20 new a minute across connections and restores, 200 a connection across restores, and nobody followed while nobody looks or during a timeout.
- **The race fix holds.** `follow` reads reachability and the count of ended connections together under sync's lock, and sync changes both in one hold at every end of a connection. With the fix switched off, its test fails.
- **Four test gaps, T1 to T4,** each a path of a rule the branch tests elsewhere, deciding what interfaces are told or whom jawd follows while it runs: sync's count of connections (T1, the first auditor's), a watch again from `since` (T2), a move's answer (T3), and a presence push that finds no room (T4). Their five tests are printed below. Each passes 3 of 3 at the head and fails 3 of 3 with its rule switched off, and the lander takes them (Landing, step 1). T5 is M6's and untested since M6, a follow-up.
- **The cost to a message's ack is nothing measurable.** Over 22 rounds of floods in three sets, presence at 20 changes a second was higher at p99 than the bare flood in 8 and lower in 14. The design's "about 5 ms" isn't supported (O2).
- **But the branch adds a cost of its own, on a message's way to interfaces (U1).** A change of an open one-to-one chat's person's presence reads the conversation's window, and the window's pacing counts that read, so a message in that chat in the next 50 ms waits until they're up. Measured: 41 ms rather than 2 ms, where main takes 2 to 3 ms either way. It's bounded at 50 ms and rare at real presence rates, so it doesn't break the budget's p99; it's a follow-up, with its fix and its test.
- **Main has moved** to `e12d8d34`, places and events with migrations 21 to 24 among it. The merge is clean, and on the trial landing the branch's tests, the five to adopt among them, pass.

The follow-ups are in Other. The one that changes behaviour (O1): presence last heard now stays through a drop, an engine restart or a logout until the next connection comes online, where main forgot it as the connection ended.

### WhatsApp's limits, and the race fix

- **How a connection is counted.** `Live` counts ended connections (`sync/mod.rs:44-46`), adding one in `cut_short` (`:158`). That runs under sync's lock, in the same hold as the state that follows it, at every end:
  - Disconnected, StreamReplaced, ConnectFailed, Connecting, LoggedOut, Unlinked, a ban or an update needed (`apply`, `:780-784`);
  - Connected after a keepalive timeout, whatsmeow's reconnect when its pings keep failing (`:777-779`);
  - the engine stopping (`restarting`, `:539`), and a logout jawd asked for (`logged_out`, `:259`).

  A keepalive timeout and its restore end none, nor does a restore heard late (`:738-740`). jawd's start reads the state and the count in one lock (`main.rs:604-607`), so `follow` never sees a count that has moved with the state still reachable, but at the Connected that begins a new connection, where it should.
- **What `follow` does with it.** It starts again (clears whom it followed, refused and what's written, and writes `Unknown`) only when WhatsApp can be reached on a count it hasn't followed on (`presence.rs:258-268`). It follows nobody while nobody looks or WhatsApp can't be reached (`:269`, `:303-307`), and stops at 200 or the minute's 20 (`:282`); the minute isn't cleared at a new connection or a restore. It wakes on the count as well as on the pushes (`:333`), so an end counted before its push still wakes it, and it then finds WhatsApp unreachable. The phone offline counts as reachable (`presence.rs:148-151`): WhatsApp's servers send presence whether or not the phone answers.
- **Switched off, each fails a test** (Switches): the minute cleared at a new connection or at a restore, the minute's 20 or the 200 off, following while nobody looks or during a timeout, a restore taken as a new connection with everyone asked for again, `Unknown` skipped at a new connection, the race fix (reachability from the pushes' word beside sync's count: "nobody known once the connection ended didn't happen"), and no wake on the count.
- **Through sync's own loop.** The first auditor's second probe wires `follow` to sync's `run` and a real `Live`, read as jawd's start reads it, and sends connection events through the loop:
  - a keepalive timeout leaves nobody known and who was around as it was, and its restore brings back the five at once, none asked for again;
  - Connected after a timeout, and a drop with its reconnect and no Connecting between, each forget who was around and ask for the five again, once.

  It passes 3 of 3 at the head.
- **But nothing of the branch's tests sync's count** (T1). With `cut_short` counting nothing, or a keepalive timeout counted as an end, every test of the branch's passes, and only the two probes fail.

### The cost

- **What the branch puts on presence's way.** M6 writes each change WhatsApp says, in a live transaction on the writer the inbox shares (`write_heard`), and reads the rosters again; that's unchanged. For a change of an open one-to-one chat's person, the branch adds one read of that conversation, carrying `chat_presence`'s two key lookups, and at most one push. Typing adds a lock and a push on sync's loop per event, and nothing on the writer.
- **The ack.** My release build of the head, the builder's method: 2,000 messages a second for 30 s, 256 in flight, on APFS clones of one made-up account of a million messages. Six rounds, the flood alone and with presence at 20 changes a second, at a load of 47 to 70 on 20 cores:

  | Arm | Ack p50 | Ack p99 (range) | Paired p99 | Commit to interface p50 / p99 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Flood alone | 15.5 ms | 48.6 ms (46.3 to 71.9) | | 30.1 / 60.0 ms |
  | Presence at 20 a second | 15.1 ms | 44.4 ms (37.9 to 50.9) | −5.9 ms, lower in 6 of 6 | 29.9 / 59.4 ms |

  Presence was heard about 650 times a run, none dropped, 20 followed, and 1.2 to 1.6 presence pushes a second reached the flood's interface. The first auditor's four rounds found −5.9 ms (lower in 4 of 4), and the builder's twelve +4.8 ms (higher in 8 of 12). Over all 22 rounds, presence was higher in 8 and lower in 14: nothing measurable.
- **The writer.** A slow flood of 10 messages a second for 40 s keeps the writer's own work small, and I asked jawd for the writer's run times at 37 s (three rounds of each arm):
  - The writer's transactions' run p99 was 7.4 to 8.5 ms bare and 7.0 to 8.0 ms with presence, its synced inbox commits on a loaded drive.
  - Their p50 fell from 344-383 µs to 179-213 µs, presence's own short transactions among them.
  - The queue's wait p99 rose from 0.5-0.8 ms to 2.6-3.6 ms, mostly presence's own jobs waiting behind a synced commit.
  - The ack's p50 was 5.2 to 5.6 ms bare and 5.6 to 5.7 ms with presence; its p99 (of 400) 10.2 to 13.4 and 10.7 to 15.5 ms.
- **A message's way to interfaces.** That slow set showed what the fast one hides. The open chat's messages' commit-to-interface max (96 a run) was 7.7 to 19.8 ms bare, and 46.0 and 53.0 ms in two of the three runs with presence. That's U1: a probe (Measurements) gives 41 ms for a message 10 ms after its person's presence changed, where alone it's 2 ms, and main 2 to 3 ms either way.
- **Within the design's budget?**
  - The ack's 10 ms p99 is measured on an unloaded M-series Mac; here the bare flood alone is at 46 to 72 ms. Only the difference can be judged on this machine, and it's nothing measurable.
  - 20 changes a second is far past what real comings and goings send for the 200 people jawd follows at most, and at 2 a second the builder found nothing either.
  - A message's way to interfaces (5 ms p99) holds at real presence rates, where a message within 50 ms of its person's presence change is rare. At the demo's rate, about one change a second for the open chat's person, it doesn't (U1).
- **Does the branch add to M6's cost?** On the writer, no: it writes M6's steps less often. On the read pool, one conversation read per presence change of an open one-to-one chat's person. On a message's way to interfaces, yes: U1.

## Findings

### Data loss

None. Nothing on typing's or presence's way reaches the inbox, an ack, the outbox, the spool or a file, and the only writes the branch moves are M6's runtime presence steps.

### Inconsistency

None. Typing moves no `v` or `seq`. A conversation's presence is read in the same read transaction as its window, and M6's events are in the same transaction as their rows. At every step the switches and probes drive, `followed` says whom jawd follows on the connection it's on.

### Test gaps

None of these blocks. Each is a path of a rule the branch tests elsewhere. Each decides what an interface is told, which jaw.db doesn't keep, or whom jawd follows, which jaw.db keeps only while jawd runs (cleared at every start). T1 to T4 have tests printed under "The tests to adopt", each passing 3 of 3 at the head and failing 3 of 3 with its rule switched off; the lander takes them (Landing, step 1).

- **T1, test gap. sync's count of connections is untested.** The first auditor's.
  - **Where:** `sync/mod.rs:158`, `:777-784`, `:539`, `:259`, and `main.rs:604-607`.
  - **What happens:** the keepalive fix and the race fix rest on that count, and presence's tests set it from their own harness. With `cut_short` counting nothing (`A_uncounted`), or with a keepalive timeout counted (`A_timeout_counts`), every test of the branch's passes.
  - **What a regression would do:** counting nothing leaves people `followed`, and shown as known, on connections that never asked for them, and who was around never forgotten. Counting timeouts asks for everyone again at each restore.
  - **Fix:** adopt `audit_each_connections_end_is_counted_with_its_state_and_a_keepalive_timeout_ends_none` and `audit_presence_follows_connections_as_syncs_loop_counts_them`. Each fails under both switches.
- **T2, test gap. A conversation watched again from `since` isn't tested for its presence.**
  - **Where:** `feed/client.rs:1343-1363`, `:695`.
  - **What happens:** the protocol says a watch again from `since`, after `resync` or a reconnect, says how things are now, and the design's step asks feed tests to watch again from `since` and hold what a fresh watch gives. With the answer from `since` leaving presence out (`X_since_no_presence`), every jawd test passes. `told_presence` would still note it as told, so the interface would show no presence for that conversation until its person's next change.
  - **Fix:** adopt `a_conversation_watched_again_from_since_says_its_persons_presence_as_a_fresh_watch_does`, which fails with it ("and so does one from since").
- **T3, test gap. A move's answer isn't tested for its presence.**
  - **Where:** `feed/client.rs:1365-1380`.
  - **What happens:** with a move's answer leaving presence out (`X_move_no_presence`), every jawd test passes. jaw-client takes an answer without presence as a chat with none (`jaw-client/src/client.rs:1383`), so the header would lose the person's presence at every scroll.
  - **Fix:** adopt `a_conversations_move_answers_with_its_persons_presence_as_its_watch_does` ("the move's answer").
- **T4, test gap. A presence push that finds no room isn't tested to wait for it.**
  - **Where:** `feed/client.rs:1061-1076`.
  - **What happens:** with a presence push that finds the queue full let go rather than owed (`X_full_drops`), every jawd test passes. A window isn't read while the queue is full, so the loss needs one read to bring a row's push, which takes the last room, and the person's presence, which then can't go. The interface would keep the person's presence from before until the next change.
  - **Fix:** adopt `a_presence_push_that_finds_no_room_waits_for_it_and_goes_as_they_are_then` ("its interface was left with … hidden").
- **T5, test gap (a follow-up, M6's). Those refused on one connection being asked for again on the next is untested.**
  - **Where:** `presence.rs:261-262`.
  - **What happens:** with `refused` never cleared (`X_refused_kept`), every test passes. Someone whose subscribe failed once would then not be followed again until jawd restarts. It's M6's code, untested since M6; the branch moved it from every change of reachability to each new connection (O8).
  - **Fix:** a demo world hook refusing the next subscribes, as `fail_asks` fails asks of the phone, and a test that a person refused on one connection is asked for on the next.

### Unpredictable performance

- **U1, unpredictable performance. A presence change of an open one-to-one chat's person holds back the next message in that chat for up to 50 ms.**
  - **Where:** `feed/client.rs:911` (a conversation marked by its person's presence), `:1530` (every read counts as the window's last), `:885-892` (`Window::due`).
  - **What happens:** each change of the person's presence, or of whether jawd follows them, marks the conversation's window, which is read at once as the first change after a quiet spell. A message in that chat that commits within the window's burst (50 ms) after that read isn't the first change after a quiet spell any more, so it waits until the burst is over.
  - **Measured** with an ignored probe in the feed's tests, at the feed's own timing, 20 rounds of each and two runs:
    - a message 10 ms after its person's presence changed reached the interface in 40.5 and 40.8 ms (median), 51.9 ms at most;
    - alone, in 1.7 and 1.8 ms;
    - the same probe built from `3878dd66`, where presence doesn't touch conversations, gave 2.4 and 3.0 ms with the change before it and 2.2 and 2.3 ms alone.

    In the slow floods, the open chat's commit-to-interface max went from 7.7-19.8 ms to 46 and 53 ms in two runs of three.
  - **Against the budget:** "an incoming message on its way to interfaces, once committed: 5 ms (p99)". It's bounded at the burst, and it touches only a message that commits within 50 ms of its own person's presence change, or of jawd starting or stopping following them (a change of focus, a keepalive timeout or restore). At real presence rates that's well under 1% of a chat's messages, so it doesn't break the p99. At the demo's rate, about one change a second for the open chat's person, it does, and at any rate it's an edge case of the kind the seventh principle calls a defect. Main has no such coupling.
  - **Fix (a follow-up):** tell a conversation's presence apart from its window's pacing, as typing is told. On a change of its person's presence, read `chat_presence` for that chat (its two lookups) and owe the push, paced and held for room as typing's are, without marking the window. Or keep it in the window's read, but don't let a read that only presence asked for count as the window's last. Either way the protocol's "It's read with the conversation's window, so it's paced as the window's updates are" changes, and the probe above becomes the test: the message goes at once, as on main. The probe's source is in this auditor's store (`presence-round1-b/probe_pacing.rs`). If the owner wants it before landing, the second fix is the smaller: `Window::wait` notes whether every mark since the last read was its person's presence alone, and `flush_one` then leaves `window.read` as it was.

### Other (follow-ups; none blocks)

- **O1, other. Presence last heard now stays through a real disconnect, until the next connection comes online.**

/// Runs a connection for `long` while its interface reads nothing.
async fn unread(watcher: &mut Watcher, long: Duration) {
    let until = Instant::now() + long;
    while let Ok(ready) = tokio::time::timeout_at(until.into(), watcher.client.ready()).await {
        if let Ready::Changes(Ok(changes)) = &ready {

        watcher.client.handle(ready).await;
    }

    assert_eq!(watcher.presences.get(OPEN), Some(&presence_of(ana, "hidden", None, false)));
    // Its connection takes all that was written before, up to a message in another chat, so the
    // next read of her conversation is the one that finds what follows.
    let elsewhere = bed.apply(vec![Change::Message(text(&person(5), "other", 30_000, Some(&person(5)), "olá"))]).await;
    watcher.settle(elsewhere).await;
    let came = text(OPEN, "now", 20_000, Some(OPEN), "cheguei");
    let following = Step::Following { people: vec![ana] };
    let online = Step::Presence { jid: OPEN.into(), state: Presence::Online };
    let seq = bed.apply(vec![Change::Message(came), Change::Folders(following), Change::Folders(online)]).await;
    // Its connection takes the change while its interface reads nothing.
    let end = Instant::now() + TYPED;
    while watcher.seen < seq || watcher.lines.is_empty() {
        assert!(Instant::now() < end, "the change was never taken");
        unread(&mut watcher, ms(20)).await;
    }
    // Long enough for the presence it owes to find no room.
    unread(&mut watcher, ms(300)).await;
    assert_eq!(watcher.lines.len(), 1, "its queue is full");
    // It reads the message, and is told she's online.
    let online = presence_of(ana, "online", None, true);
    let end = Instant::now() + TYPED;
    while watcher.presences.get(OPEN) != Some(&online) {
        assert!(Instant::now() < end, "its interface was left with {:?}: {:?}", watcher.presences, watcher.pushes);
        if let Ok(line) = watcher.lines.try_recv() {
            watcher.take(&line);
        }
        if let Ok(ready) = tokio::time::timeout(ms(10), watcher.client.ready()).await {
            watcher.client.handle(ready).await;
        }
    }
    assert_eq!(watcher.pushes.first().map(|(method, _)| method.as_str()), Some("messages"), "{:?}", watcher.pushes);
    assert_eq!(watcher.presence_told, [(OPEN.to_string(), online)], "her presence once there was room");
}
