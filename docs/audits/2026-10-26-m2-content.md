### Hygiene (follow-ups; none blocks)

- **H18. `status` costs twice what it does on main, and main's settings tests that poll it run more than twice as long.**
  - **Where:** each counter in `stored` opens a reader connection of its own (`store/mod.rs:723`, `:742`, `:750`, called from `state.rs:218-249`), and each connection's first statement parses the whole schema. The branch's schema has 101 objects where main's has 74, and the branch adds 7 counters to about 18.
  - **Measured:** `status` takes 13.3 to 13.8 ms p50 on a fresh jawd and 13.4 ms on the owner's copy, against 5.7 to 7.4 and 7.0 ms on main (debug builds, loads of 10 to 15). Opening a connection and counting costs 542 µs on the branch's schema and 340 µs on main's.
  - **What it costs:** no budget covers `status`, and only an interface's `status` request computes it (`sock.rs:682`). But three of main's settings tests poll it hundreds of times while durable writes fill a connection's queue (`fill_then`, `sock.rs:1754`; the loop at `:1447`).
    - Together they take 9.1 s at the head against 4.0 to 4.3 s on main, at loads of 11 to 18.
    - Each ran past 60 s in both of my suites at the head, where none did in main's.
    - Their 10 s waits for an answer (`sock.rs:1651`) are what ran out in the builder's gate at `011fdb3`, at a load of 52.
  - **The fix:** read the counters on one connection. A mutant doing so answered `status` in 0.45 ms p50.
  - **The test has to change with it.** With `status` that quick, `a_connection_full_when_its_change_is_answered_hears_the_answer_before_the_later_changes_push` deadlocks, 3 runs of 3.
    - Its loop writes `themes` requests to a connection it doesn't read until `status` says a reply waits. jawd stops reading that connection once 64 wait in its lane, so the test blocks in its own write first and fails at its 120 s.
    - So that loop needs a bound, or reads between rounds, in the same change.
    - Both pieces are main's code; this branch only made `status` dearer.

## The second audit's items

### I3, closed

- **Where:** `start_merge` now calls `follow` (`store/write.rs:1375`, `:1397`).
  - `follow` moves the gone chat's `pending` rows to the kept chat, and renames its set-aside votes, which now says which polls they're for (`store/write/polls.rs:54`).
  - Then, for each message they wait for that the kept chat already has, it calls `apply_pending`, which brings that poll's set-aside votes back first.
- **Why it's whole:**
  - A row the kept chat can't apply yet waits there, and is applied when its message arrives or a merge step moves the gone chat's copy (`move_merged`), as before.
  - Moving rows can't conflict: `pending`'s only key is its rowid, and renamed set-aside rows can't collide, as the second audit showed.

### I4, closed

- **Where:** `join` calls `polls::joining` before the JIDs move (`store/write.rs:1296`, `store/write/polls.rs:156`). It versions each poll a JID of each of the two people voted in, with its event, and the poll's chat if the poll is its newest message.
- **Why both, and only both:**

  - If only one of the two voted, the join maps their group onto one group again, you or a person, with the same newest vote, so the count stands. Only a poll both voted in counts two groups as one.
  - Nothing else changes a JID's person: `join` is the only write that does (`store/write.rs:1305`), and a JID that's new has no votes.
- **Tests:** with no poll versioned, the builder's window and re-watch test fails (`feed/tests.rs:1196`), and so do 6 of my scene's 40 seeds, which check at the join that every poll whose tally changed has a new version.

## What else was asked, checked

### P1's durability, as a change to every write

- **What SQLite does.** From `sqlite3PagerSetFlags` and `sqlite3WalFrames` in the bundled `sqlite3.c`:
  - A commit syncs the WAL only when `synchronous` is FULL or above. It then syncs with `SQLITE_SYNC_FULL`, which is `F_FULLFSYNC` on macOS, when `fullfsync` is on.
  - At NORMAL, the commit's sync flags are 0 and it syncs nothing.
  - A checkpoint syncs the WAL before it copies frames and `jaw.db` after, with FULL whenever `fullfsync` or `checkpoint_fullfsync` is on. The writer has both on, and the checkpointer the second.
  - `synchronous` isn't a flag PRAGMA, so setting it expires no statement. SQLite refuses to change it inside a transaction, and the writer only switches between transactions.
- **What the writer does.**
  - Every commit that precedes an answer or an ack checks `synchronous >= 2 && fullfsync == 1` on its own connection just before committing (`store/mod.rs:131`, `:1185`, `:1400`), and is refused otherwise.
  - The cached `PRAGMA` reads are current: SQLite makes every PRAGMA statement expire itself once it has run (`sqlite3VdbeRunOnlyOnce`), so a cached one is prepared again each time it runs.
- **So a normal commit can't stand in for a durable one.**
  - A NORMAL commit's frames become durable only once a later FULL commit has flushed the WAL behind them, or a checkpoint has copied them into `jaw.db` and flushed it.
  - Nothing is answered on a NORMAL commit itself.
  - The only flush a NORMAL commit ever asks for is of the WAL's header, after the WAL starts again, and it comes before the commit's frames (H10).
- **Measured** with the builder's file layer (`audit7_p1`), three rounds of live, inbox and durable commits, each round ending in a WAL restart:
  - live commits synced nothing;
  - each inbox commit and durable write flushed the WAL with `F_FULLFSYNC`;
  - the writer's checkpoint flushed the WAL and `jaw.db` fully;
  - the first live commit after each restart flushed the WAL's new header, and nothing else.
- **Main's own writes** gain from it too: they no longer prepare their statements again on each switch, which is why a conversion here is faster than main's.

### The flush-logging test (`store/mod.rs:2354`)

Every way I found of breaking durability fails it, each switched on in turn:
- `fullfsync` never set, with the read-back not checking it;
- `checkpoint_fullfsync` off;
- the checkpointer at `synchronous=OFF`;
- an inbox commit made at NORMAL while said to be synced;
- a durable write the same way;
- `journal_mode=wal` before `fullfsync`.

`fullfsync` switched as before fails the re-prepare test (`:2330`) alone, as a performance regression should.

### The new-database hole

- On a new file, `journal_mode=wal` commits its header change through the rollback journal, with the connection's sync flags, which were plain `fsync`s before `fullfsync` was set.
- By SQLite's rules the hole was real. On opening a database, SQLite deletes a WAL that sits beside an empty `jaw.db` (`pagerOpenWalIfPresent`), and with it every commit already flushed into that WAL.
- On macOS it was narrow in practice: the first FULL commit's `F_FULLFSYNC` asks the drive to flush everything it holds, the header's write among it, and nothing is acked before that commit.
- Setting `fullfsync` first closes it by construction, and the old order fails the test.

- **I3. A vote waiting in the chat being merged away, for a poll the kept chat already has, is never applied.**

    - `audit7_a8`: Ana's poll is in her chat under her number. Her vote, addressed to her chat under her LID before jaw knows they're one person's, waits there. ## What was asked, checked

### Links

- **The triggers keep `links` exact through edits, deletions, merges and replays.**
  - Every write to `messages` that can change a message's links fires a trigger in the same statement: insert, update of `chat, at, sender, mine, spans, detail, deleted`, and delete (`store/schema.rs:800-818`). The links document is keyed by `t`, which is set once at insert and never updated (`allocate`, `store/write.rs:1558`).
  - Measured (`audit6_l1`, three seeds of 150 steps; mix in Measurements): after every step, `links` equalled `message_links` over all messages, row for row. `links_fts` held one document for each message with links, its postings equal to a fresh index of `message_link_text` through `fts5vocab`, and `integrity-check` passed. Nothing was quarantined. The same check held on the first run's file (7,001 links, 61,025 postings).
  - The views can't fail on what the converter writes. Link spans have carried a `url` since the converter's first version, places and URLs are unique by construction, and a NULL preview URL is filtered out.
  - The comparison by postings matters: a contentless-delete FTS5 table merges a second insert of a rowid into the first without an error, so stale words would never raise one.
  - **Expiry** isn't implemented in jawd yet, so it can't be checked (H4).
- **The backfill is resumable, bounded and never counts a message twice.**
  - A step reads 64 messages by rowid range from `after` (pinned), skips a message the triggers have already linked (`LINKED`), and moves `after` in the same transaction.
  - `audit6_l2` interleaves steps with edits, copies and deletions on both sides of `after`, jawd restarted every fourth step and a step taken twice every fifth. Links were exact up to `after` at every step, and fully once it finished.
  - Switched off, both rules fail the builder's tests (Measurements).
  - A step that fails deterministically is retried forever, with backoff up to 5 minutes, and holds up the other backfill meanwhile (H7).
- **The fetch's SSRF guard.**
  - Every address is resolved once and filtered by the policy, and only allowed addresses are connected to, for the first request and each redirect (`send/link.rs:330-369`). That defeats DNS rebinding.
  - The check of the peer address after connecting is defence in depth: a direct connection reaches exactly the address it was given.
  - The caps hold: 512 KB of a page, 2 MB of an image (refused by `Content-Length` and while streaming), 5 redirects, 8,192 pixels a side and 128 MB decoded, no cookies, no proxy. The builder's tests pin the timeout, the redirects, the page's and the image's sizes, the decode's side and the cookies.
  - Not pinned: the redirect half of the check (T1). The policy lets IPv4-compatible IPv6 addresses through (H6), and the decode runs after the 3 s deadline (H5).
- **A waiting later message can't be lost or reordered.**
  - `send.text`, `send.poll` and forwards hold the chat's place from the request to the enqueue (`send/mod.rs:212`), so a later message to the chat is committed after the one fetching its preview. The builder's test with a slow page pins the order.
  - The fetch is bounded by its timeout, apart from the decode (H5).
  - If jawd is killed meanwhile, neither message has been answered, so nothing acknowledged is lost, and the interface sends again with its key.
  - Actions don't take the place, which is right: they aim at messages that already exist.
- **The 256 MB thumbnail bound.**
  - A file is written to `.part`, synced, renamed, and its folder synced before the row goes in (`store/thumbnails.rs:68-122`). The converter keeps a `Change::Thumbnail` only for a file it kept (pinned: `thumbnail_before_file` fails a test).
  - The bound counts the rows' bytes at start, plus what's been written since. `jawd status` reports the count, the bytes and the refusals.
  - Two gaps (H4): files whose row never committed aren't counted after a restart and are never removed, and no file is ever deleted.
  - The syncs sit on the live path (P2).

### Polls

- **Votes are idempotent.**
  - Each voter's newest vote wins by `(at, id)`. A replay, a duplicate or an older vote changes nothing, not even `v`.
  - `audit6_a6` sends Ana's three votes and Rui's two, each twice, in every rotation of their order, with the poll arriving at each of the 11 places among them, 55 runs. Each run ended with each voter's newest vote.
  - Votes before their poll wait, and count once it comes (builder's test, and a6).
  - Your phone's newer vote beats one of yours waiting to go: the overlay compares with your newest kept vote, and the outbox's `created_at` is the `at` the engine stamps on the vote (`store/write/outbox.rs:466`).
  - A vote in a poll deleted for everyone before it goes is withdrawn, with a notice.
  - **Not idempotent across a voter's two JIDs (I1).**
- **The quarantine round trip.**
  - `audit6_a7` holds `pending` at the bound with votes for polls that never come, then sends random votes, replays and slow poll arrivals. In four seeds of 600 steps, 23 to 45 real votes per run were set aside and came back, `pending` never passed 10,000, and every poll ended with each voter's newest vote.
  - **Not when the chat merges first (I2).**
- **Merges keep the newest vote** when each copy's votes come from the same JID (builder's test, and `fold_older_wins` fails it), **but not when they come from the person's two JIDs (I1).**
- **Tallies at read time scan no large table.** Each poll reads index ranges bounded by its own votes. The cost grows with votes times options (H1).
- **The 10,000 bound and quarantine never drop a vote.**

## Findings

### Data loss

None. No path drops a vote, a link or a thumbnail that was kept, and every file is synced before a row points to it. H3 is a latent exception, unreachable in practice.

### Inconsistency

These block approval.

- **I1. One person's votes under two JIDs count as two voters'.**
  - **Where:** `poll_voters` and `poll_votes` are keyed by the voter's `person_jids.n`, the JID WhatsApp gave (`store/write/polls.rs:23-31`, `store/schema.rs:852-858`). The tallies count rows per JID (`store/read.rs:350-359`).
  - **What happens:** two scenes in `/tmp/jaw-m2c-audit-s.diff`:
    - `audit6_a1`: a group poll, Ana known as one person under her number and her LID (one `person`). Her vote for Ramen from her number, then for Pizza from her LID: the poll shows Ramen 1, Pizza 1 and 2 voters. WhatsApp shows Pizza 1 and 1 voter.
    - `audit6_a2`: Ana's poll in her one-to-one chats under her number and her LID, each with her vote from that chat's JID, as WhatsApp addresses them. After the merge: Ramen 1, Pizza 1, 2 voters.
    - The builder's merge test passes because Ana votes from her number in both chats.
  - **Why it's inconsistency:** the design's exactly-once rule names "a JID as a phone number and as a LID", and the commit says merged copies keep each voter's newest vote. A derived count counts one vote twice, and a superseded choice stays counted.
  - **The fix:** tallies are read-time, so no data has to change. Resolve each `poll_voters` row to its person (`person_jids.person`), keep each person's newest `(at, id)`, and count that row's choices and voters. `mine` and your newest vote already read the account's JIDs. Pin it with a1 and a2.
  - Reactions have had the same JID-keyed shape since main.
- **I2. A vote set aside past the bound doesn't come back once its chat has merged.**
  - **Where:** `start_merge` moves what waits in `pending` to the kept chat's JID (`store/write.rs:1328`) but not the set-aside votes in quarantine, whose `ref` begins with the gone chat's JID and whose `raw` holds it as `chat` (`store/write/polls.rs:33-46`). And `set_aside_back` runs only when a new message is written (`store/write.rs:1766`), not where a merge applies what waits for a message it moves (`move_merged`, `store/write.rs:1432`).
  - **What happens:** `audit6_a3`. Ana's vote in her poll, which jaw hasn't seen, in her LID chat, is set aside at the bound. Her chats merge, then the poll comes. The poll shows no votes, and the vote stays in quarantine for good, counted in `votes_set_aside`.
  - The control, `audit6_a4`, does the same without the bound: the vote waits in `pending`, moves with the merge, and counts.
  - By reading, the other order fails too: a poll already in the chat being merged, with its votes set aside under the kept chat, moves without bringing them back.
  - **The fix:** treat set-aside votes as `pending` rows are treated. In `start_merge`, rewrite their `ref` prefix and `raw ->> '$.chat'` from the gone JID to the kept one, keeping the same row on a conflict. Bring them back wherever `apply_pending` runs, `move_merged` included. Pin it with a3 and the other order.

### Unpredictable performance (break a budget)

These break the live budget, "an incoming message on its way to interfaces, once committed: 5 ms (p99)", so they can't stand as follow-ups.

- **P1. Every insert into `messages` re-prepares about 0.4 ms of link-trigger programs whenever the writer switches commit kind.**
  - **Where:** preparing a statement that inserts into `messages`, or updates the columns the trigger watches, compiles `messages_links_insert` or `messages_links_update`, and through `links_derived` the whole `message_links` and `message_link_text` program (`store/schema.rs:762-813`). The writer sets `PRAGMA fullfsync` on each switch between synced and normal commits (`store/mod.rs:851-858`). `fullfsync` is a flag pragma, and setting one expires every prepared statement on the connection. Live traffic alternates synced inbox commits with normal conversions, so a conversion re-prepares its statements.
  - **What happens (SQL):** an insert with its statement cached costs 20 µs on main's schema and on the branch's. Toggling `synchronous` between inserts changes nothing. Toggling `fullfsync` makes it 44 to 48 µs on main's and 391 to 397 µs on the branch's, p99 0.76 to 3.4 ms.
  - **What happens in jawd:** `jawd bench inbox` with no inbox stream, three interleaved runs of 12 s each, at load 28 to 43: a live conversion 1.14 to 1.21 ms p50 and 5.8 to 6.2 ms p99 here, against 0.69 to 0.79 and 2.2 to 4.0 on main; a durable write 0.3 to 0.7 ms slower at p50 too. With 300 inbox rows a second, both were alike. The builder's "20 µs, level with main" was measured with statements prepared once.
  - **The fix:** leave `fullfsync` on, on the writer, and switch only `synchronous`. In WAL mode a `NORMAL` commit doesn't sync, so nothing changes in what's durable, and `commits_synced` still reads both. That also saves main's own re-prepares. Alternatively, or as well, make the triggers cheap to compile, for instance by deriving links in the writer's code. Re-measure with `bench inbox` against main.
- **P2. A live message's preview thumbnail is synced to disk before its batch is applied.**
  - **Where:** `keep_thumbnails` is awaited before the converted batch is applied (`sync/drain.rs:237-247`, `:319`). `Thumbnails::keep` writes, `F_FULLFSYNC`s the file, renames it and `F_FULLFSYNC`s the folder (`store/thumbnails.rs:68-122`).
  - **What happens:** `audit6_m1` measures one thumbnail kept at 19.3 to 19.8 ms p50 and 40.6 to 50.8 ms p99 (60 runs each, load 48). Every live message with a thumbnail, and every other message in its batch, waits that long before it's applied.
  - **The fix:** apply the batch first, then keep the thumbnails off the hot path and apply their `Change::Thumbnail` rows in a later transaction. That change already gives the message a new version, so windows show the thumbnail when it lands. `send.text` does the same before its durable commit (`send/outbox.rs:295`), holding the chat's place through two syncs. That's less pressing, but the same fix fits.

### Tests (missing)

These block approval: each is a rule the change touches that no test fails without, here.

- **T1. The redirect half of the SSRF guard.**
  - **Where:** `send/link/tests.rs:249-277` asserts only that a redirect to `10.0.0.1`, `169.254.169.254` or `[::ffff:192.168.1.1]` gives no preview within 500 ms.

On a network where the router at 192.168.1.1 answers, the switch would fetch its page.

### Hygiene (follow-ups; none blocks)

- **H1. A window over big polls costs 0.75 ms per 1,000 voters.**
  - **Where:** `mine` joins `person_jids` for every vote of every option, and your newest vote and the voter count walk every voter (`store/read.rs:350-359`).
  - **What happens:** at load 66 to 74, a 100-message window with one 12-option poll of 1,000 voters read in 1.5 ms p50; with 100 polls of 50 voters, 5.9 ms; with 20 polls of 1,000, 16 ms; with 5 polls of 5,000, 19 ms. The builder's 100 polls of 50 voters with 3 options read in 3.9 ms p50, 4.9 ms worst.
  - It's an index range, not a scan. But a page with big group polls passes the 5 ms page budget, and every vote re-reads its poll in each window showing it.
  - **The fix:** find the account's voter keys once, and probe `poll_votes` (message, voter, position) and `poll_voters` by key for `mine` and your newest vote.
- **H2. Votes history keeps as messages of their own hold places among the 10,000 for good.**
  - **Where:** history's standalone `PollUpdateMessage`s become `Keep { kind: "vote" }`, which go to `pending` as kind `vote` even when their poll is there (`store/write.rs:1837-1853`). `apply_pending` skips them.
  - **What happens:** `audit6_a5`: `votes_waiting` is 1 with the poll present. The first run kept 50.
  - **The fix:** keep them under a kind of their own, outside the bound and `votes_waiting`.
- **H3. The set-aside key can repeat for two different votes.**
  - **Where:** `SET_ASIDE` keys a vote without an ID (only those H2 votes) by `pending.n`, which `pending` reuses (no `AUTOINCREMENT`). `DROP_SET_ASIDE` deletes whatever `SET_ASIDE` skipped on a conflict (`store/write/polls.rs:33-41`).
  - **What happens:** in a long-contrived order, two such votes from one voter in one poll could take one key, and the second be dropped.
  - **The fix:** key them by a hash of their raw, or delete only the rows whose key is now in quarantine.
- **H4. Thumbnail files are never deleted.**
  - **Where:** nothing deletes from `media/links/`. A file whose row never committed (a crash, a failed commit after `keep`) isn't counted after a restart, since `Thumbnails::open` counts rows (`store/mod.rs:408-409`), and stays.
  - A deletion for everyone clears the message's raw and detail but leaves its thumbnail's file.
  - When expiry lands, "Erased when asked" needs it to remove the thumbnails no message names, after a synced commit, and to merge `links_fts` as it merges `messages_fts`.
  - `m3-media-in`'s `media_drop` is the pattern to follow, and its `media_files` is a second store of files by hash to reconcile with this one.
- **H5. The thumbnail's decode runs after the 3 s deadline** (`send/link.rs:121-124`). The largest image the caps let through, a 5,600-pixel-square PNG, took the preview to 397 ms with a 300 ms deadline, and about 0.3 s with the default. Bound it by the same deadline.
- **H6. The SSRF policy lets IPv4-compatible IPv6 addresses through** (`::a.b.c.d`, `::/96`). `to_canonical` converts only `::ffff:` (`send/link.rs:162-182`). Refuse `::/96`, or check its embedded IPv4. I couldn't see how macOS routes them: the sandbox refuses the connection.
- **H7. A backfill step that fails deterministically is retried for ever,** and holds up the other backfill in the same loop (`sync/backfill.rs`). It's visible through `links_backfill`, `polls_backfill` and quarantine.
- **H8. A chat's place is keyed by the JID the interface gives,** so sends to one chat under its LID and its number could pass each other (`send/mod.rs:212`).
- **Not rules, noted:** incoming votes in a poll deleted for everyone are dropped, and windows hide a deleted poll's tallies; switched off (`deleted_poll_counts`), nothing fails, nor should it.
