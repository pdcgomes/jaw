### Unpredictable performance (stand as follow-ups)

**P1. A merge step that keeps failing is retried in a loop with no pause, forever.** This is new, and fix it before release.
- `merge::run` (`sync/merge.rs:31`) pauses only when `store.apply` returns an error. A merge step that fails for a reason that doesn't pass is quarantined, and `apply` then returns `Ok`.
- The task immediately finds the merge still waiting and tries again. One failing background transaction follows another, each writing a log line, and the quarantine row says only `merge`, not which chats.
- I ran into it by accident: a mutation of mine broke a statement's parameters, and the test spun for 18 minutes on "kept a change that failed in quarantine (merge )".
- Nothing is lost, and live writes still go first. But it holds a CPU and the writer's background lane, and the log grows without a bound, against "Bounded files".
- This dates from `957b9a7`.
- *Fix:* back off when `applied.quarantined > 0`, doubling up to a minute. Name the chats in the quarantine reference. Count a stuck merge in `jawd status`.

**P2. Still open from the second audit:**
- Live writes during history are over budget on a loaded Mac: 9.95 ms at p99, against 5 ms. The owner's lean is to accept that for now and re-measure on a quiet Mac.
- `jaw.db` is 78% larger after migration 6 (685 MB became 1,218 MB at a million messages), since the old pages stay on the freelist. Reclaim that space at a quiet moment, or say so.

### Hygiene (stand as follow-ups)

- **H1. The move-first paths have no test of their own.** These are `located`, the `moved_from_merging` call in `upsert_message`, and the one in `set_read`. Removing either of the first two leaves every test passing, because the merge step's own tombstone and pending handling reaches the same end. What they add is the state during a merge: a revoke or edit shows at once rather than when the step reaches its message. Add a test that checks the state before the merge steps run.
- **H2. A quarantine row outlives the failure it records.** When a history notification quarantined in one run is fetched in a later one, the drain deletes the inbox row but leaves its `quarantine` row, so `status` keeps counting it. The same goes for any quarantined inbox row that later converts. Delete the quarantine row with what it names.
- **H3. A refused second jawd can leave its socket file behind.** If it starts while the first is stopping (socket gone, lock still held), it binds a socket, is refused by `jaw.lock`, and leaves the socket. Take the lock before binding, or remove the socket on refusal.
- **H4. Converting the spike's history is slow.** It took 25.6 s for 84,394 rows (3,300 a second) on the owner's copy, twice. That's a one-off at the upgrade, against 20,000 messages a second through the same lane in the bench. Worth a look, but not a budget.
- **H5. Still open from earlier audits:**
  - second audit H5 (kept inbox rows), H8 (fixtures), H9 (`session.db` without `fullfsync`) and H10 (the first audit's H4: `STRICT` for migration 4's tables, `pending`'s growth, `clearChat` and `deleteChat`);

## The sending rebase: is the builder's account of a merge sound?

Yes, with five conditions. The account matches the code: `join`, then `merge_direct_chats`, then `start_merge`. Writes for the gone chat go to the kept one from then on. `merge_step` moves 200 messages at a time through `move_merged`, then removes `chat_merges`, then the gone chat (cascading), and records the removal. `Writer::transaction` runs `settle` before every commit.

1. **Merge `reads` in `start_merge`, and again just before the gone chat is deleted.**
   - Merging at the start is right, since every write naming the gone chat by JID goes to the kept one.
   - But a writer that still holds the gone chat's `n` (an interface's open chat, a queued read) would write a `reads` row the final cascade then deletes, with its owed receipts.
   - Merging again at the end, by the same rule (the later position, the earlier "told", the larger `known`, the phone's counts from the winner), is idempotent and closes that gap.
   - This replaces my send audit's "only in the last merge step".
2. **Don't merge `regroup`.** Only direct chats merge. Assert that the two chats aren't groups, as my send audit asked, rather than moving rows that can't exist.
3. **`fold` moves `outbox` and send-key rows to the kept copy before the folded copy is deleted.** Use `UPDATE OR IGNORE`, keeping the kept copy's own row if it has one, and drop a pending duplicate only if the kept copy reached the server. Messages that move keep their `n`, so their rows follow them unchanged.
4. **A message deleted for me loses its outbox row.** `move_merged`'s tombstone branch calls `delete_row`, and the cascade takes the outbox row of a send still pending. Decide whether deleting for me cancels a send that hasn't gone yet. On the phone it doesn't, so either keep the row and send it, or make it an explicit choice with a test.
5. **Everything sending writes about a message finds it through `located`.** That covers edits and revokes of your own messages, owed read receipts and outbox lookups by message ID, so a message still in the gone chat is moved first. Chats are found by JID through `chat_jid`, never by a stale `n`. And `advance_read` calls `stamps.unsettle` and leaves the counting to `settle`.

With these, the plan in my send audit (I5, amendments 1 to 6) stands. Removing `start_merge`'s `UPDATE outbox SET chat …` at the rebase is right.

## Findings

### Data loss

**L1. The spike's history rows that don't become a message row are deleted, and nothing keeps them.**
- `history_rows` (`sync/drain.rs:403-416`) adds a `Drained::History` for every row that decodes, whatever `convert::message` made of it. `Drained::History` deletes the `history_raw` row (`store/write.rs:511-514`).
- A row that becomes a message keeps its raw in `messages.raw`. Any other row's raw is simply gone.
- On a copy of the owner's `jaw.db`, this happened to 1,025 of its 84,394 history rows:
  - **997 edits** (`protocolMessage` type 14). Their effect is in the edited messages' `text` and `edited_at`, but their raw is gone. The edited message's own raw is the original, so a rebuild of `messages` from raw would undo them.
  - **21 final positions of live locations.** These carry `WebMessageInfo.final_live_location` and a duration, with no `message`. jaw shows nothing for them and now keeps nothing of them.
  - **7 messages with no content and no stub,** carrying only receipts.
- The blob path keeps exactly these rows: `remainder()` puts every message that didn't become a row into `history_kept`. Only the path for history the spike kept raw drops them.
- The spike's history can't be fetched again. The backup that still holds these rows is removed at the first clean stop (H2), which in my run was 13 minutes after the upgrade. So the first time a jawd from `main` runs on the owner's `jaw.db` and stops cleanly, they're gone for good.

*Fix:* drain a `history_raw` row only when one of its changes is a `Change::Message` or `Change::Edited` that carries its raw. Keep any other row, in the same transaction, either:
- left in `history_raw` (it's the raw table, and nothing else writes to it now), or
- moved into `history_kept` under a key of its own, such as `history_raw <chat> <id>`.

*Test:* three `history_raw` rows (one holding an edit, one holding only `final_live_location`, one with no message). After the drain, each raw is still in `jaw.db`.

### Inconsistency

**I1. A delete for me, revoke or edit that arrives during a chat merge is undone or never applied.**
- `start_merge` (`write.rs:1001`) points every write for the gone chat at the kept one straight away. `merge_step` (`write.rs:1034-1074`) then moves the gone chat's messages, 200 at a time, in background transactions.
- Until a message has moved, `amend_message` (`write.rs:1376`) looks for it in the kept chat (`target`, `write.rs:1360`) and doesn't find it. Then:
  - **A delete for me** writes a tombstone in the kept chat and returns. `merge_step` later moves the message in without checking tombstones (`write.rs:1053`). The message is back, and it stays back: the next replay finds the tombstone and does nothing.
  - **A revoke, edit, reaction, star or receipt** waits in `pending` under the kept chat's JID. Moving a message doesn't apply `pending`; only an insert does (`apply_pending`, `write.rs:1337`). So it waits forever, and a message its sender deleted for everyone keeps showing.
- Reproduced with a scratch test (Measurements). After the join and before the merge steps, I sent a delete for me, a revoke and an edit for three messages still in the LID chat. After the merge, all three showed as before, with two rows in `pending` and one tombstone.
- This isn't a rare race. Joins come in bulk at each connect (contacts and the LID map), at the same moment the full app-state refetch replays every `deleteMessageForMe`. Meanwhile the merge's steps queue behind history in the background lane.

*Fix:* in `merge_step`, for a message the kept chat has a tombstone for, delete it with its event instead of moving it. For each message moved, apply what `pending` holds for it under the kept chat's JID. Alternatively, have `target` and `upsert_message` also look in chats being merged into the kept one.

*Test:* the scratch test.

**I2. The chat a merge removes leaves its key to the next new chat.**
- `merge_step` deletes the gone chat (`write.rs:1072`). `chats.n` is `INTEGER PRIMARY KEY` without `AUTOINCREMENT` (`schema.rs:60-61`).
- The gone chat is usually the LID one. It was made recently, so its `n` is often the largest, and the next new chat takes it. Reproduced: the LID chat had `n = 2`, was merged away, and the next chat (Rui's) got `n = 2`.
- The feed names chats by key, both as a chat event's `key` and as a message event's `chat`. Migration 2 says this is "so the feed can be sorted into windows without reading the rows, even ones since removed". A client catching up across the merge would sort the LID chat's old message events into Rui's window.
- This is the first audit's I1 again, for a table this branch starts deleting from.

*Fix,* either:
- (a) Rebuild `chats` with `AUTOINCREMENT` in migration 6, which hasn't merged and can still change. `chats` is small. Recreate every index, including `chats_by_sort` on the generated `sort` column, with foreign keys off as the runner already has them.
- (b) Don't delete the gone chat. Keep it hidden, with its `chat_merges` row, as the alias the design asks for ("found through its alias table").

*Test:* the scratch test.

### Tests

**T1. No test fails if inbox and durable commits stop syncing.**
- I changed `store_inbox`'s `synced(true)` and `live_write`'s `synced(durable)` to `synced(false)`. Every test still passed, including `inbox_rows_and_durable_changes_are_synced_before_they_are_answered_and_live_ones_are_not` (`mod.rs:1679`).
- The reason: `synced` is counted after any successful commit (`mod.rs:780`, `mod.rs:941`), not from the setting the commit ran under.
- This is the third point of the first audit's H3, for the rule the owner chose to fix first.

*Fix:* count a commit as synced only when `Writer::synced` is `Some(true)` at its commit (the PRAGMAs are read back there), or record the setting in a test-only commit hook. The existing test then fails without the switch.

### Unpredictable performance (may stand)

**P1. The ack path and live writes are at or past their budgets on a loaded Mac.** Load was 17 to 24 throughout.
- A synced inbox commit took 5.0 / 10.5 / 12.1 ms (p50 / p99 / max). The budget is 10 ms at p99 for the whole round trip.
- A live message took 11.6 ms at p99, 172 ms at most, while history went in. The budget is 5 ms; the owner's lean is to accept this for the first version.
- During indexing, it took 4.5 ms at p99 and 64 ms at most.
- The cost is the flush. Grouped acks, the review's option B, are the way down. Re-measure on a quiet Mac, as the owner asked.

**P2. A data step needs about 2.6 times the database free, not the 1.2 times checked.**
- `back_up` checks for 1.2 times the database (`schema.rs:734`).
- Migration 6 on a 707 MB database peaked at 2.53 GB on disk: the backup (696 MB), the WAL (711 MB, since the step is one transaction), and `jaw.db` itself, grown to 1,240 MB with the old pages left on the freelist.
- With only 1.2 times free, the step fails with `SQLITE_FULL`, rolls back, and jawd refuses to start. Nothing is lost, but the check says there's room when there isn't, and `jaw.db` stays 75% larger afterwards.
- *Fix:* check for the backup plus 1.8 times the database for a step that rebuilds `messages`, or write each data step's measured need beside it. Correct the design's 1.2 as well.
- Not blocking for the owner: 73 MB needs about 190 MB free.

### Hygiene (stand as follow-ups)

- **H1. `messages` is declared "derived from raw"** (`schema.rs:401`), but edits, revokes, receipts' status and stars keep no raw once applied, so a rebuild from raw would lose them. Until amendments keep their raw, say so in the class comment, which can still change since migration 6 hasn't merged. The tables from migrations 1 to 4 have no declared class anywhere; list them.
- **H2. The backup goes at the first clean stop** (`main.rs:287-291`), minutes after an upgrade. Keep the latest one until the next upgrade, or for some days, and show it in `jawd status`. Do this with L1.
- **H3. A failed full app-state refetch is only logged** (`refresh.rs:37`). Until the next engine connects, anything a crashed engine left behind stays missing; retry with back-off. The refetch's cost on a real account isn't measured either. Each mutation is one synced inbox commit of about 5 ms, and an account can have thousands (stars, contacts, mutes). Watch the "fetched app state again in full in N s" line on the owner's first run.
- **H4. `fold` can drop a downloaded copy's media row.** It moves media with `UPDATE OR IGNORE` (`write.rs:1115`), then deleting the gone message cascades to the rows that collided. A downloaded copy's row can go while the kept row says `remote`. Prefer the row with a `path`.
- **H5. Inbox rows kept on purpose stay forever.** That covers poll votes, encrypted reactions and gone history notifications in quarantine. They're converted again at every start and logged as "converted … left from before" (`drain.rs:269`). Bound them and count them in `status` (first audit H4).
- **H6. Two jawds starting together can race the backup's `.part` file.** Each removes it and writes its own. Take `jaw.lock` before migrating (review P9).
- **H7. Schema steps run with foreign keys off and no `foreign_key_check`.** Say in `migrations.lock`'s header that a step which drops or rebuilds a referenced table is a data step.
- **H8. No checked-in fixture databases yet.** The upgrade test builds each version from the migrations with one inbox row and one history row. My upgrade of the owner's copy stands in this once.
- **H9. `session.db` commits at `FULL` without `fullfsync`,** relying on `jaw.db`'s next `F_FULLFSYNC` to flush the drive. As the review says, that's right for anything that precedes an ack. Prekeys uploaded with no `jaw.db` commit after them rely on the drive's own flush.
- **H10. The first audit's H4 items still open:** `STRICT` for migration 4's tables, `pending`'s growth, and `clearChat` and `deleteChat`.

## Sending, which rebases onto this

My send audit's read-position plan (its I5, with six amendments) and its rebase list stand. Nothing here makes that plan harder, but it adds these points:

1. `start_merge`'s `UPDATE outbox SET chat = ?1 WHERE chat = ?2` (`write.rs:1007`) is written for main's outbox, keyed by chat JID. Sending's rebuilt outbox has no `chat` column, so the statement goes. Outbox rows follow their message, whose `n` the merge keeps. `fold` must move them before it deletes the folded copy (send audit, rebase item 3).
2. I2's fix decides where `reads` is merged:
   - if gone chats are kept as aliases, merge `reads` at the merge's end, with nothing cascading;
   - if `chats` is rebuilt, the rebuild leaves `reads`, `outbox` and `recipients` untouched (send audit, rebase item 4).

   This is the one choice the two branches must agree on.
3. I1's fix applies what waits for a message as it moves. Sending's own waits for messages in a gone chat (owed receipts, outbox rows) must follow the same path.
4. Every transaction that changes a message or a read position runs `write::settle` before it commits, as `Writer::transaction` does. `advance_read` should call `stamps.unsettle` and let `settle` count, rather than count itself. Any sending transaction outside `Writer::transaction` that touches messages or reads must call `settle` too.
5. `settle` counts `at > read_at`. With `(at, n)` positions this becomes `(at, n) > (?, ?)`, keeping the `system`/`call` exclusion and the 1,000 cap, with its plan pinned (send audit, amendment 5).
6. A message's status only rises by `status_rank` (`write.rs:1166-1182`): `failed` replaces only `pending`, and nothing goes back to `pending`. A manual retry that marks a failed message pending again can't go through `upsert_message`. Give it its own path, or a rank.
7. Send targets resolve through `chat_jid`, so a message to someone's LID or number lands in their one chat, during a merge as well.
8. Migrations: sync takes 5 and 6, so sending's become 7 and 8, each with a line in `migrations.lock` and its kind declared. Dropping `read_at` from `chats` is a data step, which takes a second backup of the owner's file. The fingerprint, fault-at-every-statement and hash tests cover the new migrations without changes.

## Findings

### Data loss

**L1. Events are acknowledged before they're durable (review D1; the owner chose option A, "durable acks now").** `crates/jawd/src/store/mod.rs:129` sets `synchronous=NORMAL`, and `realtime_write` (`mod.rs:598-619`) commits the inbox row at `NORMAL` and then sends the ack (`mod.rs:606-607`). Since `7fcd8eb`, everything the engine `keep()`s goes this way: messages, app-state mutations, push names, groups, group changes and pictures, and since `943ae79` receipts too. A power cut or kernel panic after the ack loses events WhatsApp considers delivered and won't send again. `apply_durable` exists now but only the spool drain and the clean marker use it. The design's rule ("Durable before acknowledged") and the owner's decision both apply to this path, and this range widens it.
*Fix:* commit inbox rows at `FULL` + `fullfsync` before their ack (the `durable()` switch, with every PRAGMA read back; see H1). Then measure `jawd bench link --commit` and the live-message latency against the restated budget (10 ms p99 for "durable and acknowledged").

**L2. No connection sets `checkpoint_fullfsync` (review D2).** None of the writer (`mod.rs:127-135`), the checkpointer (`mod.rs:138-140`), the readers (`read.rs:52-61`), the ad hoc connections in `Store::remembered` and `Store::count` (`mod.rs:230-241`), or `session.db` (`engine/internal/wa/engine.go:80-84`) sets it. On macOS a power cut after a checkpoint can leave `jaw.db` or `session.db` malformed, not just short of its last commits. Sync is what makes the checkpointer copy gigabytes (L9), so this range makes the risk real.
*Fix:* `PRAGMA checkpoint_fullfsync=ON` on every `jaw.db` connection, read back. On `session.db`, through a registered mattn driver with a `ConnectHook`; that driver should also take `_txlock=immediate` (review D4).

**L3. App-state changes can be lost without the resync meant to bring them back (D4's app-state point, `943ae79`).** whatsmeow stores a collection's new version before it dispatches that version's mutations (`appstate/decode.go:230`, `storeMACs` called from `DecodePatches`; `FetchAppState` dispatches afterwards). A collection is fetched in full again only after a stop that wasn't clean. Three things defeat that:
- (a) The running marker `"0"` is written at `NORMAL` through the live lane (`sync/mod.rs:110-113`), after the engine has been spawned (`main.rs:184` spawns it, `main.rs:188` starts `sync::run`). whatsmeow can advance a version before `"0"` is committed, or the commit can be lost in a power cut while `session.db`'s version survives. The next start then reads the previous `"1"` and doesn't resync. If that apply fails (see I5), the error is logged and the start goes on with `"1"` still stored.
- (b) A clean stop (`sync/mod.rs:213-226`) calls `LinkCancel`, which is only `client.Disconnect()` (`engine.go:362-364`), waits a fixed 300 ms (`SETTLING`, `sync/mod.rs:231`), then durably writes `"1"`. Disconnecting doesn't stop a `FetchAppState` already dispatching, and 300 ms covers about 60 mutations once acks are durable (L1, about 5 ms each). Mutations dispatched after that are never kept, the version has moved on, and the marker says clean.
- (c) If the engine dies and the supervisor restarts it while jawd keeps running, nothing resyncs: `refreshed` is set once per jawd run (`sync/mod.rs:122, 162`).
The mutations at stake include delete-for-me, pins, archives, mutes, read marks and stars.
*Fix:* make the resync's trigger sound.
- Option 1, simplest: request a full resync of every collection at every start and after every engine restart, made idempotent (I2 fixes the one mutation that isn't), with its cost measured on a real account.
- Option 2: patch whatsmeow to store the version only after its mutations have been dispatched (the review's option B already plans a whatsmeow patch).
With either, write `"0"` durably before the engine is started. A longer sleep is not a fix.

**L4. What WhatsApp sent and jaw doesn't read yet is thrown away (review I11, the parts that lose data now).** Two places:
- History: `fetch.rs:139-148` keeps only what `convert::history::blob` turns into changes (`convert/history.rs:12-90`): each conversation's name, pin, archive, mute, unread counts, community and messages, the push names, and the phone-to-LID map. Then `fetch.rs:178` deletes the blob, and a blob can't be fetched again. Every other `Conversation` field goes, along with the blob's status messages, past participants, global settings and anything else in it.
- Live messages: `convert::web_message` (`convert/mod.rs:73-98`) rebuilds a `WebMessageInfo` and stores that as `raw` (`convert/mod.rs:315`). The engine's `MessageInfo` fields that don't fit (`SenderAlt`, `RecipientAlt`, addressing mode, server ID, the edit attribute, retry count, multicast, the device-sent destination) are dropped once the inbox row is deleted.
The design says "nothing WhatsApp sends is lost before jaw understands it". Once this lands on the owner's account, whatever was dropped is gone for good.
*Fix:*
- Before deleting a blob, keep the `HistorySync` with each conversation's `messages` emptied (their raws are kept per message), compressed, in a table of raw data.
- Keep a live message's whole engine envelope (the inbox payload, which also holds `Decrypted`) as its raw, or in a column beside it, with a format marker, so a history copy can't replace it (I2).

**L5. A history blob that can never be downloaded holds up every blob after it.** `engine.go:468-471` answers every `DownloadHistorySync` error with `retry: true`, including whatsmeow's `ErrMediaDownloadFailedWith403/404/410`, which mean the blob is gone. `fetch.rs:70-87` then retries the same offer forever, at up to one try a minute, and handles offers one at a time. Every later blob waits, including on-demand history from `history.more` and the blobs of a new sync. Blobs on WhatsApp's servers can expire while they wait. The owner's `jaw.db` holds spike-era notifications in its inbox (20 of them were quarantined against the demo engine), so with the real engine the first expired one would stall all history. The design's rule is that a failure "never blocks the changes after it".
*Fix:* answer 403, 404 and 410 with `retry: false`, so jawd quarantines the notification. And don't hold the queue on a `Later` failure: after a few tries, move on to the next offer and come back to the failed one later.

### Inconsistency

**I1. Row keys others hold are reused (review I12), and a reused key loses its feed event.** `messages.n` and `people.n` are `INTEGER PRIMARY KEY` without `AUTOINCREMENT`, and since `7fcd8eb` rows of both are deleted: delete-for-me (`write.rs:497-521`) and joining people (`write.rs:792`). Within one transaction, `Stamps::record` (`write.rs:325-336`) treats `(kind, n)` as already stamped, so a new row that reuses a deleted row's `n` writes no event. Its `v` then equals the seq of a different row's event. I reproduced this in a scratch test: one live batch deletes chat A's newest message (n=2) and adds a message to chat B, which gets n=2 and `v`=9. The only events are "message 2" for chat A (seq 7) and "chat" events at seq 8 and 9, so chat B's messages window is never told about the new message. Across transactions, the new row inherits the old key, which the feed and find already named for something else. The background lane's `SELECT coalesce(max(n), 0) + 1` (`write.rs:902-903`) depends on the same reuse. The rule file: "Row keys others hold are never reused (`AUTOINCREMENT` where rows are deleted)".
*Fix:* never reuse `messages.n` or `people.n`. Either rebuild both tables with `AUTOINCREMENT` (twelve steps, every index and trigger recreated, `foreign_keys` off around the transaction; measured as a data step, about 6 s per million messages per the review), or allocate `n` from a persisted high-water mark on every insert. Add tests: delete the newest message or person and insert one in the same transaction and in the next one. The new row gets a new key and its own event.

**I2. Derived counters are adjusted by hand, and the new resync zeroes unread counts (review I4, rule "Derived values are derived").**
- `message_count` is adjusted with `+1` at `write.rs:963-965` and `-1` at `write.rs:506`.
- `unread` and `mentions` are adjusted with `+1` or set to 0 at `write.rs:970-979`, set from the phone's count in history (`convert/history.rs:72-73`), and zeroed by read receipts and app state.
- `markChatAsRead` sets unread and mentions to 0 unconditionally (`convert/app_state.rs:45-52`), ignoring the action's `messageRange`. The full resync that `943ae79` adds replays every collection's mutations after any stop that wasn't clean, so each crash zeroes the unread count of every chat that got messages after its last mark-read.
- A history conversation's `unread_count`, a snapshot older than the live messages, overwrites what live messages counted.
- Deleting an unread message for me doesn't lower the count.
The owner decided that unread is derived from the read position.
*Fix:*
- `message_count` kept by `AFTER INSERT` and `AFTER DELETE` triggers on `messages`, recounted in the migration, with the hand updates removed.
- A read position per chat, set from your own messages, read-self receipts and `markChatAsRead`'s range (it only moves forward).
- `unread` and `mentions` recomputed from it in the same transaction (capped, a `mentions_me` column), with the phone's count standing in only until history has arrived.
- Tests: a replayed mark-read, a late history snapshot, and deleting an unread message.

**I3. One conversation can still become two chats (review I1, the part left).** `chat_jid` (`write.rs:582-594`) finds an existing direct chat through the person's other JID, but only if that person is already joined. App state, receipts and messages that name a LID before the join is known create a chat of their own (`ensure_chat`, `write.rs:596-605`; app state's chat changes go through `upsert_chat`). The later join (`write.rs:770-795`) moves people, not chats. The result is two chat-list entries for one person, and the same message ID can be stored in each. `pending.chat` names a JID, so amendments can wait under the wrong one.
*Fix:* when a join leaves one person with two direct chats, merge them. Move messages in background batches, keeping one row per message ID by the merge rules, and move tombstones, pending rows and folder items. Recompute the kept chat's summary and counters, and remove the other chat with its events. Add a test: messages, an app-state pin and a receipt under a LID, then the join, then one chat.

**I4. A history copy still undoes newer state (review I2, the part left).** In `upsert_message`:
- An incoming copy with an older `edited_at` replaces a newer edit; only a copy without one is ignored (`write.rs:890-893`).
- The `UPDATE` (`write.rs:937-947`) replaces `raw` with whichever copy came last, so a history copy replaces the live envelope L4 requires keeping.
- A reaction removal deletes whatever reaction is there, whatever its time (`write.rs:1097-1098`), against the rule that a reaction wins by its time.
*Fix:* the edit applies only if `edited_at >= ` the stored one; `raw` keeps the live envelope over a history copy; a removal deletes only a reaction no newer than itself. Tests for each.

**I5. Writes fail at once with `SQLITE_BUSY` under concurrent reads, and some callers drop them (the second flaky test; product, not test).** Every writer transaction is `BEGIN DEFERRED` (`mod.rs:408, 467, 564`) and reads before it writes. When a deferred transaction upgrades to a write, SQLite doesn't call the busy handler (to avoid deadlock), so any momentary holder of the WAL write lock makes it fail at once with "database is locked". A reader or the checkpointer takes that lock for a moment when it reads a torn wal-index header while the writer commits (`walIndexReadHdr`). The writer's 5 s `busy_timeout` never applies. I reproduced the error in 2 of 25 runs of the full suite (method under Measurements). With `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` on the writer's three transactions, 30 runs gave none.
What the error does in the product:
- The converter and the fetcher retry, which is fine.
- `apply_soon` (`sync/mod.rs:87-94`) logs and drops the change: the account's JIDs and app-state versions.
- `refresh::store_changes` drops contacts, groups and LID numbers until the next start.
- The running marker's apply only logs (L3a).
- A failed index step clears `backlog` (`mod.rs:359-363`), so text written in the background stays unsearchable until another background batch arrives or jawd restarts.
*Fix:* `transaction_with_behavior(TransactionBehavior::Immediate)` for every writer transaction. No caller may drop a transient failure: retry it, as the converter does. An index step that fails tries again later instead of switching indexing off.

### Unpredictable performance

**P1. The WAL grows without bound during history (review D3), and live messages stall.** Measured on `943ae79`'s `jawd bench store` (1,000,000 messages):
- The WAL peaked at 9,331 MB for a 541 MB database, against the review's 7.1 GB.
- A live message waited 1.36 / 8.30 / 328 ms (p50 / p99 / max) while history went in, and 0.82 / 7.60 / **1,963 ms** during the 158 s of indexing.
- The builder's own 7.6 ms p99, and the review's 6.3 ms on an idle Mac, are over the 5 ms live budget too.
Sync is what writes this much, so the rule "Bounded files: the WAL is kept under 64 MiB" applies to this range.
*Fix:*
- `journal_size_limit=67108864`.
- The checkpointer running PASSIVE continuously while frames wait.
- The writer stopping background work past a soft limit (16,384 frames), and running one short PASSIVE itself when few frames are left, so its next transaction restarts the WAL.
- A `TRUNCATE` checkpoint when idle and at clean shutdown, with the WAL size and frames waiting in `jawd status`.
Then show the bench's WAL peak under 64 MiB, with no live write over 5 ms at p99 and no multi-second maximum.

**P2. New statements sort in temporary b-trees, and no plan is pinned.** `apply_pending`, which runs for every new message (`write.rs:991-994`), plans as `SEARCH pending USING INDEX pending_by_target (chat=? AND target=?) | USE TEMP B-TREE FOR ORDER BY`. The avatar seed (`sync/avatars.rs:58-60`) plans as `SCAN chats USING INDEX chats_by_sort | USE TEMP B-TREE FOR LAST TERM OF ORDER BY`. There is no plan test in the repository, and the rule file says to pin new statements' plans in it.
*Fix:*
- `CREATE INDEX pending_by_target_at ON pending (chat, target, at)` in place of `pending_by_target`.
- Two seed queries, `archived = 0` and then `archived = 1`, each `ORDER BY sort DESC`.
- A plan test that snapshots every statement's `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN`. It may allow the scan of `person_jids` in `refresh::lids` (`refresh.rs:76-79`), which runs once per connection, and the scan of `quarantine` for `status`.

**P3. A failed inbox commit drops the payload and stalls the engine (review D7).** `mod.rs:612-615` drops the payload and leaves the engine's `keep()` waiting for an ack that never comes, so whatsmeow's queue stops until the engine is restarted. Nothing is lost, since WhatsApp keeps the message, but L1 changes this function.
*Fix:* keep the payload and retry with back-off (10 ms doubling to 1 s), and tell interfaces while the inbox can't commit.

### Hygiene

**H1. `durable()` (`mod.rs:424-434`) only logs when a PRAGMA fails.** A durable commit can then report success at `NORMAL`. The rule is that every PRAGMA is read back. Read back `synchronous` and `fullfsync`, and fail the apply if they aren't what was asked. *Required, being the durability path.*

**H2. Migration 5 (`schema.rs:305-343`) declares neither its kind nor its tables' classes,** and no test hashes the merged migrations or rejects a duplicate number. Mark it a schema step: the inbox rebuild costs 7 ms per 1,000 inbox rows, and the inbox is small. Classes: `inbox` raw, `quarantine` jaw's own, `tombstones` jaw's own. Add the frozen-migration hash test and the unique-number check. *Required (rule file).*

**H3. Tests the rules touched in this range need, and which are missing.** *Required.*
- The spool file still exists until the drain is committed `FULL`, and a restart before the drain reuses the blob without duplicating anything.
- A stop that wasn't clean, of jawd and of the engine, requests the full resync, and a clean stop doesn't.
- The durable commit actually runs at `FULL` + `fullfsync`.

**H4. Follow-ups that may stand with an approval if written down:**
- `STRICT` for migration 4's `fts_backlog`, `sync_state` and `app_state` (rebuilds of small tables, all written with typed parameters).
- Inbox rows that don't decode are logged but not quarantined, so `status` doesn't count them (`drain.rs:171-174`).
- `pending` grows with receipts for messages that never come, and the inbox with kept poll votes and encrypted reactions; bound both and report them in `status`.
- `Store::remembered` and `Store::count` open read-write connections without a busy timeout; use the read pool (review H5).
- The inbox insert isn't `prepare_cached` (review H1).
- A history copy's `starred = true` can re-star a message unstarred through app state (`write.rs:894`).
- `clearChat` and `deleteChat` app state aren't applied yet; their raw is kept in `app_state`.
- `tombstones` cascade from `chats`: chat deletion, when it lands, must keep them.
- Reactions, edits, revokes and receipts keep no raw once applied, so those tables aren't rebuildable from raw; until they are, declare them jaw's own.
- `session.db` at `synchronous=FULL` and `UseRetryMessageStore` (the rest of review D4), before sending lands.
- The schema fingerprint check and fixture databases.
- Review findings this range doesn't touch remain open on `main`: I6, I7, I9, P2 to P11, and the review's other hygiene items.

2. **The store returned an error at `apply(...).unwrap()`** (`a_burst_of_2000…`). Cause: the product, I5. I reproduced "database is locked: Error code 5" at `feed/tests.rs:43` (burst) and at `feed/tests.rs:644` (slow client), in 2 of 25 full-suite runs. `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` removed it (0 of 30).

The feed spaces sends at least 50 ms apart (`feed/client.rs:555-556`), but the test timestamps each batch when its collecting task receives it, and scheduling jitter can bring two receipts closer than 50 ms. Timestamp at send, or allow one batch more.
4. **A hang in `a_slow_client_is_bounded…`** (1 of 30, over 60 s, killed at 150 s; also seen once on the unchanged build). Cause: the test. Its `while !writing.is_finished() { select! { slow.client.ready(), quick.client.ready() } }` (`feed/tests.rs:656-672`) has no timeout and doesn't watch the writer. If the writer finishes while both clients are idle, it waits forever, and an idle client correctly never wakes. Add the writer's handle, or a timeout, to the `select!`.

## Verdict

Changes needed. Before this lands:

1. L1: inbox commits at `FULL` + `fullfsync` before their ack, PRAGMAs read back (H1), then the ack-path and live-latency benches against the restated budget.
2. L2: `checkpoint_fullfsync=ON` on every `jaw.db` connection and on `session.db` through a `ConnectHook` driver.
3. L3: a sound resync trigger, either a full resync at every start and engine restart, or a whatsmeow patch that stores versions after dispatch. Plus `"0"` written durably before the engine starts, with tests (H3).
4. L4: keep each history blob's remainder before deleting it, and keep each live message's engine envelope as raw.
5. L5: 403, 404 and 410 history downloads quarantined, and a failing blob no longer holding up the queue.
6. I1: `messages.n` and `people.n` never reused, with the tests above.
7. I2: `message_count` by triggers; `unread` and `mentions` derived from a read position; `markChatAsRead` by its range.
8. I3: merging a person's two direct chats when a join reveals them.
9. I4: edits by `edited_at`, the live envelope kept over history copies, reaction removals by time.
10. I5: `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` for every writer transaction; no caller drops a transient failure; an index step that fails tries again later.

12. P2: the `pending` index, the avatar seed without a sort, and a plan test pinning every statement.

14. H2 and H3: migration 5's kind and classes declared, the frozen-migration and unique-number tests, and the missing durability tests.
