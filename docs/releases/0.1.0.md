# jaw 0.1.0

The first release of jaw: WhatsApp in a terminal, fast, from the keyboard, with the mouse too. A daemon, jawd, keeps your chats and the connection to WhatsApp, which it speaks through jaw-engine on [whatsmeow](https://github.com/tulir/whatsmeow); `jaw`, the terminal interface, shows them. Opening jaw never waits on WhatsApp, and notifications come with no window open.

jaw isn't affiliated with WhatsApp or Meta. WhatsApp's terms don't allow unofficial clients, and a number that uses one can be banned, so try jaw on a spare number first, and use it at your own risk.

## Install

With Homebrew, on macOS:

```sh
brew install pdcgomes/jaw/jaw
```

It builds from source, so Homebrew first installs Go, Rust and cmake to build it, and ffmpeg, which jaw recommends (`--without-ffmpeg` leaves it out). The build takes about four minutes on an M1 Ultra, longer on a smaller Mac. From a checkout, with Go, Rust and cmake (and on Linux, ALSA's headers and pkg-config):

```sh
make install PREFIX=~/.local  # jaw and jawd in ~/.local/bin, jaw-engine in ~/.local/libexec/jaw
make uninstall PREFIX=~/.local
```

Then run `jaw`. It starts jawd, and shows a QR code: on the phone, go to WhatsApp › Settings › Linked devices › Link a device and scan it, or run `jaw link --phone 351912345678`, with your number, for a code to type on the phone instead. jaw links as a device of yours, as WhatsApp Web does, and fetches your history from the phone. `jaw agent install` starts jawd at login, and again if it stops.

To look around first without an account, run `jaw demo`: a made-up account, with nothing sent anywhere.

### Jaw.app, the Mac app, as a preview

On a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 26 or later: download `Jaw-0.1.0.zip` from this release, unzip it, move Jaw.app to Applications and open it. It's signed with a Developer ID and notarized, and carries its own jawd, jaw-engine and terminal jaw. It shows the same QR code to link, or, if jaw has already linked, the same chats: Jaw.app and jaw share one jawd and one folder, whichever of them starts jawd first, and jawd goes on when either quits.

## What's in it

- **Your chats, on your machine.** jawd keeps every chat and message in its own database, in `~/Library/Application Support/jaw` on macOS (a folder only you can read), and nothing is acknowledged to WhatsApp before it's safely on disk. jaw shows what's there at once, and catches up after time offline with a summary rather than a notification per message.
- **Messages.** Text with mentions, replies, reactions, edits, deletions for you or for everyone, forwards, stars (* on a message, which your phone follows) and each message's info; ticks for sent, delivered and read; drafts kept for you; link previews on what you send and receive; polls with their tallies, which Enter votes in; places, and events with who's going; contact cards, received and sent; a line for each group, community and chat event; and, for anything jaw can't read yet, "jaw can't show this yet", kept whole to be shown once a later jaw can.
- **Photos, videos and files.** Photos, stickers, GIFs and voice messages download as they arrive, and videos and documents when you open them; an HD copy joins its photo. Pictures are drawn with kitty's, iTerm2's or sixel graphics, or half-blocks elsewhere. Ctrl-O sends photos, videos, GIFs, documents and audio files, in SD or HD (Tab), with their progress, and x stops one while it uploads. s saves a message's file into Downloads, or wherever you type.
- **Voice messages.** Played with their waveform, seeking with the arrows and changing speed with x, and recorded with Ctrl-R and sent as voice notes.
- **Disappearing and view-once messages.** A chat's timer shows in its title, and the palette's Disappearing messages sets it as the phones do: off, 24 hours, 7 days or 90 days. A message with a time to go has ⏱ by its time, and jaw erases it then, its file and its words in search with it, though not a notification already shown. Enter opens someone's view-once photo over the screen for a minute at most, or plays a view-once video or voice message in the system's app; once it's closed, or its time is up, jaw erases it, as it does one opened on your phone.
- **The library.** Every photo, video, voice message, file and link ever shared, across chats or in one, with Ctrl-L.
- **Finding anything.** Ctrl-K opens the palette: chats, people, folders, actions, messages, links and files in one search, whatever their accents, with the actions' keys beside them. `/search` searches the open chat.
- **Chats.** A community's groups gathered under it. Pinning (three at most, as on the phone), archiving, muting and marking read or unread from jaw, which your phone and other devices follow.
- **Folders and the roster.** Nested folders, a chat or a person in several (m files one), smart folders for unread chats, groups, communities and the archived, and up to nine favourites on Alt-1 to Alt-9. The roster lists people online first, then by when they were last seen; who's typing or recording shows in the chat list and the conversation. jaw tells WhatsApp you're online only while its window has focus, and follows the presence of a bounded few at a time, as WhatsApp expects.
- **Settings.** Omarchy's 23 themes, and your messages on the right or, IRC-style, on the left: Ctrl-, (in terminals with the kitty keyboard protocol), the palette's Settings, or `jaw settings`.
- **When something fails, jaw says so.** jawd starting or out of reach, reconnecting, WhatsApp out of reach (what you send waits and goes once it's back), the phone offline, logged out from the phone, and WhatsApp wanting a newer version. jawd starts the engine again if it stops; however jawd itself stops, even killed, the engine goes with it, and two engines never open your account's keys at once. `jaw status` says how everything stands.
- **A disk running short.** With under 2 GB free (or 5% of a disk smaller than 40 GB), jawd holds back history, downloads, even of what you open, and search's indexing until there's room again, and jaw says so; messages still arrive and send, and disappearing ones still go at their time. jawd keeps 64 MB in reserve in its folder and gives it up if the disk fills, to go on storing what arrives while you make room.
- **Jaw.app, a preview.** The Mac app on the same jawd, in a window of its own: chats and messages, sending text, photos, videos and files, link previews and polls, contact cards, the library, and folders and favourites. It's early: what it doesn't do yet is under "Not in this release".
- **Trying ideas.** Split panes, a message box along the whole bottom of the screen, a status line, a focus mode and a quick look at the chats that need you are in jaw but off: `JAW_EXPERIMENTS=all jaw` turns them on.

Press `?` in jaw for every key.

## Not in this release

- **In Jaw.app,** voice messages, stars, disappearing and view-once messages, presence, saved layouts and settings, which jaw in the terminal has; they come in later releases.
- **From the terminal,** creating polls, sending a place or an event, and making a group, which come in 0.1.1, and saving an album's photos and videos one by one, which comes later. jawd already does all of these but making a group.
- **Unlinking from jaw.** Log jaw out on the phone, in Linked devices.
- **Played receipts** for voice messages you've listened to, and **deleting or clearing a chat**.
- **After this release:** GIF search, messages scheduled to send later and reminders, AI assistants reaching jaw over MCP, and summaries of what you missed.
- **Not planned for now:** calls (they show as lines in the chat), status, channels, community admin, business tools and WhatsApp's own settings.

## Known limits

- **Search fills in after the first sync.** On a made-up account of a million messages, the history went in within a minute and a half, and search filled in over about a minute more.
- **Catching up after time offline goes at about 160 to 170 messages a second,** since each message is on disk before WhatsApp hears it arrived, one at a time.
- **jawd isn't quiet when idle yet.** With nothing happening it wakes about four times a minute (4.0 and 4.5 times in two counts, and 4.0 twice in two more, against 52.5 to 53.5 before this release's fix), where jaw's design asks for none, though it spends only 0.6 to 1.6 ms of CPU a minute doing so. While the disk has less than about 22 GB free, it wakes about 18 times a minute more: its disk watchdog looks at the disk no later than the disk could come down to the 2 GB jaw keeps free, and 256 MB more, if filled at 2 GB a second, and within 10 minutes; with less than about 22 GB free such a fill would get there within 10 s, so it looks every 10 s, as it did before the fix.
- **Media from before jaw was linked** is often gone from WhatsApp's servers. jaw asks the phone for it again, which needs the phone online, and says so while it waits.
- **jaw's folder is left out of Time Machine.** If it's lost, linking jaw again brings your messages and media back from the phone, as far as it still has them, but not your folders, favourites, drafts, settings and layouts: they have no backup until a later release backs them up daily.
- **Spotlight indexes jaw's folder** on macOS 26 and 27, despite the marker jawd puts in it, so the photos and files jaw downloads can turn up in its searches.
- **jaw and jawd from Homebrew aren't signed** with a Developer ID, only ad hoc as they're built (Jaw.app's are signed and notarized), so macOS takes each upgrade for a new program. It asks for the microphone the first time you record: for the terminal app, once, when jaw started jawd from it; or for jawd, when `jaw agent install` runs it, and then again after each upgrade.
- **Without ffmpeg** (`--without-ffmpeg`), jaw can't send videos, GIFs, audio files, HEIC photos or photos over 25 megapixels, and says so when you try, and a video or GIF you receive shows only the thumbnail it came with. Text, voice messages both ways and everything else you receive work without it.
- **After an upgrade,** the jawd still running is the old one until it stops, and jaw says how to stop it. An older jawd won't open a database a newer one has upgraded.
- **Link previews on what you send** are fetched by jawd from your machine, so the site sees your address; a message whose page doesn't answer within three seconds goes without one.
- **Your database isn't encrypted.** It's in a folder only you can read.
- **One account at a time.**
- **Where it's been run:** macOS on Apple silicon. Intel Macs and Linux build from the same source, but neither has been tried with a real account.

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue at [github.com/pdcgomes/jaw/issues](https://github.com/pdcgomes/jaw/issues) with:

- what you did, what you saw, and what you expected;
- `jaw --version` and `jaw status`;
- the end of jawd's log, `jawd.log` in jaw's folder (`~/Library/Application Support/jaw/jawd.log` on macOS), with anything you'd rather not share taken out: it can name your chats and the numbers in them.

Never attach `jaw.db`, which holds your messages, or `session.db`, which holds the keys to your account. For anything about security, use Report a vulnerability on the repository's Security tab rather than an issue.
