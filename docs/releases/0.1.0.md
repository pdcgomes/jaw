# jaw 0.1.0

The first release of jaw: WhatsApp in a terminal, fast, from the keyboard, with the mouse too. A daemon, jawd, keeps your chats and the connection to WhatsApp, which it speaks through jaw-engine on [whatsmeow](https://github.com/tulir/whatsmeow); `jaw`, the terminal interface, shows them. Opening jaw never waits on WhatsApp, and notifications come with no window open.

jaw isn't affiliated with WhatsApp or Meta. WhatsApp's terms don't allow unofficial clients, and a number that uses one can be banned, so try jaw on a spare number first, and use it at your own risk.

## Install

With Homebrew, on macOS:

```sh
brew install pdcgomes/jaw/jaw
```

It builds from source, so Homebrew first installs Go, Rust and cmake to build it, and ffmpeg, which jaw recommends (`--without-ffmpeg` leaves it out). The build takes about four minutes on an M1 Ultra, longer on a smaller Mac. From a checkout, with Go, Rust and cmake (and on Linux, ALSA's headers and pkg-config):

```sh
make install PREFIX=~/.local  # jaw and jawd in ~/.local/bin, jaw-engine in ~/.local/libexec/jaw
make uninstall PREFIX=~/.local
```

Then run `jaw`. It starts jawd, and shows a QR code: on the phone, go to WhatsApp › Settings › Linked devices › Link a device and scan it, or run `jaw link --phone 351912345678`, with your number, for a code to type on the phone instead. jaw links as a device of yours, as WhatsApp Web does, and fetches your history from the phone. `jaw agent install` starts jawd at login, and again if it stops.

To look around first without an account, run `jaw demo`: a made-up account, with nothing sent anywhere.

## What's in it

- **Your chats, on your machine.** jawd keeps every chat and message in its own database, in `~/Library/Application Support/jaw` on macOS (a folder only you can read), and nothing is acknowledged to WhatsApp before it's safely on disk. jaw shows what's there at once, and catches up after time offline with a summary rather than a notification per message.
- **Messages.** Text with mentions, replies, reactions, edits, deletions for you or for everyone, forwards and each message's info; ticks for sent, delivered and read; drafts kept for you; link previews on what you send and receive; polls with their tallies; places, and events with who's going; contact cards, received and sent; a line for each group, community and chat event; and, for anything jaw can't read yet, "jaw can't show this yet", kept whole to be shown once a later jaw can.
- **Photos, videos and files.** Photos, stickers, GIFs and voice messages download as they arrive, and videos and documents when you open them; an HD copy joins its photo. Pictures are drawn with kitty's, iTerm2's or sixel graphics, or half-blocks elsewhere. Ctrl-O sends photos, videos, GIFs, documents and audio files, in SD or HD (Tab), with their progress, and x stops one while it uploads.
- **Voice messages.** Played with their waveform, seeking with the arrows and changing speed with x, and recorded with Ctrl-R and sent as voice notes.
- **The library.** Every photo, video, voice message, file and link ever shared, across chats or in one, with Ctrl-L.
- **Finding anything.** Ctrl-K opens the palette: chats, people, folders, actions, messages, links and files in one search, whatever their accents, with the actions' keys beside them. `/search` searches the open chat.
- **Chats.** A community's groups gathered under it. Pinning (three at most, as on the phone), archiving, muting and marking read or unread from jaw, which your phone and other devices follow.
- **Folders and the roster.** Nested folders, a chat or a person in several (m files one), smart folders for unread chats, groups, communities and the archived, and up to nine favourites on Alt-1 to Alt-9. The roster lists people online first, then by when they were last seen; who's typing or recording shows in the chat list and the conversation. jaw tells WhatsApp you're online only while its window has focus, and follows the presence of a bounded few at a time, as WhatsApp expects.
- **Settings.** Omarchy's 23 themes, and your messages on the right or, IRC-style, on the left: Ctrl-, (in terminals with the kitty keyboard protocol), the palette's Settings, or `jaw settings`.
- **When something fails, jaw says so.** jawd starting or out of reach, reconnecting, WhatsApp out of reach (what you send waits and goes once it's back), the phone offline, logged out from the phone, and WhatsApp wanting a newer version. jawd starts the engine again if it stops. `jaw status` says how everything stands.
- **Trying ideas.** Split panes, a message box along the whole bottom of the screen, a status line, a focus mode and a quick look at the chats that need you are in jaw but off: `JAW_EXPERIMENTS=all jaw` turns them on.

Press `?` in jaw for every key.

## Not in this release

- **The Mac app.** Jaw.app is being built in the same repository, on the same jawd, and comes in a later release.
- **Disappearing and view-once messages** are kept and shown like any other: jaw doesn't erase them yet once their time is up or they've been opened.
- **From the terminal,** creating polls and voting in them, sending a place or an event, starring a message, saving a message's file elsewhere, and making a group. jawd already does most of these; the terminal's keys for them come next.
- **Unlinking from jaw.** Log jaw out on the phone, in Linked devices.
- **Played receipts** for voice messages you've listened to, and **deleting or clearing a chat**.
- **After this release:** GIF search, messages scheduled to send later and reminders, AI assistants reaching jaw over MCP, and summaries of what you missed.
- **Not planned for now:** calls (they show as lines in the chat), status, channels, community admin, business tools and WhatsApp's own settings.

## Known limits

- **The first sync of a big history takes memory.** On a made-up account of a million messages, jawd took 8.4 GB while the history went in, and 89 MB once it was in. Search fills in afterwards, over about a quarter of an hour for a million messages.
- **Catching up after time offline goes at about 150 messages a second,** since each message is on disk before WhatsApp hears it arrived, one at a time.
- **Media from before jaw was linked** is often gone from WhatsApp's servers. jaw asks the phone for it again, which needs the phone online, and says so while it waits.
- **The microphone.** macOS asks for it the first time you record: for the terminal app, when jaw started jawd from it, or for jawd, when `jaw agent install` runs it. jawd isn't signed with a stable identity yet, so it can ask again after an upgrade.
- **After an upgrade,** the jawd still running is the old one until it stops, and jaw says how to stop it. An older jawd won't open a database a newer one has upgraded.
- **Link previews on what you send** are fetched by jawd from your machine, so the site sees your address; a message whose page doesn't answer within three seconds goes without one.
- **Your database isn't encrypted.** It's in a folder only you can read.
- **One account at a time.**
- **Where it's been run:** macOS on Apple silicon. Intel Macs and Linux build from the same source, and Linux passes jaw's tests on every change, but neither has been tried with a real account.

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue at [github.com/pdcgomes/jaw/issues](https://github.com/pdcgomes/jaw/issues) with:

- what you did, what you saw, and what you expected;
- `jaw --version` and `jaw status`;
- the end of jawd's log, `jawd.log` in jaw's folder (`~/Library/Application Support/jaw/jawd.log` on macOS), with anything you'd rather not share taken out: it can name your chats and the numbers in them.

Never attach `jaw.db`, which holds your messages, or `session.db`, which holds the keys to your account. For anything about security, use Report a vulnerability on the repository's Security tab rather than an issue.
