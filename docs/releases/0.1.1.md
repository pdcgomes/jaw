# jaw 0.1.1

jaw's second release. Jaw.app, the Mac app, gets its Settings in a window of its own and a gallery of themes, with a theme for dark and one for light; jaw logs out of WhatsApp from jaw itself, and a folder links only its own account; and jaw builds from source with mise, in place of make. Beyond logging out and linking, jawd, jaw-engine and jaw in the terminal are 0.1.0's but for the words of a few messages and one fix in jawd, so what [0.1.0's notes](https://github.com/pdcgomes/jaw/releases/tag/v0.1.0) say of the rest holds, its known limits with it.

jaw isn't affiliated with WhatsApp or Meta. WhatsApp's terms don't allow unofficial clients, and a number that uses one can be banned, so try jaw on a spare number first, and use it at your own risk.

## What's new

- **Jaw.app's Settings, in a window of its own.** ⌘, or Jaw › Settings… opens it, or brings it to the front, on the pane you had last: Appearance, Messages and Panes, as icons in its toolbar, as the Mac's own apps have them. There's no Done button: each change shows in every window as you make it, and is kept, and the chats stay in use beside Settings, so a theme you pick is on them at once. ⌘W or its close button closes it. It stays on the screen when a taller pane opens, and while it's open a click on Jaw in the Dock brings back the chats' window. Choose a Theme, in the palette (⌘K), opens it on Appearance.
- **Themes.** 46 to choose from, in three sections: Jaw's Tokyo Night, with Tokyo Night Day as its light half; Omarchy's 23, which jawd serves, mapped as jaw in the terminal maps them; and the 22 theme families of Redlamp, a photo editor by jaw's author, each with a dark and a light half: Neutral, grey with the Mac's own accent and colours, Redlamp's own, and twenty more, Kanagawa, Catppuccin, Rosé Pine, Nord, Gruvbox and Dracula among them.
- **A theme for dark and one for light.** Appearance holds a slot for each, both Tokyo Night at first. With Appearance on the system's, Jaw draws the dark slot's theme while the Mac is dark and the light slot's while it's light, switching as the Mac does.
- **The gallery.** Each theme is drawn as a small chat in its colours, an unread chat's row, their message mentioning you and your answer with its ticks, in the half the slot you're choosing for would take. A click fills the slot. A theme with both halves, Tokyo Night or any of Redlamp's, fills both slots, each with its own half, so your choice holds when the Mac changes appearance; one of Omarchy's, which have one mode each, fills the slot of its mode. ⌥-click, or Use for Dark Only (or Light Only) in a card's menu, fills only the slot you're choosing for, so dark and light can come from different themes. The arrow keys move through the cards, showing on the chats what a click on each would do, as the terminal previews a theme as it moves through its list, and the one they stop on stays chosen. A click on the theme the slot already holds changes nothing, with ⌥ or without. VoiceOver reads each card's name, its section and the slot it fills.
- **Readable in every theme.** Every colour Jaw draws as text or as a mark, times, links, ticks, mentions, typing and senders' names among them, reads at 3:1 or more on whatever it's drawn on, in every theme, Tokyo Night Day included: where a theme's own colour reads less, Jaw draws it a little nearer the text, keeping its hue. Links and read ticks have a colour of their own, the theme's cyan in Omarchy's, as the terminal draws them. So Tokyo Night's secondary and dim text are a shade lighter than in 0.1.0, and Tokyo Night Day's links, red, mentions and dim text a shade deeper.
- **Your themes from the first frame.** Jaw keeps the colours of the themes in its slots, so it opens in them before jawd answers, and keeps them while jawd can't be reached; meanwhile Omarchy's section shows the ones your slots hold, and says why the rest wait. A theme a later jawd stops serving is drawn as Tokyo Night in its slot, and comes back with a jawd that serves it.
- **Logging out from jaw.** `jaw unlink` logs jaw out of WhatsApp after saying what that does and asking (`--yes` doesn't ask), as do Log out of WhatsApp in the terminal's palette and Help › Log Out… in Jaw.app. jaw stops being one of your account's linked devices, and only your phone can link it again. jawd keeps your messages, media, folders, drafts and settings, and the linking screen follows, as after a logout from the phone.
- **A failed link no longer leaves jaw stuck.** whatsmeow saves the device as the phone pairs, before WhatsApp has confirmed it. So a link that failed after that, WhatsApp refusing it or the connection not coming, could leave a half-made device, and linking again then said jaw was already linked. jaw-engine now clears it and says why, and linking starts afresh. `jaw link` links again when jawd only thought it was linked, and Jaw.app's linking screen no longer disconnects an account it has just linked.
- **A folder links only its own account.** A folder whose jaw.db keeps an account's messages links only that account, so two accounts' messages never mix in one folder. The phone of another number is refused as it pairs, before anything of it is kept, and jaw and Jaw.app say which number the folder belongs to. Another account takes a folder of its own: `JAW_HOME=<folder> jaw`, or Jaw.app with `--home` or `JAW_HOME`. A folder that keeps no account's messages links any number. It goes by the number, so an account whose number has changed is refused too, until jaw can choose what becomes of the messages.
- **Fixed in Jaw.app.** On macOS 27.2, the library's search drew its filters and tokens upside down; they're the right way up now. And a preference given on the command line, such as a theme with `open -a Jaw --args -themeDark omarchy:nord`, lasts that run and is never saved, where a change made during the run could keep it for good.
- **Fixed in jawd.** A photo or file of yours whose upload, or the copy jaw makes to send it, was to be tried again, and whose time came while jawd was looking through what it had to send, waited for your next send, another file's copy or upload, or a reconnect; now it goes at its time. Nothing was lost meanwhile.
- **Building from source, with mise.** jaw's Makefile is gone: each of its targets is a [mise](https://mise.jdx.dev) task under the same name, a script in `mise/tasks/` that runs without mise too, and `mise tasks` lists them. `mise run install` builds jaw, jawd and jaw-engine for release as you, then copies them, asking for sudo only for a folder you can't write, so one command installs into `/usr/local`, where make needed `make release` and then `sudo make install`. make's variables come from the environment now, as in `PREFIX=~/.local mise run install`: `mise run install PREFIX=~/.local` is refused, saying so. Arguments go after `--`, as in `mise run check-parity -- --table`; `make hooks` is `mise run setup`, and `scripts/release.sh` is `mise run release`. `mise.toml` pins the Mac app's Tuist, SwiftFormat and SwiftLint, for macOS only; Rust and Go stay yours, rustup's stable and Homebrew's Go.
- **Homebrew's formula runs the same install,** as a script, so Homebrew needs no mise, and lays out the same files. `brew install --HEAD pdcgomes/jaw/jaw`, which builds main and has failed with 0.1.0's formula since main's Makefile went, works again.
- **Xcode 27 for the Mac app.** Jaw.app's project takes Xcode 27 as well as 26, where it took only 26 before; this release's Jaw.app is built with Xcode 26.6, and a build with 27 hasn't been tried yet.

## Install or upgrade

With Homebrew, on macOS, from 0.1.0:

```sh
brew update
brew upgrade pdcgomes/jaw/jaw
```

New to jaw, `brew install pdcgomes/jaw/jaw` instead, and 0.1.0's notes say how to link it. Either builds from source, as 0.1.0 did: Homebrew's Go, Rust and cmake build it, in about four minutes on an M1 Ultra and longer on a smaller Mac, with ffmpeg, which jaw recommends (`--without-ffmpeg` leaves it out).

After the upgrade, the jawd still running is 0.1.0's until it stops: jaw says it's another build, with the command that stops it. Once it has stopped, whichever of jaw and Jaw.app starts jawd next starts its own, and with `jaw agent install` launchd starts Homebrew's by itself. Homebrew's jaw and jawd are signed ad hoc, as 0.1.0's were, so where `jaw agent install` runs jawd, macOS asks for the microphone again the first time you record.

From a checkout, with [mise](https://mise.jdx.dev), Go, Rust and cmake (and on Linux, ALSA's headers and pkg-config):

```sh
mise run install                  # jaw and jawd in /usr/local/bin, jaw-engine in /usr/local/libexec/jaw
PREFIX=~/.local mise run install  # or under your home folder, without sudo
mise run uninstall                # with the same PREFIX, takes them out again
```

They're the files `make install` put in the same places, so `PREFIX=~/.local mise run install` upgrades a 0.1.0 that `make install PREFIX=~/.local` installed, and `PREFIX=~/.local mise run uninstall` takes out either. Without mise, the same tasks run as scripts, as in `PREFIX=~/.local mise/tasks/install`.

### Jaw.app, the Mac app, still a preview

On a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 26 or later: download `Jaw-0.1.1.zip` from this release and unzip it, quit Jaw if it's open, and move the new Jaw.app to Applications in place of 0.1.0's. It carries its own jawd, jaw-engine and terminal jaw, 0.1.1's. Your settings carry over, and 0.1.0's theme, Tokyo Night, fills both slots. Jaw.app and jaw share one jawd and one folder, as before: Jaw.app uses the jawd that's running, 0.1.0's included, until it stops.

This time Jaw.app is signed with a Developer ID but not notarized, since notarizing this release waits for jaw's author. So the first time you open it, macOS says it can't check it for malicious software and doesn't open it, which is expected. To open it, once:

1. Open Jaw.app, then close the message.
2. Within the hour, in System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security and click Open Anyway.
3. Confirm with your login password or Touch ID, and choose Open when macOS asks once more.

If macOS asks the same about jawd or jaw-engine, Jaw's helpers, the first time Jaw starts them, allow each the same way, then open Jaw again. From then on it opens as any app does. Control-click › Open no longer gets past this, since macOS 15.

## What's the same

- **jawd, jaw-engine and jaw in the terminal** do what 0.1.0's do, built from the same code, but for logging out and linking, under What's new. All else that changes in them is the fix in jawd under What's new, for a photo or file of yours that could wait too long to be tried again, and the words of the messages that named make's commands, which name mise's now, as in "mise run build builds it". What's in 0.1.0, what isn't, and its known limits hold as [its notes](https://github.com/pdcgomes/jaw/releases/tag/v0.1.0) say them.
- **Your chats and your folder.** 0.1.1's jawd keeps them in the same database as 0.1.0's, with nothing to upgrade, so either opens the folder the other used, and there's nothing to link again. What jaw-engine sends to WhatsApp changes only as you log out from jaw, which has WhatsApp remove jaw's device, or link, when the phone of another number than the folder's is refused as it pairs.
- **Where it's been run:** macOS on Apple silicon. Intel Macs and Linux build from the same source, but neither has been tried with a real account.

## Not in this release

- **In Jaw.app,** voice messages, stars, disappearing and view-once messages, and who's online in a chat's header (the roster has it), which jaw in the terminal has. Its settings, in their own window now, and its named layouts are still its own, not kept by jawd and shared with the terminal: a theme chosen in Jaw.app isn't the terminal's, nor the terminal's Jaw.app's. A theme's tint and translucency, controls in its accent and the palette in a theme of its own come later, as do settings for what downloads by itself, the cache's size and notifications. Jaw.app doesn't update itself yet: download each release.
- **From the terminal,** creating polls, sending a place or an event, and making a group, which 0.1.0's notes said would come in 0.1.1, aren't in it after all, nor is saving an album's photos and videos one by one: they come later. jawd already does all of these but making a group.
- **Still to come, as 0.1.0's notes have them:** played receipts, deleting or clearing a chat, GIF search, messages scheduled to send later and reminders, AI assistants reaching jaw over MCP, and summaries of what you missed. Calls, status, channels, community admin, business tools and WhatsApp's own settings aren't planned for now.

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue at [github.com/pdcgomes/jaw/issues](https://github.com/pdcgomes/jaw/issues) with:

- what you did, what you saw, and what you expected;
- `jaw --version` and `jaw status`;
- the end of jawd's log, `jawd.log` in jaw's folder (`~/Library/Application Support/jaw/jawd.log` on macOS), with anything you'd rather not share taken out: it can name your chats and the numbers in them.

Never attach `jaw.db`, which holds your messages, or `session.db`, which holds the keys to your account. For anything about security, use Report a vulnerability on the repository's Security tab rather than an issue.
